# Ch 66 — Anemia and Polycythemia: practice questions

**1.** A 60-year-old man has a hematocrit of 23%. The laboratory reports a reticulocyte count of 1.0% (manual percentage). What does this indicate?

A. Appropriate marrow response to hemolysis
B. Underproduction; the corrected reticulocyte count is about 0.5%
C. Acute blood loss with brisk compensation
D. A normal reticulocyte response
E. The count can't be interpreted without a bone marrow

**2.** A 34-year-old woman with heavy menstrual periods has fatigue and craves ice. Hb is 9.2 g/dL, MCV 72 fL, reticulocyte count low. Which test should be ordered first?

A. Hemoglobin electrophoresis
B. Bone marrow aspirate
C. Vitamin B12 level
D. Serum ferritin
E. Direct antiglobulin test

**3.** A 26-year-old man of Mediterranean descent has Hb 11.8 g/dL, MCV 64 fL, a normal RDW and a normal ferritin. He has no symptoms. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Thalassemia
B. Iron deficiency
C. Anemia of inflammation
D. Sideroblastic anemia from lead
E. Vitamin B12 deficiency

**4.** A 41-year-old woman has 2 weeks of fatigue and jaundice. Hb is 7.9 g/dL, reticulocytes 9%, LDH elevated, haptoglobin undetectable, and the smear shows many spherocytes. Which test is most appropriate next?

A. Bone marrow biopsy
B. Serum ferritin
C. Hemoglobin electrophoresis
D. Vitamin B12 level
E. Direct antiglobulin (Coombs) test

**5.** A 70-year-old woman has Hb 9.0 g/dL, MCV 118 fL, and a smear showing macro-ovalocytes and hypersegmented neutrophils. She has numbness in her feet. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Bone marrow biopsy first
B. Thyroid function only
C. Vitamin B12 and folate levels
D. Iron studies
E. Hemoglobin electrophoresis

**6.** A 76-year-old man has macrocytic anemia (MCV 106 fL) with mild thrombocytopenia. The smear shows oval macrocytes. B12, folate, TSH and liver tests are normal, and he doesn't drink alcohol. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Empiric B12 injections
B. Bone marrow examination
C. Repeat CBC in 1 year
D. Abdominal ultrasound
E. Start erythropoietin

**7.** A 68-year-old man with prostate cancer has new anemia. The smear shows teardrop cells, nucleated red cells and immature white cells. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Oral iron trial
B. Erythropoietin
C. Reassurance
D. Bone marrow examination
E. Hemoglobin electrophoresis

**8.** A 72-year-old man with coronary artery disease has new chest pain at rest. Hb is 6.8 g/dL from a slow GI bleed, and the workup has just begun. Which of the following is the most appropriate immediate step?

A. Red cell transfusion
B. Oral iron and outpatient follow-up
C. Erythropoietin
D. Defer any treatment until the cause is known
E. IV vitamin B12

**9.** A 58-year-old man has Hb 19.2 g/dL, oxygen saturation 97%, aquagenic pruritus and a palpable spleen. Platelets and basophils are elevated. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Arterial blood gas only
B. Abdominopelvic CT for renal tumor
C. Carboxyhemoglobin level
D. Sleep study
E. Serum EPO level and JAK2 V617F mutation testing

**10.** A 49-year-old man who smokes two packs daily has Hct 54%. Oxygen saturation by pulse oximetry is 96%, the spleen isn't enlarged, and serum EPO is elevated. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. JAK2 testing and hydroxyurea
B. Bone marrow biopsy
C. Measure carboxyhemoglobin and urge smoking cessation
D. Start erythropoietin
E. Echocardiogram for congenital heart disease

**11.** A 45-year-old nonsmoking woman has persistent Hct 55%, normal oxygen saturation, normal spleen size, negative JAK2 testing and an elevated EPO level. She doesn't live at altitude. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Phlebotomy and no further workup
B. Abdominopelvic CT
C. Hydroxyurea
D. Repeat CBC in 1 year
E. Chest radiograph only

**12.** A 30-year-old woman has a sudden drop in hemoglobin to 5.8 g/dL with a reticulocyte count near zero. White cells and platelets are normal. Bone marrow shows absent erythroid precursors with normal other lineages. Which infection is a recognized cause?

A. Epstein-Barr virus
B. Hepatitis C
C. Cytomegalovirus
D. HIV
E. Parvovirus B19

**13.** Which serum ferritin value indicates depleted body iron stores in an adult without inflammation?

A. 250 µg/L
B. 120 µg/L
C. 80 µg/L
D. 18 µg/L
E. 45 µg/L

## Answers

**1. B** — The reticulocyte percentage rises artificially as the hematocrit falls. Corrected count = 1.0 × (23/45) ≈ 0.5%, which is inappropriately low and indicates impaired production.
- A: Hemolysis would raise the corrected count.
- C: Brisk compensation would raise the corrected count.
- D: Low for this degree of anemia.
- E: It can be interpreted with the correction.
Source: Ch 0066, The Reticulocyte Count

**2. D** — For low-reticulocyte microcytic anemia, check ferritin first. Ferritin <30 µg/L indicates depleted stores. Menstrual losses make iron deficiency common.
- A: Electrophoresis comes if ferritin is normal.
- B: Marrow isn't the first test.
- C: B12 is for macrocytic anemia.
- E: DAT is for hemolysis with a high retic count.
Source: Ch 0066, Evaluation of Anemia; Nutritional

**3. A** — Microcytosis with normal ferritin points to thalassemia. Thalassemic cells are uniform (low RDW), while iron deficiency causes anisocytosis (high RDW).
- B: Ferritin is normal.
- C: Inflammation is a diagnosis of exclusion with an inflammatory disorder.
- D: Nothing suggests lead or sideroblastic anemia.
- E: B12 deficiency is macrocytic.
Source: Ch 0066, Evaluation of Anemia

**4. E** — High reticulocytes with ↑ LDH, ↓ haptoglobin and spherocytes indicate hemolysis. Autoimmune hemolytic anemia shows spherocytes and red cell autoantibodies on direct antiglobulin testing.
- A: Marrow isn't needed.
- B: Iron studies don't address hemolysis.
- C: Electrophoresis evaluates hemoglobinopathies.
- D: B12 doesn't fit the picture.
Source: Ch 0066, Hyperproduction: Hemolysis; Evaluation of Anemia

**5. C** — Megaloblastic features (macro-ovalocytes, hypersegmented neutrophils) with neuropathy call for B12 and folate levels.
- A: Marrow is for normal nutrient levels.
- B: Hypothyroidism causes round macrocytes.
- D: Iron studies are for microcytosis.
- E: Electrophoresis is for microcytosis.
Source: Ch 0066, Evaluation of Anemia; Nutritional

**6. B** — Oval macrocytes with normal nutritional tests raise concern for myelodysplasia, so bone marrow testing is next.
- A: Empiric B12 isn't appropriate with a normal level.
- C: Waiting delays diagnosis.
- D: Imaging doesn't evaluate marrow.
- E: EPO before diagnosis isn't appropriate.
Source: Ch 0066, Evaluation of Anemia; Stem Cell Defects

**7. D** — A myelophthisic smear (nucleated RBCs, teardrops, immature WBCs) indicates marrow infiltration, here possibly by prostate cancer, and mandates marrow examination.
- A: Iron doesn't address infiltration.
- B: EPO doesn't address infiltration.
- C: Reassurance is inappropriate.
- E: Electrophoresis is irrelevant.
Source: Ch 0066, Marrow Replacement; Bone Marrow Exam

**8. A** — Clear indications for transfusion include hypotension or signs of cardiac compromise such as angina, even while the workup is ongoing.
- B: Too slow with ongoing ischemia.
- C: Too slow with ongoing ischemia.
- D: Supportive transfusion shouldn't wait.
- E: B12 isn't indicated.
Source: Ch 0066, Treatment: Anemia

**9. E** — Polycythemia with normal oxygen saturation, aquagenic pruritus, splenomegaly, basophilia and thrombocytosis suggests polycythemia vera. Check EPO (low in PV) and JAK2 V617F (positive in 90–95%).
- A: Saturation is already normal.
- B: CT is for high-EPO secondary polycythemia.
- C: Carboxyhemoglobin is for smokers with high EPO.
- D: A sleep study is for hypoxia-driven polycythemia.
Source: Ch 0066, Polycythemia; Approach to the Patient

**10. C** — Smokers with normal saturation can have high EPO because carbon monoxide displaces oxygen. A high carboxyhemoglobin confirms smoker's polycythemia. Urge cessation, and phlebotomy if he can't stop.
- A: High EPO argues against PV.
- B: Marrow isn't indicated.
- D: EPO is contraindicated.
- E: Nothing suggests a shunt.
Source: Ch 0066, Approach to the Patient: Polycythemia

**11. B** — With elevated EPO, normal saturation and no smoking, look for autonomous EPO production. Hepatoma, uterine leiomyoma, and renal cancer or cysts are detectable with abdominopelvic CT. High-affinity hemoglobin is another consideration.
- A: The cause must be found.
- C: Hydroxyurea is for PV.
- D: Delay may miss a tumor.
- E: Chest radiograph alone is insufficient.
Source: Ch 0066, Approach to the Patient: Polycythemia

**12. E** — Pure red cell aplasia (very low reticulocytes, absent erythroid precursors, other lineages spared) can be autoimmune or caused by parvovirus B19.
- A: The source names parvovirus B19 as the infectious cause.
- B: The source names parvovirus B19 as the infectious cause.
- C: The source names parvovirus B19 as the infectious cause.
- D: The source names parvovirus B19 as the infectious cause.
Source: Ch 0066, Absence of Red Cell Precursors

**13. D** — Serum ferritin <30 µg/L indicates depleted body iron stores. Ferritin is an acute-phase reactant, and >200 µg/L means there is at least some stored iron.
- A: Above 30, so not depleted.
- B: Above 30, so not depleted.
- C: Above 30, so not depleted.
- E: Above 30, so not depleted.
Source: Ch 0066, Nutritional
