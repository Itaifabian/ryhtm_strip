# Ch 80 — Oncologic Emergencies: practice questions

**1.** A 66-year-old man with metastatic prostate cancer has 3 weeks of worsening midthoracic back pain that is worse lying down. Today he has bilateral leg weakness and a sensory level at T10. Bladder function is normal. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Plain thoracic spine films and outpatient follow-up
B. Start dexamethasone immediately and obtain an MRI of the entire spine
C. Bone scan
D. Myelography
E. Lumbar puncture

**2.** A 58-year-old woman with metastatic breast cancer has cord compression at T8. MRI shows vertebral body collapse with retropulsed bone fragments into the canal and spinal instability. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

A. Radiation therapy alone
B. Glucocorticoids alone
C. Vertebroplasty
D. Surgical decompression and stabilization
E. Systemic chemotherapy alone

**3.** A 29-year-old man has 3 weeks of facial swelling worse when lying flat, distended neck veins and chest-wall collaterals. He has no history of cancer. CT shows an anterior mediastinal mass compressing the SVC. He has no stridor and is hemodynamically stable. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Obtain a tissue diagnosis (e.g., biopsy) before starting therapy
B. Empiric high-dose glucocorticoids and radiation before biopsy
C. Immediate thrombolysis
D. Observation only
E. Surgical resection without biopsy

**4.** A 62-year-old woman with lung adenocarcinoma has dyspnea, hypotension, elevated JVP and pulsus paradoxus. Echocardiography shows a large pericardial effusion with right ventricular diastolic collapse. Which of the following is the most appropriate immediate management?

A. IV diuretics
B. Systemic chemotherapy
C. Thoracoscopic pericardial window next week
D. Measure pericardial fluid tumor markers
E. Urgent pericardiocentesis

**5.** A 19-year-old man with bulky Burkitt lymphoma (LDH 2,800 U/L, uric acid 9.8 mg/dL, creatinine 1.4 mg/dL) is about to start chemotherapy. G6PD testing is normal. Which of the following is the most appropriate prophylaxis?

A. Oral fluids and allopurinol only
B. Sodium bicarbonate to alkalinize the urine
C. Aggressive IV hydration plus rasburicase
D. Furosemide without IV fluids
E. Delay chemotherapy until uric acid normalizes without treatment

**6.** A 54-year-old man of Mediterranean descent with high-risk tumor lysis features has known G6PD deficiency. Which of the following agents is contraindicated?

A. Allopurinol
B. Rasburicase
C. Febuxostat
D. IV normal saline
E. Calcium gluconate for symptomatic hypocalcemia

**7.** A resident plans to add sodium bicarbonate to IV fluids to alkalinize the urine for tumor lysis prophylaxis. Which of the following is the best reason to avoid this?

A. Alkalinization lowers uric acid solubility
B. It causes hyperkalemia
C. It is required only with rasburicase
D. High urine pH lowers the solubility of xanthine, hypoxanthine and calcium phosphate, promoting intratubular crystals
E. It increases phosphate excretion too much

**8.** A 57-year-old woman with metastatic melanoma has a generalized seizure. MRI shows two cortical brain metastases. She is starting a tyrosine kinase inhibitor. Which antiseizure drug is preferred?

A. Levetiracetam
B. Phenytoin
C. Carbamazepine
D. Phenobarbital
E. No antiseizure drug

**9.** A 63-year-old man with lung cancer has a newly diagnosed solitary brain metastasis and no history of seizures. Which of the following is recommended regarding antiseizure medication?

A. Start levetiracetam prophylactically for life
B. Start phenytoin prophylactically
C. Start valproate prophylactically
D. Start lacosamide prophylactically
E. Don't give prophylactic antiseizure medication

**10.** A 34-year-old man receiving induction chemotherapy for AML has fever, an ANC of 80/µL, right lower quadrant pain with rebound, and watery diarrhea. CT shows marked cecal wall thickening with mesenteric stranding and no perforation. Which of the following is the most appropriate management?

A. Immediate right hemicolectomy
B. Oral vancomycin alone
C. Broad-spectrum antibiotics, bowel rest, nasogastric suction and myeloid growth factors
D. Colonoscopy with biopsy
E. Loperamide and observation

**11.** A 61-year-old woman finished gemcitabine for pancreatic cancer 6 weeks ago. She now has anemia with schistocytes, platelets of 52,000/µL, rising creatinine and hypertension. Coombs test and coagulation studies are normal. Eculizumab is planned. Which of the following must be done first?

A. Bone marrow biopsy
B. Meningococcal vaccination
C. Restart gemcitabine at a lower dose
D. Platelet transfusion
E. Start warfarin

**12.** A 45-year-old man received CAR T-cell therapy 4 days ago. He now has a temperature of 40°C, hypotension requiring fluids and a vasopressor, and new hypoxemia. Cultures are pending. Which of the following is the most appropriate targeted therapy?

A. Rituximab
B. Infliximab
C. Interferon-γ
D. Granulocyte colony-stimulating factor
E. Tocilizumab

**13.** A 40-year-old woman will receive high-dose ifosfamide for sarcoma. Which measure best prevents hemorrhagic cystitis?

A. Mesna with high urine flow
B. Prophylactic cystoscopy
C. Alum bladder irrigation before each dose
D. Cidofovir
E. Fluid restriction

**14.** A 66-year-old man with a large retroperitoneal sarcoma has recurrent fasting confusion. Glucose during an episode is 38 mg/dL, with suppressed insulin and C-peptide and a low β-hydroxybutyrate. Which mechanism is most likely?

A. Insulinoma
B. Sulfonylurea ingestion
C. Adrenal insufficiency
D. Tumor secretion of incompletely processed (big) IGF-II
E. Pseudohypoglycemia from leukocytosis

**15.** A 59-year-old man completed thoracic radiation for lung cancer 3 months ago. He now has dyspnea, dry cough and low-grade fever, with a hazy infiltrate confined to the radiation field. Infection has been excluded. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

A. No treatment
B. Broad-spectrum antibiotics for 14 days
C. Prednisone
D. Immediate lung biopsy in all cases
E. High-flow oxygen targeting 100% saturation

## Answers

**1. B** — Patients with cancer and back pain need urgent evaluation. Signs of cord compression call for immediate dexamethasone and MRI of the whole spine (multiple epidural sites in 25%). Outcomes are best while patients still walk.
- A: Plain films are insensitive and delay treatment.
- C: Bone scans are less specific.
- D: Myelography is only for patients who can't have MRI.
- E: LP isn't indicated.
Source: Ch 0080, Spinal Cord Compression

**2. D** — With mechanical instability or bone retropulsion into the canal, surgery is the treatment of choice.
- A: Radiation doesn't stabilize the spine.
- B: Steroids alone don't address instability.
- C: Vertebroplasty is for painful fractures without instability.
- E: Chemotherapy is for chemosensitive tumors in non-surgical patients.
Source: Ch 0080, Treatment: Spinal Cord Compression

**3. A** — In a young man, a mediastinal mass with SVCS raises lymphoma vs germ cell tumor. Without known cancer, a detailed evaluation including tissue diagnosis is essential. Biopsy can be done safely. Steroids have a limited role except in lymphoma and could obscure the diagnosis.
- B: Empiric therapy may compromise the diagnosis.
- C: Thrombolysis is for catheter-related thrombosis.
- D: The cause must be found.
- E: Surgery without diagnosis is inappropriate.
Source: Ch 0080, Superior Vena Cava Syndrome

**4. E** — Acute tamponade with hemodynamic instability requires immediate drainage, achieved quickly by pericardiocentesis.
- A: Diuretics worsen preload.
- B: Chemotherapy is too slow.
- C: A window next week is too slow.
- D: Tumor markers in pericardial fluid aren't helpful.
Source: Ch 0080, Treatment: Pericardial Effusion/Tamponade

**5. C** — High risk (bulky Burkitt lymphoma, LDH >1500, hyperuricemia, renal dysfunction) calls for aggressive hydration plus rasburicase, which rapidly converts uric acid to soluble allantoin. G6PD status must be confirmed first.
- A: Insufficient for this level of risk.
- B: Alkalinization is no longer recommended.
- D: Fluids are essential.
- E: Delay is inappropriate.
Source: Ch 0080, Tumor Lysis Syndrome; Treatment

**6. B** — Rasburicase is contraindicated in G6PD deficiency because hydrogen peroxide from the urate oxidase reaction can't be detoxified.
- A: Allopurinol is safe in G6PD deficiency.
- C: Febuxostat is safe.
- D: Saline is safe.
- E: Calcium is safe for symptomatic hypocalcemia.
Source: Ch 0080, Treatment: Tumor Lysis Syndrome

**7. D** — Urinary alkalinization is no longer recommended. It increases uric acid solubility, but high pH decreases the solubility of xanthine, hypoxanthine and calcium phosphate, raising intratubular crystallization risk.
- A: Alkalinization increases uric acid solubility.
- B: Hyperkalemia isn't the rationale.
- C: It isn't required with rasburicase.
- E: Phosphate excretion isn't the rationale.
Source: Ch 0080, Treatment: Tumor Lysis Syndrome

**8. A** — Seizures from brain metastases should be treated with levetiracetam (preferred), lacosamide or lamotrigine, which don't induce CYP450 and won't alter TKI or chemotherapy metabolism.
- B: Phenytoin induces CYP450.
- C: Carbamazepine is also an enzyme inducer.
- D: Phenobarbital is also an enzyme inducer.
- E: A seizure has occurred, so treatment is indicated.
Source: Ch 0080, Treatment: Seizures

**9. E** — Prophylactic anticonvulsant therapy isn't recommended for brain metastases without seizures.
- A: Prophylaxis isn't recommended.
- B: Prophylaxis isn't recommended.
- C: Prophylaxis isn't recommended.
- D: Prophylaxis isn't recommended.
Source: Ch 0080, Treatment: Seizures

**10. C** — Neutropenic enterocolitis (fever, neutropenia, RLQ pain, cecal thickening) is treated with prompt broad-spectrum antibiotics, bowel rest and NG suction. Myeloid growth factors improve outcome. Surgery is reserved for perforation, peritonitis, gangrene or refractory bleeding.
- A: Surgery isn't indicated without those complications.
- B: C. difficile coverage is added only if it can't be excluded; it isn't sole therapy.
- D: Colonoscopy is dangerous here.
- E: Loperamide and observation are inappropriate.
Source: Ch 0080, Neutropenic Enterocolitis

**11. B** — Chemotherapy-associated HUS (mitomycin, gemcitabine) presents 4–8 weeks after the last dose with MAHA, thrombocytopenia and renal failure. Eculizumab is first-line for atypical HUS, and meningococcal vaccination is mandatory before giving it.
- A: Marrow biopsy isn't needed.
- C: The offending drug shouldn't be restarted.
- D: Transfusion can worsen HUS.
- E: Warfarin isn't indicated.
Source: Ch 0080, Hemolytic-Uremic Syndrome

**12. E** — Cytokine release syndrome is the most common acute toxicity of CAR T cells. Tocilizumab, an IL-6 receptor blocker, is effective for severe or life-threatening CRS, alongside supportive care.
- A: Rituximab isn't CRS therapy.
- B: Infliximab isn't the source's therapy.
- C: Interferon-γ is one of the cytokines driving CRS.
- D: G-CSF isn't indicated.
Source: Ch 0080, Human Antibody Infusion Reactions (CRS)

**13. A** — Ifosfamide and cyclophosphamide produce acrolein. Prevention is best: high urine flow and mesna (typically three doses, each 20% of the ifosfamide dose).
- B: Not preventive.
- C: Irrigation treats established bleeding.
- D: Cidofovir has been tried for BK virus cystitis.
- E: Fluid restriction concentrates acrolein.
Source: Ch 0080, Hemorrhagic Cystitis

**14. D** — Large mesenchymal tumors can secrete incompletely processed big IGF-II. It activates insulin receptors, with suppressed insulin and C-peptide, a high IGF-II:IGF-I ratio and low β-hydroxybutyrate.
- A: Insulinoma has high insulin.
- B: Sulfonylureas raise insulin and C-peptide.
- C: Adrenal insufficiency doesn't fit the suppressed insulin with a large tumor.
- E: Pseudohypoglycemia requires hyperleukocytosis.
Source: Ch 0080, Hypoglycemia

**15. C** — Radiation pneumonitis usually develops 2–6 months after radiotherapy with dyspnea, cough, low-grade fever and an infiltrate confined to the field. Prednisone is given to symptomatic patients. Asymptomatic infiltrates don't need treatment.
- A: He's symptomatic, so treatment is indicated.
- B: Infection has been excluded.
- D: Biopsy is needed only if the diagnosis is unclear.
- E: High oxygen can potentiate radiation and drug lung injury; use the lowest adequate FiO₂.
Source: Ch 0080, Pulmonary Infiltrates
