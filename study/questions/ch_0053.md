# Ch 53 — Abdominal Swelling and Ascites: practice questions

**1.** A 57-year-old man with heavy alcohol use has 6 weeks of increasing abdominal girth. Exam shows shifting dullness, spider angiomata and palmar erythema. Ultrasound confirms moderate ascites. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step to determine the cause?

A. Start spironolactone and furosemide without further testing
B. CT of the abdomen
C. Diagnostic paracentesis with ascitic albumin, total protein and cell count, and a simultaneous serum albumin
D. Liver biopsy
E. Laparoscopy with peritoneal biopsy

**2.** A 63-year-old woman with cirrhosis and ascites is admitted with new confusion and low-grade fever. She has no abdominal tenderness. Which of the following is the most appropriate step?

A. Diagnostic paracentesis within 12 hours of admission
B. Lactulose only, with paracentesis if fever persists after 72 hours
C. CT of the head first, then paracentesis in 2 days
D. Empiric antibiotics without paracentesis
E. Large-volume paracentesis only after the encephalopathy resolves

**3.** A 58-year-old man with cirrhotic ascites has an ascitic PMN count of 410/µL. Gram stain is negative, and culture in blood culture bottles later grows E. coli alone. He acquired the infection in the community and isn't critically ill. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

A. Oral ciprofloxacin prophylaxis only
B. Exploratory laparotomy
C. Observation and repeat paracentesis in 1 week
D. Vancomycin alone
E. IV cefotaxime for 5 days, with repeat paracentesis at 48 hours

**4.** A 66-year-old woman has ascites and elevated jugular venous pressure. Ascitic fluid shows a serum-ascites albumin gradient of 1.7 g/dL and total protein of 3.4 g/dL. Which of the following is the most likely cause?

A. Cirrhosis
B. Heart failure
C. Peritoneal carcinomatosis
D. Nephrotic syndrome
E. Tuberculous peritonitis

**5.** A 61-year-old man with cirrhotic ascites has abdominal pain and fever. Ascitic fluid shows a PMN count of 2,400/µL, glucose 28 mg/dL, and an LDH higher than the serum LDH. Gram stain shows multiple organisms. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Cefotaxime alone and repeat paracentesis in 48 hours, as for routine SBP
B. Start SBP prophylaxis
C. Evaluate urgently for a ruptured viscus or abscess (secondary peritonitis)
D. Large-volume paracentesis with albumin
E. Discharge with oral antibiotics

**6.** A 55-year-old woman with newly diagnosed cirrhotic ascites (SAAG 1.8, protein 1.2 g/dL) has moderate ascites. Serum sodium is 134 mEq/L and creatinine is normal. Which of the following is the most appropriate initial management?

A. Fluid restriction to 1 L/day
B. Furosemide monotherapy
C. TIPS
D. Sodium restriction to 2 g/day with spironolactone and furosemide (about 100:40)
E. Lisinopril to reduce portal pressure

**7.** A 60-year-old man on spironolactone 200 mg and furosemide 80 mg for cirrhotic ascites develops painful gynecomastia. His ascites is well controlled. Which of the following is the most appropriate change?

A. Substitute amiloride for spironolactone
B. Stop all diuretics
C. Add an ACE inhibitor
D. Switch to hydrochlorothiazide
E. Increase spironolactone

**8.** A 62-year-old woman with refractory cirrhotic ascites undergoes large-volume paracentesis removing 6 L. What should accompany this procedure to reduce the risk of post-paracentesis circulatory dysfunction?

A. IV normal saline 1 L
B. No additional therapy
C. IV furosemide
D. Oral midodrine only
E. IV albumin, about 6–8 g per liter removed

**9.** Which of the following patients with cirrhosis and ascites should receive prophylactic antibiotics against spontaneous bacterial peritonitis?

A. Ascitic protein 2.1 g/dL, no prior SBP, no bleeding
B. Ascitic fluid total protein 0.7 g/dL
C. Well-controlled ascites on diuretics, protein 1.8 g/dL
D. Recent TIPS with resolved ascites
E. Hepatic encephalopathy controlled on lactulose, ascitic protein 2.5 g/dL

**10.** A 34-year-old man who recently immigrated from a TB-endemic country has ascites without cirrhosis. Ascitic fluid shows a SAAG of 0.7 g/dL, high protein, and predominantly lymphocytes. AFB smear is negative. Ascitic adenosine deaminase is 68 U/L. If the diagnosis remains uncertain, which of the following is the gold standard?

A. Repeat AFB smear
B. Ascitic amylase
C. Ascitic cytology
D. Laparoscopy with peritoneal biopsy for histology and culture
E. Serum BNP

**11.** A 49-year-old man has abdominal distention after retroperitoneal lymph node dissection. Paracentesis yields white, milky fluid. Which ascitic finding confirms the suspected diagnosis?

A. Triglycerides >200 mg/dL
B. Amylase >1000 U/L
C. Bilirubin greater than serum
D. Adenosine deaminase >40 U/L
E. PMN ≥250/µL

**12.** A 59-year-old woman with cirrhosis has a large right pleural effusion and minimal ascites. Pleural fluid shows a serum-to-pleural albumin gradient of 1.4 g/dL with no infection. Which of the following should be avoided?

A. Sodium restriction
B. Diuretics
C. Therapeutic thoracentesis
D. TIPS evaluation
E. Chest tube placement

**13.** A 64-year-old woman with known metastatic ovarian cancer has new ascites. Ascitic fluid shows a SAAG of 0.6 g/dL. Which of the following is true about managing malignant ascites?

A. It responds well to 2 g sodium restriction and spironolactone
B. It is best treated with TIPS
C. It doesn't respond to sodium restriction or diuretics; serial large-volume paracentesis or a drainage catheter is used
D. SAAG ≥1.1 is expected
E. IV albumin is required with every tap of any size

## Answers

**1. C** — The cause of ascites is best determined by paracentesis. Send ascitic albumin, total protein and cell count, with a simultaneous serum albumin to calculate the SAAG.
- A: Treating before diagnosis skips the evaluation.
- B: CT doesn't characterize the fluid.
- D: Biopsy isn't the first step.
- E: Laparoscopy is reserved for an uncertain diagnosis.
Source: Ch 0053, Evaluation

**2. A** — Paracentesis within 12 hours of admission reduces mortality by detecting SBP early. SBP may present only with fever or worsening encephalopathy, and tenderness occurs in just 40%.
- B: Delaying paracentesis is the error.
- C: Delaying paracentesis is the error.
- D: Antibiotics without paracentesis skip the diagnosis.
- E: Delaying paracentesis is the error.
Source: Ch 0053, Complications

**3. E** — SBP (PMN ≥250/µL, single organism) is treated with a third-generation cephalosporin such as cefotaxime 2 g IV q12h. Five days suffices if the patient improves, with repeat paracentesis at 48 hours to confirm a ≥25% PMN fall.
- A: Prophylaxis doesn't treat active infection.
- B: Surgery is for secondary peritonitis.
- C: Observation is inadequate.
- D: Vancomycin alone doesn't cover gram-negative rods.
Source: Ch 0053, Complications

**4. B** — A SAAG ≥1.1 indicates portal hypertension. Ascitic protein ≥2.5 g/dL shows normal sinusoids, as in heart failure or constrictive pericarditis. BNP can help confirm it.
- A: Cirrhosis has protein <2.5.
- C: Carcinomatosis has SAAG <1.1.
- D: Nephrotic syndrome has SAAG <1.1.
- E: TB has SAAG <1.1.
Source: Ch 0053, Evaluation; Fig 53-3

**5. C** — Ascitic glucose <50 mg/dL, ascitic LDH above serum, and multiple organisms suggest secondary peritonitis from a ruptured viscus or abscess, not SBP.
- A: Treating as routine SBP misses a surgical cause.
- B: Prophylaxis doesn't address active infection.
- D: Large-volume paracentesis doesn't address the cause.
- E: Discharge is inappropriate.
Source: Ch 0053, Evaluation; Complications

**6. D** — Start with moderate sodium restriction (2 g/day). Add oral spironolactone and furosemide, usually about 100:40, when sodium restriction alone is inadequate.
- A: Fluid restriction is for sodium <125.
- B: Furosemide alone isn't the recommended regimen.
- C: TIPS is for refractory ascites.
- E: ACE inhibitors should be avoided in cirrhotic ascites.
Source: Ch 0053, Treatment: Ascites

**7. A** — If spironolactone-related gynecomastia is distressing, amiloride (5–40 mg/day) may be substituted.
- B: Stopping diuretics would let ascites reaccumulate.
- C: ACE inhibitors should be avoided.
- D: Thiazides aren't the recommended substitute.
- E: More spironolactone worsens gynecomastia.
Source: Ch 0053, Treatment: Ascites

**8. E** — IV albumin with large-volume paracentesis (>5 L) reduces post-paracentesis circulatory dysfunction and death. Give 6–8 g per liter removed.
- A: Saline isn't the recommended agent.
- B: Omitting replacement raises risk.
- C: Furosemide doesn't prevent circulatory dysfunction.
- D: Midodrine is an adjunct for refractory ascites, not a replacement for albumin.
Source: Ch 0053, Treatment: Ascites

**9. B** — Prophylaxis is indicated for prior SBP, ascitic total protein <1 g/dL, or active GI bleeding.
- A: No criterion is met.
- C: No criterion is met.
- D: No criterion is met.
- E: No criterion is met.
Source: Ch 0053, Complications

**10. D** — TB peritonitis gives low-SAAG, lymphocytic ascites. AFB smear sensitivity is 0–3%. ADA above 30–45 U/L is >90% sensitive in noncirrhotic patients. When the cause remains uncertain, laparoscopy or laparotomy with peritoneal biopsy is the gold standard.
- A: Smear sensitivity is too low.
- B: Amylase is for pancreatic ascites.
- C: Cytology is for carcinomatosis.
- E: BNP is for heart failure.
Source: Ch 0053, Evaluation

**11. A** — White, milky fluid with triglycerides >200 mg/dL (often >1000) is the hallmark of chylous ascites from lymphatic disruption (trauma, surgery, tumor, TB, cirrhosis).
- B: Amylase >1000 indicates pancreatic ascites.
- C: High bilirubin indicates biliary perforation.
- D: ADA suggests TB.
- E: PMN ≥250 indicates SBP.
Source: Ch 0053, Evaluation

**12. E** — Hepatic hydrothorax is treated like cirrhotic ascites (sodium restriction, diuretics, thoracentesis or TIPS as needed). Chest tube placement should be avoided.
- A: Sodium restriction is part of treatment.
- B: Diuretics are part of treatment.
- C: Thoracentesis is part of treatment.
- D: TIPS is an option for refractory cases.
Source: Ch 0053, Complications

**13. C** — Malignant ascites doesn't respond to sodium restriction or diuretics. Serial large-volume paracentesis, a transcutaneous drainage catheter, rarely a peritoneovenous shunt, or the Alfapump are used.
- A: Sodium restriction and diuretics don't work.
- B: TIPS treats portal hypertensive ascites.
- D: Carcinomatosis gives SAAG <1.1.
- E: The albumin recommendation is specific to large-volume paracentesis in cirrhosis.
Source: Ch 0053, Treatment: Ascites; Evaluation
