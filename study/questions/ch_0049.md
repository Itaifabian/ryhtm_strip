# Ch 49 — Diarrhea and Constipation: practice questions

**1.** A 34-year-old woman has 2 days of frequent bloody stools, cramping abdominal pain and a temperature of 39.1°C (102.4°F). She is mildly orthostatic. Which of the following is the most appropriate management?

A. Loperamide and observation
B. Colonoscopy with biopsies today
C. Bismuth subsalicylate alone
D. Oral or IV rehydration, stool microbiologic testing, and avoidance of antimotility agents
E. Celiac serologies

**2.** A 26-year-old man has had watery diarrhea for 1 day, 4 stools daily, with no blood or fever. He is drinking well and is not orthostatic. He takes no medications and hasn't traveled. Which of the following is the most appropriate management?

A. Oral fluid and electrolyte replacement without stool testing
B. Stool multiplex PCR panel
C. Empiric ciprofloxacin for 5 days
D. Flexible sigmoidoscopy
E. Stool ova and parasites ×3

**3.** A 31-year-old woman returned from a 2-week mountain backpacking trip 3 weeks ago, where she drank stream water. She now has persistent bloating, flatulence and loose, mushy stools without blood or fever. Which of the following is a reasonable, cost-effective approach?

A. Colonoscopy
B. Empiric ciprofloxacin
C. Empiric metronidazole for likely giardiasis
D. Lactose breath test
E. Loperamide only

**4.** Several people develop abrupt, intense vomiting with watery diarrhea and no fever about 2 hours after a picnic where cream-filled pastries sat in the sun. Which mechanism is most likely?

A. Invasive Shigella infection
B. Enterohemorrhagic E. coli cytotoxin
C. Giardia enteroadherence
D. Norovirus
E. Preformed Staphylococcus aureus toxin

**5.** Which of the following travelers to a high-risk country is the best candidate for antibiotic prophylaxis against traveler's diarrhea?

A. A healthy 25-year-old student
B. A 40-year-old man with hemochromatosis
C. A 60-year-old woman with controlled hypertension
D. A 35-year-old woman on oral contraceptives
E. A 50-year-old man with well-controlled asthma

**6.** A 58-year-old woman taking sertraline and omeprazole has 4 months of painless watery diarrhea, including at night. Stool studies are negative, celiac serology is negative, and colonoscopy shows normal-appearing mucosa. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Reassurance and diagnosis of IBS
B. Capsule endoscopy
C. Repeat colonoscopy in 1 year
D. Random colonic mucosal biopsies (if not obtained)
E. Fecal elastase

**7.** A 45-year-old man had 60 cm of terminal ileum resected for Crohn disease. He now has postprandial watery diarrhea. There's no evidence of active Crohn disease. Which of the following is the most appropriate initial therapy?

A. Bile acid sequestrant (e.g., cholestyramine)
B. Pancreatic enzyme replacement
C. Gluten-free diet
D. Metronidazole
E. Octreotide

**8.** A 40-year-old woman has chronic watery diarrhea that stops when she fasts. Stool sodium is 30 mEq/L and potassium is 25 mEq/L. Assuming serum osmolality is 290 mosm/kg, which of the following best characterizes her diarrhea?

A. Secretory, with no osmotic gap
B. Inflammatory
C. Osmotic, with an osmotic gap of 180 mosm/kg
D. Steatorrheal
E. Factitious from added urine

**9.** A 52-year-old man has massive watery diarrhea (4 L/d) persisting with fasting, severe hypokalemia, and low gastric acid. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Gastrinoma
B. Carcinoid syndrome
C. Lactase deficiency
D. Microscopic colitis
E. VIPoma

**10.** A 27-year-old woman has 1 year of crampy lower abdominal pain relieved by defecation and loose stools that never occur at night. She has no weight loss, bleeding or anemia. Basic labs and celiac serology are normal. Which of the following is recommended to exclude IBD and microscopic colitis?

A. CT enterography
B. Flexible sigmoidoscopy with colorectal biopsies
C. Capsule endoscopy
D. Serum gastrin
E. 72-hour fecal fat

**11.** A 63-year-old man has 4 months of new constipation, a 5-kg weight loss, and iron-deficiency anemia. Rectal exam shows no impaction. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Fiber supplementation and follow-up in 3 months
B. Colonic transit study
C. Anorectal manometry
D. Colonoscopy
E. Start linaclotide

**12.** A 38-year-old woman has chronic constipation with excessive straining and a need to digitally evacuate stool. On rectal exam, the puborectalis contracts paradoxically during simulated defecation, and anorectal manometry with balloon expulsion confirms an evacuation disorder. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

A. Subtotal colectomy with ileorectostomy
B. Botulinum toxin injection of the puborectalis
C. Internal anal sphincter division
D. Biofeedback pelvic floor retraining
E. Long-term stimulant laxatives alone

**13.** A 24-year-old man has had lifelong severe constipation. Anorectal manometry shows absence of the rectoanal inhibitory reflex, and his rectum isn't enlarged. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Slow-transit constipation
B. Irritable bowel syndrome
C. Hirschsprung disease
D. Descending perineum syndrome
E. Anal fissure

**14.** A 33-year-old nurse is evaluated for unexplained chronic watery diarrhea and hypokalemia. The stool osmolality submitted is very low, well below serum osmolality. Which of the following is the most likely explanation?

A. Bile acid diarrhea
B. VIPoma
C. Lactase deficiency
D. Addition of urine to the stool sample
E. Addition of water to the stool sample

**15.** A 76-year-old man with a mechanical aortic valve has 2 days of moderate acute diarrhea. Stool testing is pending. Which of the following is the most appropriate management in addition to fluid replacement?

A. Withhold antibiotics until a pathogen is identified
B. Start antibiotic therapy whether or not a pathogen is found
C. Loperamide alone
D. Bismuth subsalicylate alone
E. Colonoscopy

## Answers

**1. D** — Overtly bloody stools, fever ≥38.5°C and dehydration are indications for stool microbiologic studies. Fluid and electrolyte replacement is central. Antimotility agents should be avoided in febrile dysentery, which they may prolong.
- A: Loperamide is contraindicated in febrile dysentery.
- B: Endoscopy has little role in acute diarrhea except in immunocompromised patients.
- C: Bismuth alone doesn't address the evaluation.
- E: There's no indication for serologies during acute enteritis.
Source: Ch 0049, Approach to the Patient: Acute Diarrhea; Treatment: Acute Diarrhea

**2. A** — Most acute diarrhea is mild and self-limited and doesn't justify diagnostic or drug interventions. Fluid replacement alone may suffice.
- B: None of the testing indications are present (dehydration, blood, fever ≥38.5°C, >48 h, antibiotics, age ≥70, immunocompromise).
- C: Antibiotics are for moderate–severe febrile illness.
- D: Endoscopy isn't indicated.
- E: Testing isn't indicated.
Source: Ch 0049, Approach to the Patient: Acute Diarrhea

**3. C** — A therapeutic trial is often definitive and cost-effective. Bloating and diarrhea since a backpacking trip warrant metronidazole for likely giardiasis, the most common cause of persistent diarrhea.
- A: Colonoscopy isn't indicated at this stage.
- B: Ciprofloxacin doesn't treat Giardia.
- D: The history points to Giardia.
- E: Loperamide alone leaves the infection untreated.
Source: Ch 0049, Approach to the Patient: Chronic Diarrhea; Acute Diarrhea (persistent)

**4. E** — Abrupt vomiting with minimal or no fever within hours of eating suggests a preformed toxin. S. aureus comes from mayonnaise or creams (incubation 1–8 h).
- A: Invasive organisms cause high fever and dysentery after a longer incubation.
- B: EHEC incubates 12–72 h with bloody diarrhea.
- C: Giardia incubates 1–8 days.
- D: Norovirus incubates 1–3 days.
Source: Ch 0049, Table 49-2; Infectious Agents

**5. B** — Prophylaxis is indicated only for travelers in whom diarrhea would be especially likely or serious: immunocompromise, IBD, hemochromatosis, or gastric achlorhydria.
- A: Healthy travelers don't need prophylaxis.
- C: Controlled hypertension isn't an indication.
- D: Oral contraceptives aren't an indication.
- E: Controlled asthma isn't an indication.
Source: Ch 0049, Treatment: Acute Diarrhea

**6. D** — Microscopic colitis causes chronic watery diarrhea, especially in middle-aged women and those on NSAIDs, statins, PPIs and SSRIs. Biopsy of a normal-appearing colon is required.
- A: Labeling it IBS misses a treatable diagnosis.
- B: The colon hasn't been sampled.
- C: Waiting misses the diagnosis.
- E: Fecal elastase tests for pancreatic insufficiency, which doesn't fit watery diarrhea.
Source: Ch 0049, Idiopathic Inflammatory Bowel Disease (microscopic colitis)

**7. A** — Resection of less than 100 cm of terminal ileum causes bile acid malabsorption and colonic secretion. Postprandial diarrhea after ileal resection can be treated with cholestyramine, colestipol or colesevelam.
- B: Pancreatic enzymes treat pancreatic insufficiency.
- C: A gluten-free diet treats celiac disease.
- D: Metronidazole treats Giardia or bacterial overgrowth, which isn't suggested.
- E: Octreotide treats carcinoid.
Source: Ch 0049, Bowel Resection; Approach to the Patient: Chronic Diarrhea

**8. C** — Osmotic gap = 290 − 2 × (30 + 25) = 180. A gap >50 with cessation on fasting indicates osmotic diarrhea (e.g., magnesium or poorly absorbed carbohydrates).
- A: Secretory diarrhea has no gap and persists with fasting.
- B: The data don't indicate inflammation.
- D: The data don't indicate steatorrhea.
- E: Added urine raises stool osmolality.
Source: Ch 0049, Osmotic Causes

**9. E** — VIPoma (pancreatic cholera) causes watery diarrhea, hypokalemia and achlorhydria, often >3 L/d, persisting with fasting.
- A: Gastrinoma causes acid hypersecretion with refractory ulcers.
- B: Carcinoid features flushing and wheezing.
- C: Lactase deficiency is osmotic and stops with fasting.
- D: Microscopic colitis doesn't cause achlorhydria or massive volumes.
Source: Ch 0049, Hormones

**10. B** — Patients suspected of having IBS should first have flexible sigmoidoscopy with colorectal biopsies to exclude IBD and especially microscopic colitis, which is clinically indistinguishable from IBS-D.
- A: CT enterography isn't the recommended first step.
- C: Capsule endoscopy isn't the recommended first step.
- D: Gastrin isn't the recommended first step.
- E: Fecal fat isn't the recommended first step.
Source: Ch 0049, Approach to the Patient: Chronic Diarrhea

**11. D** — Weight loss, rectal bleeding or anemia with constipation mandate structural evaluation. Colonoscopy alone is most cost-effective because it allows biopsy and intervention.
- A: Waiting delays evaluation for colorectal cancer.
- B: Transit study comes after excluding structural disease.
- C: Manometry comes after excluding structural disease.
- E: Symptomatic therapy delays evaluation.
Source: Ch 0049, Approach to the Patient: Constipation

**12. D** — Pelvic floor dysfunction usually responds to biofeedback, with 70–80% success after 3 months.
- A: Colectomy shouldn't be done with an ongoing evacuation disorder.
- B: Botulinum has had mediocre success and is largely abandoned.
- C: Sphincter division has had mediocre success and is largely abandoned.
- E: Laxatives alone don't fix the dyssynergia.
Source: Ch 0049, Treatment: Constipation

**13. C** — Anorectal manometry identifies Hirschsprung disease by absence of the rectoanal inhibitory reflex. The reflex can also be absent with an enlarged rectum, which isn't present here.
- A: These conditions don't explain an absent RAIR with a normal-sized rectum.
- B: Same reason.
- D: Same reason.
- E: Same reason.
Source: Ch 0049, Anorectal and Pelvic Floor Tests

**14. E** — In factitious diarrhea, very low stool osmolarity suggests water added to stool; very high suggests urine. These patients are often women in health care careers with hypokalemia.
- A: Bile acid diarrhea doesn't produce stool osmolality far below serum.
- B: VIPoma doesn't either.
- C: Lactase deficiency doesn't either.
- D: Added urine produces high osmolality.
Source: Ch 0049, Factitial Causes

**15. B** — Antibiotics are indicated, whether or not an organism is found, in patients who are immunocompromised, have mechanical heart valves or recent vascular grafts, or are elderly.
- A: Waiting is inappropriate in this high-risk host.
- C: Symptomatic therapy alone is insufficient.
- D: Symptomatic therapy alone is insufficient.
- E: Endoscopy isn't indicated.
Source: Ch 0049, Treatment: Acute Diarrhea
