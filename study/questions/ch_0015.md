# Ch 15 — Chest Discomfort: practice questions

**1.** A 61-year-old man walks into the emergency department with 40 minutes of substernal pressure radiating to his left arm. He has hypertension and smokes. BP 150/90 mm Hg, pulse 96/min, SpO2 97% on room air. He is diaphoretic. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Chest x-ray
B. 12-lead ECG
C. Coronary CT angiography
D. Exercise treadmill test
E. Transthoracic echocardiography

**2.** A 54-year-old woman presents 1 hour after the onset of intermittent chest tightness. Her ECG is normal. A high-sensitivity troponin drawn on arrival is just below the 99th percentile. She is pain-free. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Discharge home with outpatient follow-up
B. Repeat high-sensitivity troponin in 1–3 hours
C. Repeat troponin in 24 hours
D. Immediate coronary angiography
E. D-dimer

**3.** A 59-year-old man with suspected ACS has ongoing chest pain. His initial 12-lead ECG shows only nonspecific T-wave flattening. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step regarding the ECG?

A. No further ECGs are needed
B. Repeat ECG only if troponin is elevated
C. Repeat ECGs every 30–60 minutes and consider right-sided leads
D. Obtain a Holter monitor
E. Perform an exercise ECG now

**4.** A 32-year-old man has 3 days of sharp retrosternal chest pain that is worse lying flat and with deep breaths and improves when he sits up and leans forward. The pain radiates to the trapezius ridge. He had a viral upper respiratory infection last week. Temperature 37.9°C (100.2°F). A scratchy sound is heard at the left sternal border. ECG shows ST elevation in most leads with PR-segment depression. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Anterior STEMI
B. Acute pericarditis
C. Pulmonary embolism
D. Costochondritis
E. Esophageal spasm

**5.** A 64-year-old man with poorly controlled hypertension has sudden, severe anterior chest pain that "rips" through to his back between the shoulder blades. BP 188/104 mm Hg in the right arm and 150/88 mm Hg in the left arm. A diastolic decrescendo murmur is heard at the left sternal border. ECG shows LVH without acute ST changes. Which of the following is the most appropriate imaging study?

A. Transthoracic echocardiography
B. CT angiography of the chest
C. Exercise stress echocardiography
D. Ventilation-perfusion scan
E. Cardiac MRI as the first test

**6.** A 21-year-old tall, thin man has sudden right-sided pleuritic chest pain and mild shortness of breath that began while he was sitting at his desk. He smokes. Pulse 104/min, respirations 20/min, SpO2 95%. Breath sounds are decreased over the right hemithorax. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Chest x-ray
B. High-sensitivity troponin
C. Coronary CT angiography
D. Echocardiography
E. Upper endoscopy

**7.** A 46-year-old woman has chest pain that resolved before arrival. Her history is low suspicion for ACS, ECG is normal, she has one cardiac risk factor, and her HEART score (without troponin) is 2. High-sensitivity troponin at arrival and at 2 hours are below the limit of quantification. Which of the following is the most appropriate disposition?

A. Admit for stress testing within 72 hours
B. Coronary CT angiography before discharge
C. Discharge without further cardiac testing
D. Invasive coronary angiography
E. Admit for serial troponins over 24 hours

**8.** A 57-year-old man without known coronary disease presents with chest discomfort. Serial high-sensitivity troponins are negative and the ECG is nondiagnostic, but his clinical pathway score places him at intermediate risk. He has normal renal function. Which of the following is the most appropriate next diagnostic test?

A. Coronary CT angiography
B. Repeat troponin in 24 hours
C. Invasive coronary angiography
D. Transthoracic echocardiography
E. No further testing

**9.** A 68-year-old man with prior PCI to the LAD presents with exertional chest pressure. Serial troponins are negative, ECG is unchanged from baseline, and he is at intermediate risk. He can walk on a treadmill. Which of the following is the most appropriate next test?

A. Coronary CT angiography
B. Exercise ECG without imaging
C. Exercise stress test with myocardial imaging (nuclear or echocardiographic)
D. Pharmacologic stress test
E. No testing

**10.** A 52-year-old woman tells her primary care physician that she had 20 minutes of chest pressure and nausea at rest this morning; she is now pain-free. Her physician is concerned enough to consider checking a troponin level. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Order an outpatient troponin and call her with results
B. Schedule an outpatient stress test next week
C. Refer her to the emergency department now
D. Start a proton pump inhibitor trial
E. Reassure her because she is pain-free

**11.** A 70-year-old man on hemodialysis has an elevated high-sensitivity troponin during a routine evaluation for knee surgery. He has no chest pain, and his ECG is unchanged. Repeat troponin 3 hours later is unchanged. Which of the following best describes this finding?

A. Type 1 myocardial infarction
B. Type 2 myocardial infarction
C. Chronic myocardial injury
D. Acute myocarditis
E. Unstable angina

**12.** Which of the following features in a patient's description of chest pain most increases the likelihood of acute myocardial infarction?

A. Pain reproduced by palpation
B. Pain described as sharp
C. Pain radiating to both arms
D. Pleuritic pain
E. Positional pain

**13.** A 67-year-old woman develops sudden chest pain and dyspnea an hour after learning of her husband's sudden death. ECG shows anterior ST-segment elevation, and troponin is elevated. Emergency coronary angiography shows no obstructive coronary disease. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Takotsubo (stress-related) cardiomyopathy
B. Acute pericarditis
C. Panic attack
D. Aortic dissection
E. Esophageal spasm

---

## Answers

**1. B** — An ECG within 10 minutes of presentation is recommended for all patients with acute chest discomfort, mainly to identify STEMI needing immediate reperfusion.
- A: CXR is routine but doesn't come before the ECG.
- C: CTA is for intermediate-risk patients after ACS is excluded.
- D: An exercise test with possible ongoing ACS is unsafe.
- E: Echo isn't routine first-line.
Source: Ch 0015, Electrocardiography

**2. B** — High-sensitivity troponin is measured at presentation and repeated at 1–3 h. A change between samples is needed to identify acute injury. She presented only 1 hour after onset, so a single value can't exclude MI.
- A: Premature; very early presenters need serial testing.
- C: Far too long a delay.
- D: There is no STEMI or high-risk feature.
- E: PE isn't suggested.
Source: Ch 0015, Cardiac Biomarkers

**3. C** — Serial ECGs every 30–60 min are recommended early in suspected ACS. Right-sided leads should be considered when the standard ECG is nondiagnostic, because resting ECG sensitivity can be as low as 20%.
- A, B: A single nondiagnostic ECG doesn't exclude ischemia.
- D: Ambulatory monitoring isn't for acute ACS evaluation.
- E: Stress testing during ongoing pain is inappropriate.
Source: Ch 0015, Electrocardiography

**4. B** — Pleuritic pain relieved by sitting forward, radiation to the trapezius ridge, a friction rub, and diffuse ST elevation with PR depression point to pericarditis.
- A: STEMI follows a coronary territory without PR depression.
- C: PE pain is lateral and pleuritic, with tachycardia and dyspnea.
- D: Doesn't explain the ECG or the rub.
- E: Spasm causes squeezing, angina-like pain.
Source: Ch 0015, Table 15-1; Electrocardiography; History (location)

**5. B** — Tearing pain radiating between the scapulae, hypertension, an arm BP differential, and an AI murmur point to acute aortic syndrome. CT (or TEE) is the practical diagnostic test.
- A: TTE is poorly sensitive for dissection.
- C: Stress testing is dangerous here.
- D: V/Q is for PE.
- E: MRI is accurate but infrequently used first.
Source: Ch 0015, Diseases of the Aorta; Other Noninvasive Studies

**6. A** — Sudden pleuritic pain and decreased unilateral breath sounds in a young, tall, thin male smoker suggest primary spontaneous pneumothorax. Chest radiography is most useful for pulmonary processes such as pneumothorax.
- B: Not an ACS presentation.
- C, D: Don't address the likely diagnosis.
- E: Not a GI picture.
Source: Ch 0015, Pneumothorax; Chest Radiography

**7. C** — With a low-risk clinical pathway (HEART ≤3) and negative serial high-sensitivity troponins, patients can be discharged without further testing. There's insufficient evidence that stress testing or imaging improves outcomes in this group.
- A: Routine stress testing within 72 h has fallen out of favor.
- B: CTA is for intermediate risk.
- D: Invasive angiography isn't indicated.
- E: Serial testing is already complete.
Source: Ch 0015, Integrative Decision-Aids; Exercise Electrocardiography; Fig 15-4

**8. A** — For intermediate-risk patients without known CAD after ACS is excluded, coronary CTA is a preferred test. Its strength is the negative predictive value of no stenosis.
- B: Prolonged serial troponins add little.
- C: Too invasive at this stage.
- D: Resting echo isn't routinely needed.
- E: Intermediate risk warrants testing.
Source: Ch 0015, Coronary and Myocardial Stress Imaging (CT Angiography)

**9. C** — With known obstructive coronary disease, functional testing with stress nuclear or echo imaging is preferred over CTA. Exercise is preferred over pharmacologic stress when the patient can exercise.
- A: Less useful with known CAD.
- B: With known CAD, exercise ECG should be combined with imaging.
- D: He can exercise.
- E: Intermediate risk warrants testing.
Source: Ch 0015, Stress Nuclear Perfusion Imaging or Stress Echocardiography; Exercise Electrocardiography

**10. C** — In the outpatient setting, if suspicion of ACS is high enough to consider troponin testing, the patient should be referred to the ED.
- A: Delays care for a possible ACS.
- B: A stress test is unsafe before ACS is excluded.
- D: Assumes a GI cause.
- E: Resolution of pain doesn't exclude ACS.
Source: Ch 0015, Outpatient Evaluation of Chest Discomfort

**11. C** — A stable (non-dynamic) troponin elevation without ischemia represents chronic myocardial injury, seen for example in end-stage renal disease. MI requires a rising and/or falling pattern caused by ischemia.
- A, B: Both require a dynamic troponin with ischemia.
- D: Myocarditis causes acute (dynamic) injury.
- E: Requires ischemic symptoms.
Source: Ch 0015, Cardiac Biomarkers; Fig 15-3

**12. C** — Radiation to both arms has a particularly high association with MI.
- A, B, D, E: Pain that is palpation-reproducible, sharp, pleuritic, or positional each lowers the likelihood of AMI.
Source: Ch 0015, History (Location of Discomfort); Fig 15-2

**13. A** — Takotsubo cardiomyopathy is triggered by emotional or physical stress and mimics MI, with ST elevation and elevated biomarkers but no obstructive coronary disease.
- B: Pericarditis would show diffuse ST elevation and pleuritic pain.
- C: Panic doesn't raise troponin.
- D: Dissection presents with tearing pain radiating to the back.
- E: Spasm doesn't cause ST elevation and troponin rise in this pattern.
Source: Ch 0015, Pericardial and Other Myocardial Diseases
