# Ch 31 — Dementia: practice questions

**1.** A 74-year-old woman is brought in by her daughter for 18 months of gradually worsening forgetfulness. She repeats questions and has missed bill payments. She takes no sedating medications. Neurologic examination is normal. Which of the following is the most appropriate initial evaluation?

A. Amyloid PET scan
B. Lumbar puncture for CSF Aβ42 and tau
C. CBC, metabolic panel, TSH, vitamin B12 level, and brain MRI or CT
D. EEG
E. Apolipoprotein E genotyping

**2.** A 72-year-old man has had 1 year of a slow, unsteady gait and progressive forgetfulness. MRI shows dilation of the lateral, third and fourth ventricles with a patent aqueduct, gaping Sylvian fissures, and crowding of the gyri at the vertex, with minimal cortical atrophy. Which of the following is the treatment most likely to improve his condition?

A. Ventriculoperitoneal shunting
B. Donepezil
C. Memantine
D. Levodopa
E. Lecanemab

**3.** An 81-year-old woman with moderate Alzheimer disease in a nursing home has become newly agitated and combative over the past 4 days, especially during care. Her medications haven't changed. Which of the following is the most appropriate first step?

A. Start haloperidol
B. Start lorazepam as needed
C. Start diphenhydramine at night
D. Increase her donepezil dose
E. Evaluate for pain, constipation, urinary or respiratory infection, and electrolyte abnormalities

**4.** A 70-year-old man has 1 year of fluctuating attention and alertness, well-formed visual hallucinations of children in his house, and mild bradykinesia with rigidity. His wife says that for years he has acted out his dreams, punching and kicking in his sleep. Memory is relatively preserved, but he can't copy a clock. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Alzheimer disease
B. Dementia with Lewy bodies
C. Frontotemporal dementia
D. Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease
E. Vascular dementia

**5.** A 75-year-old man with dementia with Lewy bodies has distressing visual hallucinations and apathy. Which of the following is the most appropriate pharmacologic treatment?

A. Memantine
B. Haloperidol
C. Diphenhydramine
D. A cholinesterase inhibitor
E. Lorazepam

**6.** A 68-year-old woman has mild cognitive impairment with prominent memory deficits. She and her physician are considering treatment with lecanemab. Which of the following should be done before starting therapy?

A. Confirm amyloid pathology by PET or CSF biomarkers and obtain APOE genotyping
B. Confirm the diagnosis with an FDG-PET scan alone
C. Start memantine first for 6 months
D. Obtain a brain biopsy
E. No testing is needed if the clinical picture is typical

**7.** A 71-year-old man receiving donanemab for early Alzheimer disease develops sudden right-sided weakness and aphasia. He arrives within 1 hour of onset, and CT shows no hemorrhage. Which of the following is contraindicated?

A. Brain MRI
B. CT angiography
C. Intravenous thrombolysis
D. Admission to a stroke unit
E. Swallow screening before oral intake

**8.** A 58-year-old man's wife reports 2 years of personality change. He has become apathetic, tells crude jokes to strangers, has lost empathy for family members, compulsively eats sweets, and has gained 10 kg. Memory and drawing are relatively spared. Which of the following imaging findings is most likely?

A. Hippocampal and posterior parietal atrophy
B. Cortical ribboning on diffusion-weighted MRI
C. Multiple lacunar infarcts and confluent white matter disease
D. Occipital hypometabolism with sparing of the posterior cingulate
E. Frontal, insular and anterior temporal atrophy

**9.** A 63-year-old woman has rapidly progressive cognitive decline over 4 months. She now has startle-sensitive myoclonus and rigidity. Which of the following evaluations is most appropriate?

A. Neuropsychological testing alone, repeated in 6 months
B. Diffusion-weighted brain MRI and EEG
C. Amyloid PET
D. Start donepezil and reassess in 3 months
E. APOE genotyping

**10.** A 66-year-old man has 3 months of subacute memory loss and frequent brief episodes of jerking of the face and arm on one side. MRI shows bilateral mesial temporal FLAIR hyperintensity. Which antibody is most likely?

A. NMDA receptor
B. Caspr2
C. AMPA receptor
D. LGI1
E. Anti-GBM

**11.** A 69-year-old man with 15 years of H2-receptor antagonist use for reflux has progressive cognitive decline, numbness in his feet, and loss of vibration and position sense with brisk knee reflexes. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Start donepezil
B. Amyloid PET
C. Measure the vitamin B12 level and treat deficiency
D. Lumbar puncture for 14-3-3 protein
E. Start memantine

**12.** A 66-year-old retired accountant reports trouble keeping track of appointments and recalling recent conversations over the past year. His wife confirms a decline, but he still manages his finances and daily activities. His MoCA score is normal. Laboratory tests and MRI are unremarkable. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Reassure him that memory loss is normal for age, with no further testing
B. Start donepezil
C. Comprehensive neuropsychological testing
D. Start lecanemab
E. Brain biopsy

**13.** An 88-year-old woman has 5 years of slowly progressive episodic memory loss with other cognitive domains relatively spared. MRI shows marked anterior-predominant hippocampal and medial temporal atrophy. Amyloid PET is negative. Which of the following is the most likely underlying pathology?

A. Alzheimer disease
B. Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease
C. Frontotemporal lobar degeneration with tau
D. Dementia with Lewy bodies
E. Limbic-predominant age-related TDP-43 encephalopathy (LATE)

**14.** A 70-year-old woman's husband died 4 months ago. Since then she reports poor concentration and forgetfulness, insomnia, and a 5-kg weight loss. On testing, she gives up easily on memory tasks. Laboratory tests and MRI are normal. Which of the following is the most appropriate management?

A. Start donepezil
B. Start an SSRI and reassess cognition after the mood symptoms resolve
C. Start quetiapine
D. Start lorazepam for sleep
E. Refer for anti-amyloid therapy

## Answers

**1. C** — The American Academy of Neurology recommends routine CBC, electrolytes, glucose, renal, liver and thyroid function, a B12 level, and structural neuroimaging (MRI or CT) to avoid missing reversible causes.
- A: Amyloid PET is an optional focused test, not part of the initial screen.
- B: LP is indicated for suspected CNS infection or inflammation, or in place of PET for AD biomarkers.
- D: EEG is only occasionally helpful (CJD, seizures).
- E: ApoE testing is an optional focused test.
Source: Ch 0031, Laboratory Tests; Table 31-3

**2. A** — Communicating hydrocephalus with vertex crowding and gaping Sylvian fissures despite minimal atrophy suggests normal-pressure hydrocephalus, which is treated with ventricular shunting.
- B: Cholinesterase inhibitors treat AD and don't address NPH.
- C: Memantine treats moderate–severe AD.
- D: The gait disorder is from hydrocephalus, not parkinsonism.
- E: Anti-amyloid antibodies are for early AD with confirmed amyloid.
Source: Ch 0031, Laboratory Tests (Fig 31-3); Treatment: Dementia

**3. E** — Before treating behavioral symptoms with drugs, look aggressively for modifiable causes: hunger, toothache or other pain, constipation, urinary or respiratory infection, electrolyte imbalance, drug toxicity.
- A: Antipsychotics carry significant risk, including increased mortality in dementia.
- B: Benzodiazepines can cause sedation and paradoxical disinhibition.
- C: Anticholinergic drugs should be avoided.
- D: Raising cognitive drug doses doesn't address an acute trigger.
Source: Ch 0031, Treatment: Dementia

**4. B** — Fluctuating cognition, early visual hallucinations, parkinsonism and REM sleep behavior disorder, with poor drawing but spared memory, characterize DLB.
- A: AD starts with memory loss and a normal motor exam.
- C: FTD presents with behavioral change or language deficits, with drawing spared.
- D: CJD is rapidly progressive with myoclonus.
- E: Vascular dementia features stepwise decline, focal signs and white matter disease.
Source: Ch 0031, Table 31-4; History

**5. D** — Apathy, visual hallucinations and other psychiatric symptoms sometimes respond to cholinesterase inhibitors, especially in DLB, avoiding more toxic therapies.
- A: Memantine should be used with great caution, or not at all, in DLB because it can worsen agitation and confusion.
- B: Antipsychotics have significant risks, including increased mortality in dementia.
- C: Anticholinergics should be avoided.
- E: Benzodiazepines should be avoided.
Source: Ch 0031, Treatment: Dementia

**6. A** — Candidates for anti-Aβ antibodies need early-stage AD with amyloid confirmed by PET or CSF. APOE genotyping is recommended because ε4 carriers have a higher ARIA risk.
- B: FDG-PET shows hypometabolism but doesn't confirm amyloid.
- C: Memantine is for moderate–severe AD and isn't a prerequisite.
- D: Brain biopsy is reserved for vasculitis, treatable tumors or unusual infections.
- E: Amyloid confirmation is required.
Source: Ch 0031, Treatment: Dementia

**7. C** — Thrombolytic agents are contraindicated in patients treated with anti-Aβ monoclonal antibodies because of reported catastrophic, fatal intracerebral hemorrhages.
- A: Imaging is appropriate and not contraindicated.
- B: Vascular imaging is likewise not contraindicated.
- D: Stroke-unit care is appropriate.
- E: Swallow screening is routine supportive stroke care.
Source: Ch 0031, Treatment: Dementia

**8. E** — Apathy, disinhibition, loss of empathy and compulsive overeating with spared memory and drawing indicate behavioral FTD, with focal frontal, insular and anterior temporal atrophy.
- A: Hippocampal and posterior atrophy is the AD pattern.
- B: Cortical ribboning is CJD.
- C: Infarcts and white matter disease indicate vascular dementia.
- D: The cingulate island sign is DLB.
Source: Ch 0031, Table 31-4; Laboratory Tests

**9. B** — Rapid progression with startle-sensitive myoclonus and rigidity suggests CJD. Diffusion MRI shows cortical ribbon or basal ganglia restricted diffusion in most patients, and EEG may show periodic complexes.
- A: Waiting 6 months is inappropriate for a rapidly progressive dementia.
- C: Amyloid PET evaluates AD.
- D: Empiric AD treatment doesn't address the differential of rapidly progressive dementia.
- E: Genotyping doesn't diagnose CJD.
Source: Ch 0031, History; Laboratory Tests

**10. D** — LGI1 antibody encephalitis causes subacute amnesia with faciobrachial dystonic seizures and mesial temporal T2/FLAIR changes.
- A: NMDA receptor encephalitis features psychosis, insomnia and dyskinesias.
- B: Caspr2 causes insomnia, ataxia and myotonia.
- C: AMPA receptor causes limbic encephalitis with relapses, without the characteristic faciobrachial seizures.
- E: Anti-GBM causes pulmonary-renal syndrome.
Source: Ch 0031, History

**11. C** — Dementia with myelopathy (dorsal column loss, brisk reflexes) and peripheral neuropathy suggests B12 deficiency, a reversible cause. Chronic H2-blocker therapy is a listed risk factor.
- A: Donepezil skips a reversible cause.
- B: Amyloid PET skips a reversible cause.
- D: Nothing suggests CJD.
- E: Memantine is for moderate–severe AD.
Source: Ch 0031, History; Physical and Neurologic Examination; Treatment

**12. C** — In most patients with MCI, bedside screening tests may be normal. A more comprehensive neuropsychological evaluation is needed.
- A: An informant-confirmed decline deserves evaluation, not reassurance.
- B: There's no diagnosis to justify donepezil.
- D: Anti-amyloid therapy requires confirmed amyloid pathology.
- E: Brain biopsy isn't indicated.
Source: Ch 0031, Cognitive and Neuropsychiatric Examination

**13. E** — In older patients with progressive amnestic decline and hippocampal atrophy, a negative amyloid PET strongly suggests LATE, which is anterior-predominant, memory-limited and slowly progressive.
- A: A negative amyloid PET makes AD unlikely.
- B: CJD is rapidly progressive.
- C: FTD is behavioral or language-predominant with frontal and temporal atrophy.
- D: DLB has hallucinations, parkinsonism and relatively larger hippocampi.
Source: Ch 0031, Laboratory Tests; Table 31-4

**14. B** — Recent bereavement, insomnia and weight loss suggest depression-related cognitive impairment. Treat the psychiatric disorder vigorously, then confirm whether cognitive complaints persist.
- A: A cholinesterase inhibitor doesn't treat depression.
- C: Antipsychotics aren't indicated.
- D: Benzodiazepines impair cognition.
- E: There's no evidence of AD.
Source: Ch 0031, History; Treatment: Dementia
