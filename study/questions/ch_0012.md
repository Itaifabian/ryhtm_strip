# Ch 12 — Ethical Issues in Clinical Medicine: practice questions

**1.** A 79-year-old man is hospitalized with pneumonia. On day 2 he is intermittently disoriented and inattentive, worse at night. Temperature 38.3°C (100.9°F). The team wants consent for a screening colonoscopy that had been planned before admission; he says "no" one morning and "yes" that afternoon. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Obtain consent from his wife now
B. Proceed with the colonoscopy because he agreed in the afternoon
C. Request a court-appointed guardian
D. Postpone the decision until his delirium resolves
E. Ask psychiatry to declare him incompetent

**2.** A 46-year-old woman with symptomatic gallstones is advised to undergo cholecystectomy. After a detailed discussion, she accurately restates the risks of surgery and of declining it, and explains that she prefers dietary modification because of a previous bad experience with anesthesia. She has no psychiatric history. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Respect her decision and arrange follow-up
B. Request a psychiatric evaluation of decision-making capacity
C. Ask her husband to provide consent
D. Seek a court order for surgery
E. Schedule the surgery and readdress consent on the day of the operation

**3.** A 24-year-old man with severe asthma presents with an exacerbation. He is fatigued, using accessory muscles, and has a rising PaCO2 despite maximal bronchodilator therapy. He refuses intubation, saying, "Once you go on a breathing machine you never come off." He is alert and oriented. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Document the refusal and continue current therapy
B. Intubate him because his refusal is irrational
C. Explore his concerns and explain that ventilation for this condition is expected to be temporary
D. Ask his parents to consent to intubation
E. Transfer him to the floor for comfort care

**4.** A 35-year-old man is brought to the emergency department unconscious after a motorcycle collision. He is hypotensive with a positive FAST exam and needs emergency laparotomy. He carries no identification or advance directive, and no family can be reached. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Wait until family is located
B. Obtain an emergency court order
C. Convene the ethics committee before proceeding
D. Proceed with emergency surgery
E. Provide fluids only until he regains consciousness

**5.** A 74-year-old woman with advanced dementia lacks decision-making capacity. Five years ago she completed an advance directive naming her longtime friend as her health care proxy. Her two adult sons now disagree with the friend about placing a feeding tube. Which of the following individuals should make the decision?

A. The older son
B. Both sons jointly
C. The attending physician
D. The designated friend, using the patient's known values
E. The hospital ethics committee

**6.** A 68-year-old man who is homeless is admitted with a massive stroke and cannot communicate. He has no advance directive, and despite extensive efforts no relatives or friends can be found. A decision is needed about long-term artificial nutrition. Which of the following is the most appropriate approach?

A. The attending physician decides alone based on clinical judgment
B. Follow the institution's formal process for unrepresented patients
C. Withhold all treatment because no consent is available
D. Allow the nurse who knows him best to decide
E. Ask a hospital social worker to sign consent

**7.** A 62-year-old woman's biopsy shows metastatic lung adenocarcinoma. Before you enter her room, her daughter asks you not to tell her mother the diagnosis because "it will crush her." The patient has full decision-making capacity. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Agree to withhold the diagnosis
B. Tell the patient she has a "spot on the lung" that needs follow-up
C. Ask the patient how much she wants to know about her results and who she wants involved
D. Disclose the diagnosis to the daughter only
E. Request an ethics consultation before speaking with the patient

**8.** A hospitalized 55-year-old man receives 10 times the intended dose of insulin because of an order-entry error and becomes hypoglycemic. He recovers fully after treatment. Which of the following is the most appropriate action regarding the patient?

A. Do not mention it because he had no lasting harm
B. Tell him he had a "reaction" to insulin
C. Disclose the error, explain what happened, and apologize
D. Inform only the risk-management department
E. Tell him only if he asks directly

**9.** A second-year resident notices that the attending surgeon smells strongly of alcohol and has slurred speech while scrubbing for an elective case. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Say nothing because the attending is senior
B. Confront the attending privately after the case
C. Report the concern immediately to the clinical supervisor or department director
D. Inform the patient's family
E. Call the state licensing board after the operation

**10.** A healthy 22-year-old college student asks for a prescription for a stimulant to help her concentrate during upcoming board exams. She has no history or symptoms of ADHD. Which of the following is the most appropriate response?

A. Prescribe a short course because the risk is low
B. Prescribe it only if she signs a waiver
C. Explore her reasons, then decline and explain why
D. Refer her to psychiatry for an ADHD evaluation
E. Prescribe a lower dose than usual

**11.** A 30-year-old woman asks her primary care physician for oral contraception. The physician has a sincere religious objection to prescribing contraceptives. Which of the following is the most appropriate action?

A. Refuse and recommend abstinence
B. Prescribe despite the objection
C. Ensure she can obtain the prescription from another clinician without undue delay
D. Tell her contraception is unsafe
E. End the physician–patient relationship

**12.** A 58-year-old man with diabetes and hypertension returns with an HbA1c higher than at his last visit. Pharmacy records show he did not refill the brand-name medication prescribed 3 months ago. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Add a second brand-name agent
B. Ask about cost or other barriers to filling the prescription
C. Document nonadherence and discharge him from the practice
D. Switch to insulin
E. Refer to endocrinology

**13.** A third-year medical student is scheduled to assist in surgery. The resident suggests the student perform a pelvic exam on the patient after induction of anesthesia "for practice." The patient was not told about student involvement. Which of the following is the most appropriate course of action?

A. Perform the exam because the patient is anesthetized
B. Perform the exam only if the attending is present
C. Do not perform the exam unless the patient gave consent beforehand
D. Perform the exam but document it as part of the surgical prep
E. Ask the patient's spouse for consent in the waiting room

---

## Answers

**1. D** — Delirium causes fluctuating capacity. When impairment is fluctuating or reversible, decisions should be postponed when possible and revisited once capacity returns. A screening colonoscopy is not urgent.
- A: A surrogate isn't needed for a non-urgent decision that can wait.
- B: Consent given while delirious is unreliable.
- C: Courts are rarely needed; physicians assess capacity.
- E: Capacity isn't "declared away" for a reversible condition.
Source: Ch 0012, Caring for Patients Who Lack Decision-Making Capacity

**2. A** — A competent, informed patient may refuse a recommended intervention and choose among reasonable alternatives. Disagreement alone doesn't mean she lacks capacity.
- B: Nothing suggests impaired capacity.
- C: A spouse can't override a capable patient.
- D: A court order is inappropriate for a capable patient's refusal.
- E: This disregards her informed refusal.
Source: Ch 0012, Shared Decision-Making and Obtaining Informed Consent

**3. C** — Simply accepting a young asthmatic's refusal of ventilation for reversible failure is called "morally constricted." The physician should elicit his concerns, correct misunderstandings, and try to persuade him. If he is informed and still refuses, his choice prevails.
- A: This skips correcting a clear misconception.
- B: Overriding an alert patient without addressing the misunderstanding violates autonomy.
- D: Parents can't consent for a capable adult.
- E: Comfort care for a reversible condition is not appropriate.
Source: Ch 0012, Influences on Patients' Best Interests

**4. D** — In an emergency, treatment may be given without consent when the patient can't consent and delay would jeopardize life or health. People are presumed to want emergency care unless they previously stated otherwise.
- A, B, C, E: Each delays lifesaving care.
Source: Ch 0012, Shared Decision-Making and Obtaining Informed Consent

**5. D** — A health care proxy designated by the patient should be respected. Family serves as surrogate only when no proxy was named. The proxy decides based on the patient's values and preferences.
- A, B: The sons rank below the designated proxy.
- C: The physician isn't the surrogate.
- E: Ethics committees advise; they don't replace the proxy.
Source: Ch 0012, Caring for Patients Who Lack Decision-Making Capacity

**6. B** — For unrepresented patients with no proxy, relatives or friends, decisions should follow a fair and careful institutional or legal process.
- A: Ad hoc decisions by a single physician are what the process is meant to prevent.
- C: Withholding all care isn't a default.
- D, E: Neither is an authorized surrogate.
Source: Ch 0012, Caring for Patients Who Lack Decision-Making Capacity

**7. C** — Honest disclosure promotes autonomy and trust. Some patients choose not to receive information or prefer a surrogate to decide, so the patient herself is asked.
- A, B: These withhold or blur the diagnosis.
- D: This breaches confidentiality and bypasses a capable patient.
- E: This delays a conversation the physician can handle.
Source: Ch 0012, Goals and Treatment Decisions

**8. C** — Physicians show respect for patients by disclosing and explaining errors and apologizing, offering compensation, and using errors to improve care. A non-punitive systems approach improves safety.
- A, E: Disclosure doesn't depend on harm or on being asked.
- B: This is deceptive.
- D: Internal reporting is appropriate but doesn't replace disclosure to the patient.
Source: Ch 0012, Response to Medical Errors

**9. C** — Colleagues of an impaired physician must protect patients, starting with reporting concerns to the clinical supervisor or director. This patient is at immediate risk.
- A: Silence endangers the patient.
- B: Waiting until after the case leaves the patient exposed.
- D: Informing the family is inappropriate and doesn't stop the case.
- E: The licensing board isn't the first step and comes too late.
Source: Ch 0012, Physician Impairment

**10. C** — When a request would be deceptive, unfair, or against the patient's medical interest (e.g., a cognitive enhancer for an exam), the physician should understand the reasons, then decline and explain.
- A, B, E: Each prescribes without an indication.
- D: She has no symptoms suggesting ADHD.
Source: Ch 0012, Physician as Gatekeeper

**11. C** — Conscientious objection is protected, but patients need timely care. The objector or the institution should arrange for another professional or a referral, and patients should ideally be told about the objection in advance.
- A, D: These impose the physician's values and misinform her.
- B: Physicians shouldn't be forced to violate deep convictions.
- E: Abandoning the patient is inappropriate.
Source: Ch 0012, Claims of Conscience

**12. B** — Patients with high copays may not fill prescriptions. The team should follow up on unfilled prescriptions to explore cost and other barriers, then choose affordable, effective alternatives.
- A, D: Escalating therapy ignores why the current regimen failed.
- C: Discharging him is punitive and abandons care.
- E: Referral doesn't address the barrier.
Source: Ch 0012, Influences on Patients' Best Interests

**13. C** — Patients' consent for trainee participation is required, especially for intimate exams (pelvic, rectal, breast, testicular). Anesthesia doesn't waive this.
- A, B, D: Each performs an exam without consent; D adds deception.
- E: The patient was capable before surgery, so a spouse can't consent afterward.
Source: Ch 0012, Learning Clinical Skills
