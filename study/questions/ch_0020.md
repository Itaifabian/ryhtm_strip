# Ch 20 — Fever: practice questions

**1.** An 82-year-old woman is brought to the emergency department during a heat wave after being found confused in her apartment, which has no air conditioning. She takes an anticholinergic medication for overactive bladder. Temperature is 41.2°C (106.2°F), pulse is 124/min, and blood pressure is 102/60 mm Hg. Her skin is hot and dry. A dose of acetaminophen given by paramedics did not lower her temperature. Which of the following best explains her elevated temperature?

A. Elevation of the hypothalamic set point by pyrogenic cytokines
B. Heat gain exceeding heat loss with an unchanged hypothalamic set point
C. Prostaglandin E2 acting on the EP-3 receptor
D. Endotoxin-induced release of interleukin-1
E. Hypothalamic damage lowering the set point

**2.** A 30-year-old man asks whether his oral temperature of 37.4°C (99.3°F), measured at 4 p.m., means he has a fever. Based on a large study of healthy adults, which of the following oral temperatures defines fever?

A. >37.0°C (>98.6°F)
B. >37.2°C (>99.0°F)
C. >37.5°C (>99.5°F)
D. >37.7°C (>99.9°F)
E. >38.3°C (>101.0°F)

**3.** A 6-year-old boy has had 2 days of fever, runny nose, and cough. Temperature is 38.9°C (102°F). He is uncomfortable but well hydrated, and examination shows only rhinorrhea and mild pharyngeal erythema. His mother asks what to give him for the fever. Which of the following is the most appropriate recommendation?

A. Acetaminophen or ibuprofen
B. Aspirin
C. No antipyretic, because fever helps clear viral infections
D. Oral prednisone
E. Alternating aspirin and acetaminophen

**4.** A 68-year-old man with a history of a bleeding duodenal ulcer and chronic kidney disease has influenza with a temperature of 39.0°C (102.2°F) and diffuse myalgias. He asks for something to bring down the fever. Which of the following is the most appropriate antipyretic?

A. High-dose aspirin
B. Ibuprofen
C. Naproxen
D. Celecoxib
E. Acetaminophen

**5.** A 45-year-old woman with rheumatoid arthritis has taken adalimumab for 2 years. She reports 5 days of fatigue and a temperature of 37.9°C (100.2°F). She has no localizing symptoms. Examination is unremarkable. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Reassure her that a temperature below 38.3°C is not significant
B. Recheck her temperature in 2 weeks
C. Perform an early, thorough diagnostic evaluation for infection, including tuberculosis
D. Increase the adalimumab dose
E. Start empiric oral prednisone

**6.** A 27-year-old man returns from a 3-week trip to South Asia with 6 days of fever, headache, and abdominal pain. Temperature is 39.8°C (103.6°F) and pulse is 72/min. He has faint, blanching pink macules on his trunk and mild splenomegaly. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Typhoid fever
B. Falciparum malaria
C. Dengue fever
D. Acute HIV infection
E. Infectious mononucleosis

**7.** A 24-year-old woman has had recurring fevers for 3 months. Each febrile period lasts about a week and is followed by about a week without fever. She has night sweats, weight loss, and painless cervical lymphadenopathy. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Plasmodium malariae infection
B. Cyclic neutropenia
C. Familial Mediterranean fever
D. Hodgkin lymphoma
E. Tick-borne relapsing fever

**8.** A 58-year-old man is treated with IV antibiotics for suspected vertebral osteomyelitis. Blood cultures and a biopsy culture are negative. He has been receiving scheduled acetaminophen every 6 hours since admission, and the team wants to know whether the antibiotic regimen is working. Which of the following is the most appropriate step?

A. Increase the acetaminophen dose
B. Add scheduled ibuprofen
C. Start dexamethasone
D. Stop the antibiotics and observe
E. Withhold antipyretics to follow the fever curve

**9.** A 2-year-old girl with a history of febrile seizures has acute otitis media and a temperature of 39.4°C (102.9°F). Which of the following is the most appropriate approach to her fever?

A. Withhold antipyretics so that the fever curve can be followed
B. Treat the fever aggressively with an appropriate antipyretic
C. Give an antipyretic only if her temperature exceeds 40.5°C
D. Give aspirin
E. Use a cooling blanket without an antipyretic

**10.** A 61-year-old man is admitted to the ICU with an intracerebral hemorrhage. On day 2, his temperature rises to 41.7°C (107.1°F). The nurse proposes placing a cooling blanket. Which of the following is the most appropriate management of his temperature?

A. Cooling blanket alone
B. No treatment, because fever is a beneficial host response
C. Antipyretic plus a cooling blanket
D. Ice-water immersion without medication
E. High-dose glucocorticoids alone

**11.** A 74-year-old woman with heart failure (ejection fraction 25%) is admitted with pneumonia. Temperature is 39.5°C (103.1°F), pulse is 118/min, and respiratory rate is 26/min. Antibiotics have been started. A student suggests withholding antipyretics because fever may help fight infection. Which of the following is the best response?

A. Withhold antipyretics; fever accelerates recovery from bacterial infection
B. Withhold antipyretics; they delay resolution of pneumonia
C. Give antipyretics only if her temperature exceeds 41°C
D. Use external cooling only
E. Treat the fever, because each 1°C rise increases oxygen consumption by about 13%

## Answers

**1. B** — Hyperthermia is an uncontrolled rise in temperature that exceeds the body's ability to lose heat. The hypothalamic set point is unchanged and no pyrogens are involved. Heat exposure, a drug that interferes with thermoregulation, hot dry skin and no response to antipyretics all distinguish it from fever.
- A, C, D: These describe fever, which raises the set point and usually responds to antipyretics.
- E: Hypothalamic damage usually causes subnormal temperatures.
Source: Ch 0020, Fever Versus Hyperthermia

**2. D** — In a study of more than 35,000 adults, the mean oral temperature was 36.6°C. A temperature above 37.7°C (99.9°F), the 99th percentile for healthy people, defines fever.
- A, B, C: These fall within normal daily variation (higher readings occur around 4 p.m.).
- E: 38.3°C is the temperature threshold used in the FUO definition, not the definition of fever.
Source: Ch 0020, Normal Body Temperatures

**3. A** — Treating fever with routine antipyretics does no harm and doesn't slow recovery from common infections. In children, use acetaminophen or ibuprofen; aspirin increases the risk of Reye syndrome with certain viral infections.
- B, E: Aspirin is avoided in children.
- C: No evidence shows that fever speeds recovery or that antipyretics delay it.
- D: Glucocorticoids aren't used to treat simple fever.
Source: Ch 0020, Regimens for the Treatment of Fever; The Decision to Treat Fever

**4. E** — Aspirin and NSAIDs reduce fever effectively but can harm platelets and the GI tract, so acetaminophen is the preferred antipyretic. That matters especially with prior GI bleeding.
- A, B, C, D: These carry GI and platelet effects (and COX-2 inhibitors are still NSAIDs) that are undesirable here.
Source: Ch 0020, Regimens for the Treatment of Fever

**5. C** — Anticytokine therapy lowers host defenses (e.g., latent TB can disseminate on anti-TNF therapy) and may blunt the febrile response. Low-grade fever in these patients is of considerable concern and calls for an early, rigorous evaluation.
- A, B: Blunted fever makes a low reading more, not less, concerning.
- D: More immunosuppression is the wrong direction.
- E: Steroids would further blunt fever and mask infection.
Source: Ch 0020, Fever in Patients Receiving Anticytokine Therapy

**6. A** — Temperature–pulse dissociation (relative bradycardia) occurs in typhoid fever, brucellosis, leptospirosis, some drug fevers and factitious fever. Travel, abdominal pain, rose spots and splenomegaly fit typhoid.
- B, C, D, E: These cause fever with a proportionate tachycardia and don't feature the classic temperature–pulse dissociation.
Source: Ch 0020, The Decision to Treat Fever (fever patterns); Ch 0021 Table 21-1 (rose spots)

**7. D** — In the Pel-Ebstein pattern, 3–10 days of fever alternate with 3–10 days without fever, classically in Hodgkin disease and other lymphomas. Night sweats, weight loss and lymphadenopathy support this.
- A: *P. malariae* causes fever every fourth day.
- B: Cyclic neutropenia causes fever every 21 days, with neutropenia.
- C: FMF attacks are brief with serositis.
- E: Relapsing fever follows tick or louse exposure and has a different rhythm.
Source: Ch 0020, The Decision to Treat Fever (fever patterns)

**8. E** — In bacterial infections, withholding antipyretics can help judge whether an antibiotic is working, especially without positive cultures. Routine antipyretics can mask undertreated infection.
- A, B: More antipyretics obscure the response further.
- C: Glucocorticoids suppress fever and inflammation and mask the course.
- D: Stopping treatment for suspected osteomyelitis is premature.
Source: Ch 0020, The Decision to Treat Fever

**9. B** — Children with a history of febrile or nonfebrile seizures should be aggressively treated to reduce fever. The absolute temperature doesn't correlate with seizure onset, so waiting for a threshold isn't justified.
- A: Withholding antipyretics is wrong in a seizure-prone child.
- C: A temperature threshold doesn't predict seizures.
- D: Aspirin is avoided in children (Reye syndrome).
- E: Cooling blankets shouldn't be used without antipyretics.
Source: Ch 0020, Regimens for the Treatment of Fever

**10. C** — In hyperpyrexia, cooling blankets help but should not be used without oral antipyretics. In CNS hemorrhage or trauma, lowering core temperature mitigates the harmful effects of heat on the brain.
- A, D: Cooling without an antipyretic is specifically discouraged.
- B: Hyperpyrexia after CNS hemorrhage harms the brain.
- E: Glucocorticoids are antipyretic, but the recommended approach is an antipyretic plus cooling.
Source: Ch 0020, Regimens for the Treatment of Fever

**11. E** — Fever increases oxygen demand by about 13% for every 1°C above 37°C. It can worsen preexisting cardiac, pulmonary or CNS impairment, so treatment is highly recommended in such patients.
- A, B: No evidence shows fever speeds recovery or antipyretics delay it.
- C: Waiting for 41°C ignores her cardiac strain.
- D: Antipyretics lower the set point; external cooling alone fights it.
Source: Ch 0020, Regimens for the Treatment of Fever; The Decision to Treat Fever
