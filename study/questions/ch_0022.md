# Ch 22 — Fever of Unknown Origin: practice questions

**1.** Which of the following patients meets the current definition of fever of unknown origin (FUO)?

A. A 34-year-old man with HIV (CD4 count 80/μL) with 4 weeks of fever to 38.6°C and a negative initial workup
B. A 52-year-old immunocompetent woman with 4 weeks of fevers to 38.5°C on several occasions whose history, examination, and the full set of obligatory investigations are unrevealing
C. A 40-year-old man with 10 days of fever to 39°C and a negative initial workup
D. A 60-year-old woman with 5 weeks of temperatures to 37.9°C and normal inflammatory markers
E. A 28-year-old woman with 4 weeks of fever whose chest x-ray shows a lobar consolidation

**2.** A 66-year-old man has had 4 weeks of fevers to 38.6°C (101.5°F). He takes allopurinol, furosemide, lisinopril, and an herbal supplement. History, examination, and the obligatory laboratory and imaging studies have not revealed a cause. There are no localizing clues. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Start empiric broad-spectrum antibiotics
B. Order a panel of serologies for uncommon infections
C. Perform a bone marrow biopsy
D. Discontinue all medications, including the supplement, and observe
E. Start a trial of prednisone

**3.** A 49-year-old woman is referred for 5 weeks of unexplained fever and an elevated CRP. Her primary care physician started prednisone 30 mg daily 4 days ago. She is hemodynamically stable. An 18F-FDG-PET/CT is planned. Which of the following is the most appropriate step before the scan?

A. Stop or postpone glucocorticoid treatment until after the PET/CT
B. Double the prednisone dose to control symptoms
C. Add empiric antibiotics
D. Perform the PET/CT while she continues prednisone
E. Replace the PET/CT with a bone marrow biopsy

**4.** A 57-year-old man has had 6 weeks of fever to 38.7°C (101.7°F), sweats, and a CRP of 96 mg/L. He is immunocompetent. History and examination have been repeated, the obligatory tests are unrevealing, drugs have been stopped, cryoglobulins are negative, and fundoscopy is normal. Targeted tests for the few clues found were negative. He is stable. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Empiric antituberculous therapy
B. Repeat blood cultures daily for 1 week
C. Empiric prednisone trial
D. Liver biopsy
E. 18F-FDG-PET/CT

**5.** A 45-year-old woman meets criteria for FUO. Her history, examination, and obligatory tests show no potentially diagnostic clues. Which of the following additional evaluations is recommended early in her workup?

A. Transthoracic echocardiography
B. Extended autoimmune antibody panel
C. Fundoscopy by an ophthalmologist
D. Colonoscopy
E. Bronchoscopy with lavage

**6.** A 71-year-old woman has 7 weeks of low-grade fever, fatigue, and an ESR of 92 mm/h. 18F-FDG-PET/CT shows no increased uptake in the aorta or its major branches. She now reports new scalp tenderness. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Repeat the PET/CT in 2 weeks
B. Temporal artery biopsy
C. Reassure her that a normal PET/CT excludes giant cell arteritis
D. Lumbar puncture
E. Empiric antibiotics

**7.** A 39-year-old man has had 3 months of intermittent fevers. Repeated history and examination, the obligatory tests, drug withdrawal, targeted testing of all clues, and an 18F-FDG-PET/CT obtained while his CRP was elevated have not revealed a cause. He remains clinically stable and is working full time. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Empiric prednisone trial
B. Repeat the PET/CT now
C. Empiric 6-week course of antituberculous therapy
D. Follow-up with periodic reassessment for new clues
E. Exploratory laparotomy

**8.** A 63-year-old woman under evaluation for FUO becomes hypotensive (blood pressure 82/48 mm Hg) with a temperature of 39.6°C (103.3°F). Her absolute neutrophil count is 300/μL. The source of fever is still unknown. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Start empiric broad-spectrum antibiotics after drawing cultures
B. Withhold antibiotics to preserve culture yield
C. Begin an anakinra trial
D. Start high-dose glucocorticoids
E. Order 18F-FDG-PET/CT before any treatment

**9.** A 50-year-old man born in a TB-endemic country has 8 weeks of fever, weight loss, and night sweats. His interferon-γ release assay is indeterminate, and the chest x-ray shows innumerable tiny nodules. A bone marrow aspirate shows noncaseating granulomas. Miliary tuberculosis is suspected. Which of the following procedures has the highest diagnostic yield?

A. Sputum acid-fast smear only
B. Repeat interferon-γ release assay
C. Tuberculin skin test
D. Serum angiotensin-converting enzyme level
E. Liver biopsy for acid-fast smear, culture, and PCR

**10.** A 35-year-old woman has had recurrent episodes of fever lasting about 5 days, separated by 3–4 weeks of feeling well, for 8 months. She is currently afebrile, and her examination and laboratory results today are normal. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Order an extensive serology panel today
B. Perform a PET/CT today
C. Ask her to return during her next febrile episode for repeat evaluation and testing
D. Start empiric antibiotics
E. Start empiric prednisone

**11.** A 31-year-old ICU nurse has recorded oral temperatures of 39–40°C at home for 5 weeks. In clinic, she looks well. Pulse is 72/min during a reported temperature of 39.8°C. CBC, CRP, and ESR are normal. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. 18F-FDG-PET/CT
B. Simultaneous temperature measurements at different body sites (oral, rectal, tympanic)
C. Blood cultures ×6
D. Empiric antibiotics
E. Bone marrow biopsy

**12.** A 64-year-old farmer has had 6 weeks of fever and fatigue. He has a prosthetic aortic valve, and blood cultures are negative. Which of the following is the most appropriate next diagnostic test?

A. Antinuclear antibody titer
B. Stool ova and parasites
C. Thick and thin blood smears
D. Bone marrow biopsy
E. Q fever serology by immunofluorescence assay

**13.** A 55-year-old man has had 10 weeks of fever, migratory arthralgias, chronic diarrhea with weight loss, and new memory problems. Blood cultures, the obligatory tests, and colonoscopy with routine biopsies are unrevealing. Which of the following is the most appropriate next diagnostic test?

A. PCR for Tropheryma whipplei on stool and blood
B. Serum ACE level
C. Repeat colonoscopy
D. Antinuclear antibody panel
E. HLA-B27 testing

**14.** A 68-year-old man has had 1 year of recurrent fevers, painful ear and nose cartilage inflammation, skin lesions, an unprovoked deep venous thrombosis, and macrocytic anemia. Bone marrow biopsy shows vacuoles in myeloid and erythroid precursors and myelodysplastic changes. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Schnitzler syndrome
B. Adult-onset Still disease
C. Familial Mediterranean fever
D. VEXAS syndrome
E. Hodgkin lymphoma

**15.** A 27-year-old man originally from a region where familial Mediterranean fever is common has had recurrent 2-day episodes of fever with abdominal and chest pain from serositis for 2 years. Between attacks he is well. Infection and malignancy have been excluded. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment to prevent further attacks?

A. Long-term prednisone
B. Empiric antibiotics with each attack
C. Daily colchicine
D. Antituberculous therapy
E. Hydroxychloroquine

## Answers

**1. B** — FUO now requires fever of at least 38.3°C on at least two occasions, at least 3 weeks of illness, no known immunocompromised state, and an unclear diagnosis after a thorough history, examination and the obligatory investigations.
- A: Immunocompromised patients are excluded; they need a more aggressive approach.
- C: Ten days is under the 3-week requirement.
- D: 37.9°C doesn't reach the 38.3°C threshold, and normal markers rule out IUO too.
- E: The chest x-ray has already identified a likely cause.
Source: Ch 0022, Definition

**2. D** — All medications, including nonprescription drugs and supplements, should be stopped early to exclude drug fever. Allopurinol and furosemide are among the more common causes. If fever persists more than 72 hours after stopping a drug, that drug is unlikely to be the cause.
- A: Empiric antibiotics aren't indicated in a stable patient and impair later cultures.
- B: Serologies without clues give more false than true positives.
- C: Marrow biopsy has very low yield without clues.
- E: Steroids mask disease and are avoided early.
Source: Ch 0022, Approach to the Patient; Differential Diagnosis

**3. A** — Pathologic FDG uptake is quickly eradicated by glucocorticoids in many diseases, including vasculitis and lymphoma. Steroids should be stopped or postponed until after the PET/CT.
- B, D: Continuing or increasing steroids reduces the scan's yield and masks disease.
- C: Empiric antibiotics in a stable patient impair cultures.
- E: Marrow biopsy without clues has low yield.
Source: Ch 0022, 18F-Fluordeoxyglucose Positron Emission Tomography

**4. E** — When clue-guided tests don't yield a diagnosis, the next step is 18F-FDG-PET/CT, preferably while the ESR or CRP is elevated. Its total diagnostic yield is about 50%.
- A: Anti-TB therapy is reserved for specific indications, after cultures.
- B: More than three blood cultures without clues is useless.
- C: Empiric steroids risk masking lymphoma or infection.
- D: Liver biopsy without clues has very low yield.
Source: Ch 0022, Approach to the Patient; Figure 22-1

**5. C** — In patients without clues, or with only misleading ones, fundoscopy by an ophthalmologist may be useful early to exclude retinal vasculitis. Cryoglobulins are also a valuable screening test.
- A, D, E: Echocardiography, GI evaluation and bronchoscopy have very low yield without clues.
- B: Extra immunologic serologies give low yield and frequent false positives.
Source: Ch 0022, Approach to the Patient

**6. B** — Temporal artery biopsy is still recommended for patients 55 and older with suspected GCA. PET/CT may miss vasculitis limited to the temporal arteries because the vessels are small and brain FDG uptake is high.
- A: Repeating a well-timed negative PET adds little.
- C: A normal PET doesn't exclude temporal-limited GCA.
- D: LP has no role.
- E: Nothing suggests bacterial infection.
Source: Ch 0022, Approach to the Patient (giant cell arteritis)

**7. D** — When all clues have been evaluated without an explanation, waiting for new clues is better than ordering more screening tests. Further workup is warranted only if the patient deteriorates. Most patients with unexplained FUO do well and many recover spontaneously.
- A: Empiric steroids can mask lymphoma or infection.
- B: Repeating a well-timed negative PET adds little unless new signs appear.
- C: Empiric anti-TB therapy needs specific indications.
- E: Invasive testing should be clue-driven.
Source: Ch 0022, Later-Stage Diagnostic Tests; Treatment

**8. A** — Antibiotics can impair culture yield, but hemodynamic instability or neutropenia is a good indication for empiric antibiotic therapy.
- B: Preserving culture yield doesn't outweigh shock with neutropenia.
- C: Anakinra is for clinically IL-1-driven, undiagnosed FUO, not septic shock.
- D: Glucocorticoids can permit the spread of infection.
- E: Treatment shouldn't wait for imaging in an unstable patient.
Source: Ch 0022, Antibiotics and Antituberculous Therapy

**9. E** — If miliary tuberculosis is suspected, liver biopsy for acid-fast smear, culture and PCR probably has the highest diagnostic yield. A negative or indeterminate TST or IGRA doesn't exclude TB, and granulomas should prompt reconsideration of TB.
- A: Sputum smear is often negative in miliary disease.
- B, C: TST and IGRA can be indeterminate or falsely negative in miliary TB.
- D: An ACE level doesn't distinguish TB from sarcoidosis.
Source: Ch 0022, Approach to the Patient (tuberculosis)

**10. C** — With recurrent fever, patients should return during a febrile episode so the history, examination and laboratory tests can be repeated while symptomatic. Abnormalities may be absent between episodes.
- A, B: Testing between episodes may be falsely normal; PET should be timed to active inflammation.
- D, E: Empiric therapy isn't indicated in a well patient.
Source: Ch 0022, Approach to the Patient; 18F-FDG-PET (timing)

**11. B** — In fraudulent fever, the patient is normothermic but manipulates the thermometer. Simultaneous measurements at different body sites quickly identify it. Clues include pulse–temperature dissociation, normal inflammatory markers and access to medical equipment.
- A, C, E: Extensive testing comes after manipulation is excluded.
- D: Antibiotics aren't indicated.
Source: Ch 0022, Differential Diagnosis; Approach to the Patient

**12. E** — Q fever serology by immunofluorescence assay should be done when the patient lives in a rural area or has heart valve disease, an aortic aneurysm or a vascular prosthesis. *Coxiella* is a cause of culture-negative endocarditis.
- A: ANA is already part of the obligatory tests.
- B: Nothing suggests a parasite.
- C: There's no travel to a malaria-endemic area.
- D: Marrow biopsy without clues has low yield.
Source: Ch 0022, Differential Diagnosis

**13. A** — In unexplained symptoms localized to the CNS, GI tract or joints, PCR for *T. whipplei* on stool and blood should be performed early. Whipple disease is rare but has nonspecific presenting symptoms.
- B: ACE testing addresses sarcoidosis.
- C: Repeating a routine colonoscopy doesn't target Whipple.
- D: ANA is already part of the obligatory tests.
- E: HLA-B27 doesn't explain the CNS and GI picture.
Source: Ch 0022, Differential Diagnosis

**14. D** — VEXAS is an adult-onset autoinflammatory syndrome, mostly in middle-aged to elderly men. It features recurrent inflammation, skin lesions, chondritis, lung disease, venous thrombosis, arthritis and MDS. Vacuoles in myeloid precursors are very suspicious. It's caused by somatic *UBA1* variants.
- A: Schnitzler features urticaria, bone pain and a monoclonal gammopathy.
- B: Still disease causes a salmon rash and arthritis without marrow vacuoles.
- C: FMF begins young with brief serositis attacks.
- E: Lymphoma doesn't cause chondritis or marrow vacuolization.
Source: Ch 0022, Differential Diagnosis

**15. C** — Colchicine is highly effective in preventing FMF attacks, though not always effective once an attack is underway. Most patients improve within weeks to months of continuous treatment, so colchicine may be tried when the features fit FMF, especially in patients from high-prevalence regions.
- A: Long-term steroids cause metabolic, immunologic and GI harm; IL-1 inhibition is preferred over steroids for autoinflammation.
- B: This isn't an infection.
- D: There's no TB indication.
- E: Hydroxychloroquine isn't the described FMF therapy.
Source: Ch 0022, Colchicine, Nonsteroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs, and Glucocorticoids
