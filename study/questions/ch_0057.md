# Ch 57 — Hypercalcemia and Hypocalcemia: practice questions

**1.** A 61-year-old woman has a total serum calcium of 10.9 mg/dL on routine labs, with an albumin of 3.0 g/dL. She has mild fatigue and constipation. Which of the following is the most appropriate next test?

A. PTHrP level
B. 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D level
C. Bone scan
D. Intact PTH level
E. Serum protein electrophoresis

**2.** A 38-year-old man has a calcium of 10.8 mg/dL, a PTH of 58 pg/mL (upper normal) and normal phosphate. His mother and sister have mild hypercalcemia, and his uncle had an unsuccessful neck exploration. The calcium/creatinine clearance ratio is 0.004. Which of the following is the most appropriate management?

A. Avoid parathyroidectomy; confirm with CASR genetic testing
B. Four-gland parathyroid exploration
C. Start cinacalcet immediately
D. IV zoledronic acid
E. Sestamibi scan and minimally invasive parathyroidectomy

**3.** A 68-year-old woman with metastatic squamous cell lung cancer is confused, with a calcium of 14.6 mg/dL. She has dry mucous membranes and orthostatic hypotension. Creatinine is 1.6 mg/dL (baseline 0.9). Which of the following is the most appropriate first step?

A. IV furosemide
B. Oral prednisone
C. Cinacalcet
D. Hemodialysis
E. Aggressive IV normal saline

**4.** A 72-year-old man with multiple myeloma has a calcium of 13.8 mg/dL despite IV fluids. His estimated GFR is 24 mL/min/1.73 m². Which agent is most appropriate to inhibit bone resorption?

A. IV zoledronic acid 4 mg
B. Denosumab (reduced dose)
C. IV pamidronate 90 mg
D. Cinacalcet
E. Thiazide diuretic

**5.** A 35-year-old woman has bilateral hilar adenopathy, erythema nodosum and a calcium of 12.4 mg/dL. PTH is suppressed, and 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D is elevated. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

A. Parathyroidectomy
B. Cinacalcet
C. Glucocorticoids
D. Thiazide diuretic
E. Calcium supplementation

**6.** A 70-year-old man has a calcium of 12.1 mg/dL, suppressed PTH, normocytic anemia and a creatinine of 2.2 mg/dL. Serum protein electrophoresis is unremarkable. Which additional test is most important?

A. Sestamibi scan
B. Calcium/creatinine clearance ratio
C. Thyroid ultrasound
D. Serum free light chains with immunofixation
E. 24-hour urine calcium

**7.** A 66-year-old woman with hypercalcemia of malignancy (calcium 13.5 mg/dL) is clinically volume depleted. A resident proposes starting furosemide now to increase calciuresis. Which of the following is correct?

A. Loop diuretics should be started only after volume status has been restored with saline
B. Furosemide should be started before saline to prevent fluid overload
C. Thiazides are preferred over loops for calciuresis
D. Diuretics are contraindicated in hypercalcemia at any stage
E. Furosemide is the main long-term therapy for hypercalcemia of malignancy

**8.** A 52-year-old woman with tingling around her mouth after thyroid surgery has a blood pressure cuff inflated to 20 mm Hg above systolic for 3 minutes, producing wrist flexion and thumb adduction. What is this finding, and why is it useful?

A. Chvostek sign; very specific for hypocalcemia
B. Babinski sign; indicates a CNS lesion
C. Phalen sign; indicates median neuropathy
D. Tinel sign; indicates nerve entrapment
E. Trousseau sign; a more definitive sign of neuromuscular irritability than Chvostek (which occurs in ~10% of normal people)

**9.** A 47-year-old woman develops carpopedal spasm and perioral numbness 24 hours after total thyroidectomy. Calcium is 6.6 mg/dL and the QTc is prolonged. Which of the following is the most appropriate immediate treatment?

A. Oral calcium carbonate 1 g
B. IV calcium gluconate (10 mL of 10% diluted in 50 mL) over 5–10 minutes with telemetry
C. Oral ergocalciferol 50,000 IU
D. Teriparatide
E. IV calcium chloride push over 30 seconds without monitoring

**10.** A 56-year-old man with chronic alcohol use has hypocalcemia (7.1 mg/dL) that persists despite 2 days of IV calcium. PTH is inappropriately low-normal. Which of the following is most likely to correct his hypocalcemia?

A. Calcitriol alone
B. Thiazide diuretic
C. Magnesium repletion
D. Parathyroidectomy
E. Bisphosphonate

**11.** A 44-year-old woman has permanent postsurgical hypoparathyroidism and takes calcium carbonate and calcitriol. Her 24-hour urine calcium is 380 mg with a serum calcium of 9.9 mg/dL. Which of the following is the best adjustment?

A. Increase calcitriol to raise serum calcium to the upper-normal range
B. Add a loop diuretic
C. Stop all calcium
D. Lower therapy to target a low-normal serum calcium to reduce hypercalciuria
E. Start a bisphosphonate

**12.** A 63-year-old man with severe hypercalcemia has an ECG. Which finding is most characteristic?

A. Peaked T waves
B. Shortened QTc interval
C. Prolonged QTc interval
D. Delta waves
E. Low-voltage QRS with electrical alternans

## Answers

**1. D** — Corrected calcium = 10.9 + 0.8 × (4.0 − 3.0) = 11.7 mg/dL, so true hypercalcemia is present. After confirming it, intact PTH is the most important test.
- A: PTHrP is checked when PTH is suppressed.
- B: 1,25(OH)₂D is checked when PTH is suppressed.
- C: A bone scan is premature.
- E: Myeloma workup follows a suppressed PTH.
Source: Ch 0057, Diagnostic Approach

**2. A** — FHH causes mild hypercalcemia with high or inappropriately normal PTH and a Ca/Cr clearance ratio <0.01. A family history of mild hypercalcemia or failed surgery fits. Surgery is ineffective; sequence CASR, GNA11 and AP2S1.
- B: Surgery doesn't help FHH.
- C: Treatment isn't indicated for benign FHH.
- D: A bisphosphonate isn't indicated.
- E: Surgery doesn't help FHH.
Source: Ch 0057, Diagnostic Approach

**3. E** — Significant symptomatic hypercalcemia always causes dehydration. Start with volume expansion (4–6 L of saline in 24 hours may be needed), which improves calciuresis.
- A: Loop diuretics only after euvolemia.
- B: Steroids are for vitamin D–mediated hypercalcemia.
- C: Cinacalcet is for parathyroid carcinoma or pHPT.
- D: Dialysis is for anuric AKI or HF.
Source: Ch 0057, Treatment: Hypercalcemia

**4. B** — IV aminobisphosphonates are contraindicated with GFR <35. Denosumab is cleared by the reticuloendothelial system and can be used at lower doses in advanced kidney disease.
- A: Zoledronic acid is contraindicated at this GFR.
- C: Pamidronate is contraindicated at this GFR.
- D: Cinacalcet targets PTH.
- E: Thiazides raise calcium.
Source: Ch 0057, Treatment: Hypercalcemia

**5. C** — In granulomatous (1,25(OH)₂D-mediated) hypercalcemia, glucocorticoids reduce intestinal calcium absorption and 1,25(OH)₂D production.
- A: PTH is suppressed, so surgery doesn't apply.
- B: Cinacalcet doesn't address 1,25(OH)₂D-mediated disease.
- D: Thiazides raise calcium.
- E: Calcium supplementation worsens it.
Source: Ch 0057, Treatment: Hypercalcemia

**6. D** — With suppressed PTH, myeloma must be evaluated, including serum free light chains, because ~3% of myeloma is missed by serum or urine electrophoresis with immunofixation.
- A: Sestamibi is for PTH-dependent disease.
- B: The Ca/Cr clearance ratio is for PTH-dependent disease.
- C: Thyroid ultrasound doesn't evaluate myeloma.
- E: Urine calcium doesn't identify the cause here.
Source: Ch 0057, Diagnostic Approach

**7. A** — Loop diuretics can transiently increase calcium excretion but shouldn't be started until volume status is restored.
- B: Furosemide before saline worsens volume depletion.
- C: Thiazides cause hypercalcemia.
- D: Loops have a transient role after euvolemia.
- E: Bisphosphonates are the mainstay after hydration.
Source: Ch 0057, Treatment: Hypercalcemia

**8. E** — Trousseau sign is carpal spasm after 3 minutes of cuff inflation above systolic pressure. It's a more definitive sign of impending tetany. Chvostek sign occurs in ~10% of normal people.
- A: Chvostek is facial twitching and isn't specific.
- B: Babinski is unrelated.
- C: Phalen is unrelated.
- D: Tinel is unrelated.
Source: Ch 0057, Clinical Manifestations (hypocalcemia)

**9. B** — Acute symptomatic hypocalcemia is treated with 10 mL of 10% calcium gluconate diluted in 50 mL of D5W or saline, given IV over 5–10 minutes with telemetry. Repeat as needed, then infuse.
- A: Oral calcium is too slow with tetany and QT prolongation.
- C: Vitamin D is for deficiency, not acute tetany.
- D: Teriparatide is for refractory chronic hypoparathyroidism.
- E: A rapid unmonitored push is unsafe.
Source: Ch 0057, Treatment: Hypocalcemia

**10. C** — Hypomagnesemia (alcohol, PPIs, cisplatin) impairs PTH secretion. Hypocalcemia won't correct until magnesium is repleted.
- A: Calcitriol doesn't fix the underlying defect.
- B: A thiazide doesn't fix it.
- D: Surgery has no role.
- E: A bisphosphonate lowers calcium.
Source: Ch 0057, Hypocalcemia Etiology; Treatment

**11. D** — Without PTH, distal tubular calcium reabsorption is impaired. Raising serum calcium above low-normal increases hypercalciuria and stone and nephrocalcinosis risk. The 24-hour urine target is <250 mg (women).
- A: Raising calcium worsens hypercalciuria.
- B: Loops increase calciuria.
- C: Stopping calcium risks tetany.
- E: A bisphosphonate isn't indicated.
Source: Ch 0057, Treatment: Hypocalcemia

**12. B** — Hypercalcemia can cause bradycardia, AV block, QRS widening and a short QTc.
- A: Peaked T waves suggest hyperkalemia.
- C: QTc prolongation occurs in hypocalcemia.
- D: Delta waves indicate preexcitation.
- E: Alternans suggests effusion.
Source: Ch 0057, Clinical Manifestations (hypercalcemia)
