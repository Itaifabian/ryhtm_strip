# Ch 41 — Hemoptysis: practice questions

**1.** A 26-year-old nonsmoker has 4 days of cough with blood-streaked sputum after an upper respiratory infection. He is afebrile with normal vital signs and oxygen saturation. Examination and chest radiograph are normal. Which of the following is the most appropriate management?

A. Chest CT angiography
B. Bronchoscopy
C. Treat for bronchitis and arrange close follow-up
D. Bronchial artery embolization
E. ANCA and anti-GBM testing

**2.** A 58-year-old man with a 40-pack-year smoking history has had several episodes of coughing up small amounts of blood over 2 weeks. Vital signs are normal. Chest radiograph is normal. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

A. Chest CT
B. Reassurance and a course of antibiotics only
C. Repeat chest radiograph in 6 months
D. Sputum culture only
E. Surgical resection

**3.** A 48-year-old woman with cystic fibrosis begins coughing up large amounts of bright red blood, about 300 mL in the past hour. She is tachycardic and her saturation is falling. Crackles and recent imaging indicate the bleeding is from the left lung. While preparing for definitive therapy, which of the following is the most appropriate immediate positioning?

A. Supine and flat
B. Right lateral decubitus (right side down)
C. Prone
D. Trendelenburg
E. Left lateral decubitus (left side down)

**4.** A 62-year-old man with life-threatening hemoptysis can't protect his airway and needs intubation. Which of the following airway strategies is preferred?

A. Small (6.0) endotracheal tube to minimize airway trauma
B. Large (8.5) single-lumen endotracheal tube, which can be advanced into the mainstem of the nonbleeding lung
C. Laryngeal mask airway
D. Noninvasive positive pressure ventilation
E. Tracheostomy as the first airway

**5.** A 55-year-old woman with bronchiectasis has ongoing life-threatening hemoptysis. She has been intubated, and bronchoscopy localized bleeding to the right lower lobe. Her coagulation studies are normal. Which of the following is the procedure of choice to control the bleeding?

A. Emergent right lower lobectomy
B. Intravenous vitamin K
C. Observation with cough suppressants
D. Bronchial artery embolization
E. Systemic thrombolysis

**6.** A 34-year-old man has 1 week of hemoptysis, fatigue and dark urine. Chest radiograph shows bilateral opacities. Which of the following initial studies is most important to add?

A. Sputum cytology
B. Esophagogastroduodenoscopy
C. Echocardiogram
D. Bronchial artery angiography
E. Urinalysis, creatinine, ANCA and anti-GBM antibodies

**7.** A 74-year-old woman taking warfarin for atrial fibrillation has 2 days of moderate hemoptysis. INR is 6.2. She is hemodynamically stable. In addition to protecting the airway and localizing the bleeding, which of the following should be done first?

A. Surgical consultation for lobectomy
B. Bronchial artery embolization
C. Correct the coagulopathy
D. Add aspirin
E. Rigid bronchoscopy with argon plasma coagulation

**8.** A 60-year-old man with bronchiectasis coughs up about 200 mL of blood at once and becomes hypoxemic. His hemoglobin is normal. Which of the following statements best explains the danger in this situation?

A. Exsanguination is the usual cause of death, so the normal hemoglobin is reassuring
B. Death usually results from asphyxiation, and a normal hemoglobin doesn't exclude life-threatening hemoptysis
C. Bleeding from the pulmonary circulation is high-pressure and hard to control
D. Hemoptysis this size is almost always from the nose
E. Normal hemoglobin indicates pseudohemoptysis

**9.** A 45-year-old man with long-standing cavitary pulmonary tuberculosis has sudden massive hemoptysis. Which of the following vascular lesions is classically responsible?

A. Pulmonary arteriovenous malformation
B. Pulmonary artery aneurysm eroding into a cavity (Rasmussen aneurysm)
C. Aortobronchial fistula
D. Diffuse alveolar hemorrhage
E. Bronchial carcinoid

**10.** A 38-year-old man who immigrated from Southeast Asia has chronic cough with hemoptysis and a chest radiograph resembling tuberculosis. Repeated sputum AFB smears and cultures are negative. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Kaposi sarcoma
B. Anti-GBM disease
C. Aspergilloma
D. Paragonimiasis
E. Aortobronchial fistula

**11.** A 65-year-old man has intermittent hemoptysis. Chest CT doesn't show a source. Bronchoscopy is planned to look for subtle mucosal lesions and localize bleeding. When is bronchoscopy highest-yield?

A. During an acute episode of active bleeding
B. Only after 6 weeks without bleeding
C. After empiric antibiotics have failed for 3 months
D. Only after surgical resection
E. Timing doesn't affect yield

## Answers

**1. C** — In most patients without risk factors for malignancy or abnormal initial findings and with a normal chest radiograph, treating for bronchitis with close follow-up is reasonable. Viral bronchitis causes most small-volume hemoptysis.
- A: CT is for patients with risk factors or significant hemoptysis.
- B: Bronchoscopy is for patients with risk factors or significant hemoptysis.
- D: Embolization is for life-threatening bleeding.
- E: Serologies are sent when pulmonary-renal disease is suspected.
Source: Ch 0041, Diagnostic Studies; Fig 41-1

**2. A** — Patients with malignancy risk factors (age >40, smoking) need chest CT even with a normal radiograph. CT identifies the site and cause in most patients, and bronchoscopy can follow.
- B: Treating as bronchitis is appropriate only without risk factors.
- C: Delay could miss a carcinoma.
- D: Culture alone doesn't evaluate malignancy.
- E: Surgery is for ongoing life-threatening bleeding after other measures fail.
Source: Ch 0041, Diagnostic Studies

**3. E** — When the bleeding side is known, position the patient bleeding side down so gravity keeps blood out of the nonbleeding lung.
- A: Lying flat doesn't protect the nonbleeding lung.
- B: Right side down puts the healthy right lung dependent and floods it.
- C: Prone positioning doesn't use the known laterality.
- D: Trendelenburg doesn't use the known laterality.
Source: Ch 0041, Interventions

**4. B** — A large (8.5) single-lumen tube allows suction and bronchoscopic interventions and can be advanced into the nonbleeding lung's mainstem bronchus for isolation.
- A: Small tubes limit suctioning and bronchoscopy.
- C: An LMA doesn't protect the airway.
- D: NIV doesn't secure the airway or allow suctioning.
- E: Tracheostomy isn't the initial approach.
Source: Ch 0041, Interventions

**5. D** — Most life-threatening hemoptysis comes from the bronchial circulation. Bronchial artery embolization is the procedure of choice, with >80% immediate success.
- A: Surgery carries 15–40% mortality and is reserved for when initial measures fail.
- B: She has no coagulopathy to correct.
- C: Observation is inadequate for life-threatening bleeding.
- E: Thrombolysis would worsen bleeding.
Source: Ch 0041, Interventions

**6. E** — Hemoptysis with dark urine suggests a pulmonary-renal syndrome (GPA, anti-GBM disease). Initial labs should include urinalysis and creatinine, with ANCA and anti-GBM serologies.
- A: Cytology targets malignancy.
- B: Upper GI bleeding isn't suggested.
- C: Echo doesn't address the renal findings.
- D: Angiography is for embolization planning in massive bleeding.
Source: Ch 0041, Vascular; Diagnostic Studies

**7. C** — Any known bleeding diathesis should be corrected first when controlling hemoptysis. Anticoagulation lets even minor insults bleed.
- A: Surgery is a last resort.
- B: Embolization is for life-threatening bleeding after initial measures.
- D: Antiplatelet therapy worsens bleeding.
- E: Rigid bronchoscopy is for central airway lesions; correct the coagulopathy first.
Source: Ch 0041, Interventions; Malignancy (coagulopathy)

**8. B** — Patients rarely exsanguinate. They die of asphyxiation from blood filling the airways, which can happen with >150 mL at one time. Anemia is often absent in life-threatening hemoptysis.
- A: Exsanguination is rare, so a normal hemoglobin isn't reassuring.
- C: The bronchial circulation (from the aorta) is the high-pressure source.
- D: Nothing suggests a nasal source.
- E: Hemoglobin doesn't distinguish true from pseudohemoptysis.
Source: Ch 0041, History; Diagnostic Studies

**9. B** — In tuberculosis, cavities are the usual source, but erosion of a pulmonary artery aneurysm into a preexisting cavity (Rasmussen aneurysm) is a recognized cause.
- A: AVMs aren't TB complications.
- C: Aortobronchial fistulas arise from aortic aneurysm or pseudoaneurysm.
- D: DAH often lacks hemoptysis and causes diffuse ground glass.
- E: Carcinoid is a tumor, not a TB complication.
Source: Ch 0041, Infections

**10. D** — Paragonimiasis mimics tuberculosis, is common in Southeast Asia and China, and should be considered in recent immigrants from endemic areas.
- A: Kaposi sarcoma occurs in advanced AIDS.
- B: Anti-GBM disease causes pulmonary-renal syndrome.
- C: Aspergilloma forms in a preexisting cavity, without the immigration link.
- E: Aortobronchial fistula requires aortic pathology.
Source: Ch 0041, Infections

**11. A** — Bronchoscopy complements CT by finding subtle mucosal lesions and localizing bleeding. It's best done during an acute episode to increase yield.
- B: Waiting reduces the chance of localizing the source.
- C: Delay isn't indicated.
- D: Bronchoscopy precedes, not follows, resection.
- E: The source specifies that timing affects yield.
Source: Ch 0041, Diagnostic Studies
