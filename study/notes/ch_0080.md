# Ch 80 — Oncologic Emergencies (high-yield)

## Superior vena cava syndrome
- **Causes:**
  - **lung cancer** (small cell and squamous; ~85% of malignant cases);
  - **lymphoma** (young adults);
  - **benign causes ≥40%** (catheters, pacemaker leads).
  - **Young man with mediastinal mass + SVCS:** lymphoma vs. **germ cell tumor**.
- **Symptoms:**
  - **face, neck and periorbital swelling** worse lying down or bending forward;
  - dyspnea, cough;
  - dilated neck and chest-wall veins, plethora, arm edema;
  - **severe: cerebral or laryngeal edema** (poor prognosis, urgent).
- **Diagnosis:** clinical.
  - **CXR:** superior mediastinal widening (right).
  - **CT is most reliable** (absent central venous opacification + collaterals).
  - **Tissue diagnosis is safe if no known cancer** (bronchoscopy, EBUS, mediastinoscopy).
  - Known cancer: chest CT, then treat.
- **Treatment:**
  - **tracheal obstruction is the true emergency**;
  - head elevation, O₂, diuretics, low salt;
  - **steroids only for lymphoma**;
  - **radiation for NSCLC and solid tumors; chemotherapy for SCLC, lymphoma, germ cell tumors**;
  - **endovascular stent** for rapid relief or severe symptoms (may precipitate heart failure);
  - **catheter thrombosis:** remove the catheter + anticoagulate, or fibrinolysis if caught early.
  - **Mortality reflects the underlying cancer, not the obstruction.**

## Pericardial effusion and tamponade
- **Cancers:** lung, breast, leukemia, lymphoma.
- **~50% of symptomatic effusions in cancer patients are not malignant:** radiation, drugs (ATRA, arsenic, imatinib), hypothyroidism, infection, checkpoint inhibitors.
- **Echo** is the most helpful test. **Fluid cytology** is diagnostic in most cases (tumor markers don't help).
- **Tamponade → immediate pericardiocentesis.**
  - Recurrence ~20% after catheter drainage.
  - **Sclerosis** (bleomycin, tetracycline) or **subxiphoid pericardiotomy**.
  - Thoracoscopic window: 60% recurrence in malignant effusions.

## Bowel, urinary and biliary obstruction
- **Bowel (colorectal, ovarian):**
  - multiple sites in carcinomatosis;
  - **melanoma → isolated small bowel** (resection may prolong survival);
  - **cecum >12–14 cm = surgical emergency**;
  - **CT:** malignant features = mass, abrupt transition, irregular thickening;
  - **treatment:** surgical evaluation; stents; **NG decompression; venting gastrostomy; octreotide; steroids**.
- **Urinary:**
  - prostate, cervix, pelvic radiation;
  - flank pain, rising creatinine → **renal ultrasound**;
  - **flank pain, sepsis or fistula → urgent diversion** (stent or nephrostomy).
- **Biliary:**
  - painless jaundice, pruritus;
  - **drain only for refractory pruritus, malabsorption or infection** (stent, bypass, EUS drainage).

## Malignant spinal cord compression
- **Epidemiology:**
  - 5–10% of cancer patients;
  - **lung, breast, prostate**, myeloma;
  - **thoracic 70%**, lumbosacral 20%, cervical 10%;
  - first sign of cancer in ~10%.
- **Back pain first (days to months), worse lying supine** (unlike disc disease), worse with cough or strain.
  - Thoracic radicular pain is **band-like**. Lhermitte sign.
  - **Weakness and sensory loss come before sphincter loss** (late, poor prognosis).
  - Sensory level 1–2 vertebrae **below** the lesion.
- **Evaluation:**
  - **Cancer + back pain → evaluate urgently.**
  - **Signs of cord compression → dexamethasone immediately + MRI of the entire spine** (25% have multiple epidural lesions).
  - Plain films are insensitive. **Myelography only if MRI is impossible.**
  - **Unknown primary:** CXR, mammography, PSA, abdominal CT.
- **Treatment:**
  - **radiation + glucocorticoids** for most;
  - **surgery for spinal instability or bone retropulsion**;
  - **separation surgery + SBRT for radioresistant tumors** (NOMS framework);
  - vertebroplasty or kyphoplasty for painful fractures without instability;
  - **outcome is best if still walking at treatment**.
- **Cauda equina:** saddle anesthesia, sphincter loss, absent ankle reflexes.

## Brain metastases and raised ICP
- **Cancers:** lung (most common unknown primary), breast, melanoma.
- **Hemorrhagic metastases:** **melanoma, germ cell tumors, RCC**.
- **Gadolinium MRI is better than CT.**
- **Treatment:**
  - **dexamethasone first** for symptoms (limits immunotherapy, so use **bevacizumab** if steroids can't be weaned);
  - single metastasis → resection + SRS;
  - **1–4 metastases → SRS** (WBRT causes cognitive decline);
  - shunt for hydrocephalus.
- **ATRA → pseudotumor cerebri.**

## Neoplastic meningitis
- **Cancers:** melanoma, breast (lobular, triple-negative), lung (EGFR, ALK), lymphoma, leukemia.
- **Presentation:** multifocal — **cranial nerve palsies**, headache, radicular pain.
- **Diagnosis:**
  - CSF cytology (**false negative up to 40%, so repeat the LP**); high CSF protein;
  - **MRI of the brain and spine** (~20% have cord findings).
- **Prognosis and treatment:**
  - **median survival 10–12 weeks**;
  - intrathecal MTX, cytarabine or thiotepa (Ommaya);
  - **breast responds best**; leukemia and lymphoma may be cured.

## Seizures
- **Brain metastases** are the most common cause in cancer patients.
- **Treat with levetiracetam (preferred), lacosamide or lamotrigine** — no CYP450 interactions. (Phenytoin interacts with chemotherapy and TKIs.)
- **No prophylactic anticonvulsants.** Stop post-craniotomy prophylaxis within 2 weeks.

## Leukostasis
- **Blasts >100,000** (mainly AML, especially M4/M5, FLT3).
- **Brain:** stupor, visual change, retinal hemorrhage. **Lung:** hypoxemia, infiltrates.
- **APL on ATRA or arsenic:** differentiation-related leukostasis; prevent with concurrent chemotherapy.

## Hemoptysis and airway
- **Massive hemoptysis: >200–600 mL/day** or any life-threatening amount.
  - **Bleeding side down**, protect the airway, intubate and bronchoscopy if needed.
  - **Bronchial artery embolization = first-line** (controls 75–90%; rebleeds 20–50%).
  - Bevacizumab or ramucirumab → hemorrhage in **squamous** NSCLC.
  - Aspergillus cavities can bleed after neutrophil recovery.
- **Airway obstruction:**
  - Heliox, steroids, humidified O₂;
  - **tracheostomy if above the larynx**;
  - otherwise bronchoscopic debulking or stenting.

## Metabolic emergencies
- **Hypercalcemia** is the most common paraneoplastic syndrome. **SIADH** is the most common cause of hyponatremia in cancer.
- **Lactic acidosis:** liver involvement; lymphoma or leukemia; NRTIs.
- **Non-islet cell tumor hypoglycemia:**
  - large mesenchymal tumors, hepatoma, adrenal tumors;
  - **big IGF-II: high IGF-II:IGF-I ratio, low insulin and C-peptide**, low β-hydroxybutyrate;
  - **treatment:** resection; glucose, steroids, GH, glucagon.
  - **Pseudohypoglycemia from hyperleukocytosis** (in vitro consumption).
- **Adrenal insufficiency:**
  - metastases, hemorrhage (sepsis, anticoagulation);
  - **megestrol**, mitotane, ketoconazole;
  - rapid steroid taper;
  - **ipilimumab hypophysitis**.
  - **Draw cortisol and ACTH, then treat.**

## Tumor lysis syndrome
- **Labs:** **↑ uric acid, ↑ K, ↑ phosphate, ↓ Ca** (+ acidosis, AKI).
- **Setting:**
  - Burkitt lymphoma, ALL, AML, aggressive lymphoma;
  - **venetoclax**, rituximab, steroids;
  - **1–5 days after chemotherapy**.
- **Risk:** **LDH >1500**, high uric acid, renal dysfunction.
  - **Urine uric acid:creatinine >1** = uric acid nephropathy.
  - **Pseudohyperkalemia** from high WBC or platelets → check **plasma K**.
- **Prevention:**
  - **aggressive hydration (~3 L/m²/day) + allopurinol**;
  - **febuxostat** for allopurinol allergy or renal failure;
  - **urinary alkalinization no longer recommended** (xanthine and calcium phosphate precipitate);
  - **rasburicase for high risk or established hyperuricemia with renal failure** — **contraindicated in G6PD deficiency**; degrades uric acid in the sample tube at room temperature.

## Treatment-related emergencies
- **Antibody infusion reactions / cytokine release syndrome** (rituximab, blinatumomab, **CAR-T**):
  - fever, hypotension, ARDS; can progress to HLH;
  - **tocilizumab** for severe CRS.
- **Chemotherapy-associated HUS (mitomycin, gemcitabine** most common):
  - **onset 4–8 weeks after the last dose**;
  - MAHA, platelets <100k, AKI, **negative Coombs**, normal coagulation;
  - high mortality;
  - **eculizumab or ravulizumab** (atypical HUS; **meningococcal vaccine first**); rituximab.
- **Pulmonary toxicity:**
  - **bleomycin**, MTX, busulfan, nitrosoureas, gemcitabine;
  - **↓ DLCO**;
  - **use the lowest FiO₂** (O₂ potentiates injury);
  - steroids.
- **Checkpoint inhibitor pneumonitis:** stop the drug + high-dose steroids.
- **Radiation pneumonitis:**
  - **2–6 months after RT**, confined to the field;
  - **prednisone if symptomatic**; asymptomatic → no treatment.
- **Neutropenic enterocolitis (typhlitis):**
  - fever + neutropenia + RLQ pain;
  - **CT: cecal wall thickening, pneumatosis**;
  - **broad-spectrum antibiotics, bowel rest, NG suction, G-CSF**;
  - surgery only for perforation, peritonitis or bleeding.
  - Cover *C. difficile* if it can't be excluded.
- **Hemorrhagic cystitis:**
  - **cyclophosphamide or ifosfamide (acrolein)** → **hydration + mesna**;
  - treatment: alum or formalin irrigation;
  - **late after BMT → BK virus or adenovirus**.
- **Hypersensitivity:**
  - **taxanes** react at the 1st–2nd dose; **platinum after repeated exposure**;
  - premedicate with H1, H2 blockers + steroids;
  - desensitization;
  - **tryptase = mast cell activation**.
