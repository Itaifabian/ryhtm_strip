# Ch 23 — Syncope (high-yield)

## Core facts
- **Syncope:** transient loss of consciousness from **global cerebral hypoperfusion**. Rapid onset, brief, with spontaneous complete recovery.
- **Not syncope:** seizures, vertebrobasilar ischemia, hypoxemia, hypoglycemia.
- **Three categories:**
  1. Neurally mediated (reflex, vasovagal)
  2. Orthostatic hypotension
  3. Cardiac
- **Epidemiology:**
  - ~3% of ED visits; lifetime incidence up to 40%.
  - Bimodal: peak at 10–30 y (median ~15, almost all neurally mediated), then a sharp rise after 70.
  - Neurally mediated is the most common cause overall (more common in women). Cardiac is next, especially in the ED and in older patients.
- **Prognosis:**
  - Noncardiac or unexplained syncope in the young: excellent, with no effect on life expectancy.
  - Cardiac syncope: ↑ sudden death and all-cause mortality.
  - Orthostatic hypotension: ↑ mortality from age and comorbidity.
- **Physiology:**
  - Standing pools 500–1000 mL of blood.
  - Flow cessation for **6–8 s** → loss of consciousness.
  - SBP ~**50 mmHg** or lower usually → syncope.
  - Autoregulation holds flow steady at 50–150 mmHg perfusion pressure.

## Table 23-1 — high-risk features → admit or evaluate intensively
- **Cardiac disease:** ischemic chest pain, CHF, moderate–severe valvular or structural disease, ECG ischemia.
- **Arrhythmia history:**
  - prior ventricular arrhythmia;
  - **QT >500 ms**;
  - repetitive SA block or sinus pauses;
  - persistent sinus bradycardia;
  - **bi- or trifascicular block or QRS ≥120 ms**;
  - AF, NSVT, preexcitation, **Brugada pattern**.
- **History:** family history of sudden death, **palpitations at the time of syncope**, **syncope at rest or during exercise**.
- **Exam red flags:** unexplained SBP <90, signs of GI bleeding, persistent HR <40, undiagnosed systolic murmur.
- **New-onset symptoms:** chest discomfort, abdominal pain, dyspnea, headache.

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Vasovagal** | Fear, pain, emotion, sight of blood, orthostatic stress |
| **Situational reflex** | Pulmonary: cough, sneeze, wind instrument, weightlifting, airway instrumentation. Urogenital: postmicturition, prostatic massage. GI: swallow, glossopharyngeal neuralgia, defecation, rectal exam. Cardiac: Bezold-Jarisch. **Carotid sinus** sensitivity. Ocular pressure or surgery |
| **Orthostatic, primary autonomic failure** (synucleinopathies) | Parkinson disease, Lewy body dementia, pure autonomic failure, **MSA (Shy-Drager)** |
| **Orthostatic, secondary autonomic neuropathy** | **Diabetes**, amyloidosis (hereditary and AL), HSAN III (familial dysautonomia), autoimmune autonomic ganglionopathy, Sjögren, paraneoplastic, HIV |
| **Orthostatic, other** | Postprandial hypotension, **iatrogenic (drugs)**, volume depletion |
| **Cardiac, arrhythmia** | Sinus node dysfunction, AV block, SVT, VT, channelopathies (long QT, Brugada [SCN5A], CPVT) |
| **Cardiac, structural** | Valvular disease (AS), ischemia, HCM, atrial myxoma, effusion or tamponade |

## Neurally mediated syncope
- **Requires an intact autonomic nervous system.** Vagal surge plus sympathetic withdrawal → bradycardia and/or vasodilation.
- **Subtypes:** vasodepressor, cardioinhibitory, mixed.
- **Prodrome:** diaphoresis, pallor, nausea, yawning, hyperventilation.
- **During the event:** arrhythmic multifocal myoclonus, eyes open and deviated upward, urinary incontinence possible.
- **Rare in syncope:** fecal incontinence and postictal confusion.
- **Tilt table:** abrupt fall in BP *and* HR (Fig 23-2).
- **Treatment:**
  - **Reassurance, education, avoid triggers, fluid and salt.**
  - **Counterpressure maneuvers;** abdominal and leg tensing is most effective.
  - Refractory: only **midodrine** has multicenter RCT support. Fludrocortisone and β-blockers are used but lack that evidence.
  - Pacemaker is rarely helpful. Exception: **dual-chamber closed-loop pacing** for patients >40 with frequent syncope and a **cardioinhibitory response on tilt** [verify: an area of uncertainty per source].

## Orthostatic hypotension
- **Definition:** **SBP fall ≥20 or DBP fall ≥10 mmHg within 3 min** of standing or tilt.
- **Autonomic failure:** often **no compensatory rise in HR**; the tilt table shows a gradual BP fall with flat HR (Fig 23-3).
- **Variants:**
  - **Delayed:** after more than 3 min; may be early autonomic failure.
  - **Initial:** within 15 s, resolves by 45 s; not autonomic failure.
- **Clues:** **coat-hanger headache** (suboccipital or neck ischemia), orthostatic dyspnea or angina, worse after meals, heat or exertion.
- **Supine hypertension** occurs in >50%.
- **Treatment, in order:**
  1. **Stop offending drugs:** α-blockers, diuretics, nitrates, vasodilators, TCAs, phenothiazines.
  2. Staged rising, avoid large carbohydrate meals and alcohol, counterpressure maneuvers, **raise the head of the bed** (cuts supine hypertension and nocturnal diuresis), more fluid and salt. A **500 mL water bolus** gives a short pressor effect.
  3. Drugs: **fludrocortisone, midodrine, droxidopa**.
  4. Add-ons: pyridostigmine, atomoxetine, octreotide, DDAVP, EPO.

## Cardiac syncope
- **Stokes-Adams attack:** syncope from bradycardia or asystole.
  - Causes: sinus arrest, SA block, Mobitz II, high-grade or complete AV block.
  - **Tachy-brady syndrome:** pause after a tachycardia ends.
- **VT:** syncope is less likely at rates <200 and more likely with poor LV function.
- **Treatment:** pacing for bradycardia; ablation, antiarrhythmics or ICD for tachyarrhythmias.

## Syncope vs. seizure
| Feature | Syncope | Seizure |
|---|---|---|
| Movements | Myoclonus in up to 90%; arrhythmic, <30 s | Tonic-clonic |
| Trigger | Emotion, pain, posture | Rarely emotion or pain |
| Warning | Autonomic prodrome | Aura (smell, fear, epigastric) |
| Duration of LOC | Brief | Often >5 min |
| Recovery | Reorients almost immediately | Prolonged postictal confusion |
| Fecal incontinence | Very rare | Can occur |

**Other mimics:**
- **Hypoglycemia:** hunger and tremor; hunger is not a syncope prodrome.
- **Cataplexy:** triggered by laughter or anger, **consciousness preserved**, lasts 30 s–2 min.
- **Psychogenic:** frequent episodes, no prodrome, rarely injured, no hemodynamic change.

## Workup
1. **History** (with eyewitnesses), exam, **orthostatic vitals** (supine, then after 3 min standing), **ECG**. This step finds the cause in ~50%.
2. **Labs:** only if a specific disorder is suspected (MI, anemia, autonomic failure).
3. **Monitoring is matched to frequency:**
   - **Inpatient telemetry** if high risk (Table 23-1).
   - **Holter** for daily or near-daily episodes.
   - **External loop recorder** for episodes more than once a month.
   - **Implantable loop recorder** for less frequent episodes.
   - Monitor for **at least twice the interval between episodes**.
4. **Echo:** known heart disease, or an abnormal exam or ECG. Looks for AS, HCM, tumor, dissection, tamponade, and LVEF.
5. **Exercise treadmill test:** syncope during or just after exertion.
6. **Carotid sinus massage:** suspected carotid sinus syncope, or **>40 y with recurrent unexplained syncope**. Use ECG and BP monitoring. **Avoid with bruits, plaque or stenosis.**
7. **Electrophysiology study:** only for structural disease plus ECG abnormality after a nondiagnostic noninvasive workup. Sensitivity and specificity are low.
8. **Tilt table:** separates reflex syncope from autonomic failure and can reproduce psychogenic events without a hemodynamic change.
