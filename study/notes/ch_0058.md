# Ch 58 — Acidosis and Alkalosis (high-yield)

## Expected compensation (Table 58-1)
| Disorder | Expected compensation |
|---|---|
| **Metabolic acidosis** | **Winter: PaCO₂ = 1.5 × HCO₃⁻ + 8 ± 2** (or ≈ HCO₃⁻ + 15) |
| **Metabolic alkalosis** | PaCO₂ ↑ 0.75 per 1 mmol/L ↑ HCO₃⁻ (≈ 6 per 10) |
| **Acute respiratory acidosis** | HCO₃⁻ ↑ 1 per 10 mmHg ↑ PaCO₂ (usually no higher than 38) |
| **Chronic respiratory acidosis** | HCO₃⁻ ↑ 4 per 10 mmHg ↑ PaCO₂ |
| **Acute respiratory alkalosis** | HCO₃⁻ ↓ 2 per 10 mmHg ↓ PaCO₂ |
| **Chronic respiratory alkalosis** | HCO₃⁻ ↓ 4 per 10 mmHg ↓ PaCO₂ (can **normalize pH**, the only exception) |

- **PaCO₂ and HCO₃⁻ moving in opposite directions = a mixed disorder.**
- **Example:** HCO₃⁻ 12 → expected PaCO₂ ~26. Below 24 = added respiratory alkalosis; above 28 = added respiratory acidosis.
- **Nomogram:** convenient but doesn't replace the equations.

## Stepwise approach (Table 58-3)
1. **Draw ABG and electrolytes at the same time.** Calculated and measured HCO₃⁻ should agree within 2.
2. **Anion gap = Na − (Cl + HCO₃⁻).**
   - Normal **6–12 (~10)**; high if >10.
   - **Correct for albumin:** add **2.5 for each g/dL below 4.5**.
   - **Low AG:** lithium, cationic paraproteins (myeloma), hypoalbuminemia, severe hyperlipidemia.
3. Identify the cause: high-AG or non-AG.
4. Check the expected compensation.
5. **Compare ΔAG (AG − 10) with ΔHCO₃⁻ (25 − HCO₃⁻).**
   - **ΔAG ≫ ΔHCO₃⁻ → added metabolic alkalosis** (e.g., uremia + vomiting, AKA + vomiting).
   - **ΔHCO₃⁻ ≫ ΔAG → added non-AG acidosis** (DKA during treatment, diarrhea + lactate).
6. **A high AG matters even when pH and HCO₃⁻ are normal.**

## Mixed disorder examples (Table 58-2)
- **Sepsis or lactic acidosis + respiratory alkalosis:** PaCO₂ below predicted, pH near normal.
- **Pneumonia or pulmonary edema:** metabolic + respiratory acidosis (PaCO₂ above predicted) → severe acidemia.
- **COPD on diuretics:** respiratory acidosis + metabolic alkalosis; pH normal.
- **Salicylates:** **high-AG metabolic acidosis + respiratory alkalosis**.
- **Triple disorder:** AKA + vomiting + hyperventilation.

## Metabolic acidosis
- **Effects:**
  - hyperventilation;
  - ↓ contractility;
  - **venoconstriction → pulmonary edema with small volume loads**;
  - CNS depression.
- **High-AG causes (Table 58-4):** **lactic acidosis, ketoacidosis (diabetic, alcoholic, starvation), toxins (ethylene glycol, methanol, salicylates, propylene glycol, pyroglutamic acid), kidney failure.**
- **Initial screen:** drug history + ABG (respiratory alkalosis = salicylates); diabetes; alcohol plus β-hydroxybutyrate; BUN/Cr; **urine oxalate crystals (ethylene glycol)**; shock or leukemia (lactate).
- **Lactic acidosis:**
  - **Type A (hypoperfusion):** shock, severe anemia, **CO, cyanide**.
  - **Type B:** malignancy, **NRTIs**, metformin, liver failure, **thiamine deficiency**, seizures, toxic alcohols.
  - **Bowel ischemia** in elderly vasculopaths on pressors is common.
  - **D-lactic acidosis:** short bowel or jejunoileal bypass.
  - **Treatment:** fix perfusion. **Bicarbonate only if pH <7.0**, to pH ≤7.2 or HCO₃⁻ ≤12. It can worsen lactate production and cause overload or overshoot alkalosis.
- **DKA:**
  - **Euglycemic DKA on SGLT2 inhibitors.**
  - **Nitroprusside (urine ketones) misses β-hydroxybutyrate**; measure it in plasma.
  - **Treatment:**
    - isotonic saline (avoid overexpansion, which causes hyperchloremic acidosis);
    - **insulin 0.1 U/kg bolus + 0.1 U/kg/h until the AG closes**;
    - **bicarbonate only if pH <7.0 or shock** (50 mEq in 300 mL over 30–45 min; target HCO₃⁻ 10–12, pH ~7.2); risk of hypokalemia and cerebral edema.
- **Alcoholic ketoacidosis:**
  - binge drinking, vomiting, high AG, **glucose low or variable**, mostly β-hydroxybutyrate;
  - **nitroprusside turns more positive as the patient improves**;
  - often mixed disorders;
  - **treatment: D5NS**; watch **phosphate (falls at 12–24 h)**, K and Mg.
- **Salicylates:**
  - **charcoal**; **alkalinize urine to pH >7.5** (5× clearance) with IV bicarbonate;
  - replace K; give glucose;
  - **hemodialysis if AKI**.
- **Toxic alcohols:**
  - **Osmolality = 2Na + glucose/18 + BUN/2.8.** **Osmolar gap >10–15** = unmeasured osmoles.
  - **High AG + high osmolar gap = ethylene glycol or methanol → treat before levels return.** The osmolar gap rises first, and the AG rises as it falls.
  - **Ethylene glycol** (antifreeze): **oxalate crystals**, AKI, CNS, cardiac and lung injury.
  - **Methanol:** **optic nerve** and CNS damage (formic acid).
  - **Treatment:**
    - **fomepizole 15 mg/kg, then 10 mg/kg q12h**, until pH normal and osmolar gap <10; ethanol only if fomepizole is unavailable;
    - fluids, **thiamine and pyridoxine**;
    - **hemodialysis if pH <7.3, osmolar gap >20, or end-organ damage**.
  - **Isopropyl alcohol:** osmolar gap **without** a high AG (acetone); hypoglycemia; dialysis if level >400 or unstable.
  - **Propylene glycol:** IV lorazepam or diazepam in the ICU (alcohol withdrawal) → stop the infusion.
  - **Pyroglutamic acid (5-oxoproline):** acetaminophen in malnourished or critically ill patients; high AG **without an osmolar gap** → stop acetaminophen, give bicarbonate.
- **CKD:**
  - non-AG acidosis (stage 3B) becomes high-AG (stage 4–5);
  - consequences: bone loss, muscle wasting, faster progression;
  - **oral alkali to HCO₃⁻ >22–24** (1–1.5 mmol/kg/day; NaHCO₃ tablets 650 mg = 7.8 mEq), plus fruits and vegetables.

## Non-anion gap acidosis (Table 58-5)
- **ΔCl⁻ ≈ ΔHCO₃⁻.**
- **GI causes:** diarrhea, fistulas, **ureteral diversion**.
- **Renal causes:** RTA; also acetazolamide, saline expansion, toluene/hippurate.
- **Urine anion gap = (Na + K) − Cl:**
  - **negative = high NH₄⁺ → GI loss**;
  - **positive = low NH₄⁺ → RTA**;
  - correlation is imperfect, so measure urine NH₄⁺ when possible.
  - **Urine pH is often >6 in diarrhea** (high NH₄⁺).

| | Proximal (type 2) | Distal (type 1) | Type 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defect | HCO₃⁻ reabsorption, often **Fanconi** (glycosuria, aminoaciduria, phosphaturia) | H⁺ secretion | Aldosterone deficiency or resistance |
| K⁺ | Low (worse with alkali) | **Low** | **High** |
| Urine pH | <5.5 when HCO₃⁻ is low; >5.5 on alkali; FE HCO₃⁻ >10–15% | **>5.5** | <5.5 |
| Associations | **Acetazolamide, topiramate** | **Amphotericin, ifosfamide**; **stones, nephrocalcinosis, hypocitraturia, hypercalciuria** | **Diabetes, elderly, CKD 3–4**; NSAIDs, TMP, pentamidine, ACE inhibitors or ARBs |
| Treatment | Large alkali + **K citrate** | **Correct K first**, then alkali (target 24–26) | **Lower K** (patiromer 8.4 g BID, low-K diet, stop K-retaining drugs) ± **fludrocortisone** |

## Metabolic alkalosis
- **Generation:** H⁺ loss (vomiting or NG suction, diuretics) or alkali gain.
- **Maintenance:** volume, Cl⁻ and K⁺ depletion with ↓ GFR, **or** autonomous mineralocorticoid excess.
- **Classify by volume status, BP, K⁺ and urine Cl⁻:**
  - **Saline-responsive (urine Cl <20–25):** vomiting or NG suction, **prior** diuretics, **posthypercapnia**, villous adenoma, congenital chloridorrhea.
  - **Saline-unresponsive with normal BP (urine Cl >20):** **current diuretics, Bartter (hypercalciuria) or Gitelman (hypocalciuria)**, Mg or K depletion.
  - **Hypertensive:**
    - **high renin:** RAS, accelerated hypertension, renin tumor, estrogen;
    - **low renin:** **primary aldosteronism**, 11β- or 17α-hydroxylase deficiency, **Cushing, licorice, carbenoxolone, chewing tobacco, Liddle**.
  - **Urine pattern (alkaline urine, high Na and K, low Cl):** active vomiting or alkali.
- **Other causes:**
  - alkali loads with low ECFV (**massive transfusion citrate**, NaHCO₃);
  - **milk-alkali syndrome**;
  - high-dose penicillins;
  - recovery from lactic acidosis or ketoacidosis;
  - hypercalcemia.
- **Symptoms:** like hypocalcemia — confusion, seizures, paresthesias, tetany, arrhythmias; hypokalemia, hypophosphatemia.
- **Treatment:**
  - **stop the generator** (PPI for gastric losses, stop diuretics; treat aldosteronism or Cushing);
  - **always replace K**;
  - **isotonic saline if volume contracted**;
  - **acetazolamide 125–250 mg IV if saline isn't possible (HF)** — watch K;
  - **dilute HCl isn't advised**;
  - **Liddle → amiloride or triamterene**.

## Respiratory acidosis (Table 58-7)
- **Causes:**
  - central (opioids, sedatives, anesthetics, stroke);
  - airway (asthma, obstruction);
  - parenchyma (COPD, ARDS);
  - neuromuscular (myasthenia, polio, dystrophy, kyphoscoliosis);
  - obesity hypoventilation;
  - poorly adjusted ventilator (↑ CO₂ production from fever, sepsis, overfeeding);
  - high PEEP with low cardiac output.
- **Acute:** anxiety, confusion, coma. **Chronic:** somnolence, **asterixis, myoclonus, papilledema**.
- **Treatment:**
  - treat the cause; ventilation or intubation;
  - **titrate O₂ carefully in CO₂ retainers**;
  - **lower chronic PaCO₂ gradually** (rapid correction → arrhythmias, seizures, ↓ cerebral perfusion; posthypercapnic alkalosis);
  - **permissive hypercapnia:** bicarbonate only to pH ~7.2.

## Respiratory alkalosis (Table 58-7)
- **Causes:**
  - **pain, anxiety, fever**, CNS lesions;
  - **hypoxemia** (altitude, pneumonia, PE, edema, anemia);
  - **pregnancy (progesterone), salicylates**;
  - **sepsis, hepatic failure**;
  - mechanical overventilation, heat;
  - recovery from metabolic acidosis.
