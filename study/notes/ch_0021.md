# Ch 21 — Fever and Rash (high-yield)

## Approach
- **History:** immune status, **drugs in the past month**, travel, vaccines, pets and animals, bites (incl. arthropods), diet, heart valve disease, prosthetic material, sick contacts, sexual exposures. Also **where the rash began and which way it spread**.
- **Morphology:**
  - macule = flat;
  - papule <5 mm; plaque >5 mm and flat; nodule >5 mm and rounded;
  - wheal = transient (<24 h);
  - vesicle <5 mm, bulla >5 mm;
  - petechiae <3 mm, ecchymoses >3 mm;
  - **palpable purpura = vasculitis**;
  - eschar = black necrotic crust.
- Rashes evolve: RMSF can start as blanchable macules, may not be acral, or may have **no rash** at all.

## Central maculopapular (most common type)
| Disease | Key discriminator |
|---|---|
| **Measles** | Starts at the hairline on day 2–3 and moves down; spares palms and soles; **Koplik spots** (white or blue with a red halo; pathognomonic; not Fordyce spots, which lack a halo); cough, coryza, conjunctivitis |
| **Rubella** | Hairline downward but **clears as it spreads**, may itch; postauricular/suboccipital nodes; arthritis in adults; **keep pregnant women away** (congenital defects) |
| **Parvovirus B19** (fifth disease) | Ages 3–12, rash **after** fever: **slapped cheeks** → lacy reticular rash for 3 wk; adults get arthritis; **fetal hydrops** in pregnancy; papular-purpuric gloves-and-socks |
| **Roseola** (HHV-6/7) | Under age 3, rash **after** fever, trunk and neck, gone in 2 days, spares the face; febrile seizures |
| **EBV mono** | Rash ~20% without antibiotics (older teaching: ~90% with ampicillin); periorbital edema 50%, palatal petechiae 25% |
| **Drug exanthem** | Intensely pruritic, bright red; 2–3 d after re-exposure or 2–3 wk after first exposure; can persist **2 wk after stopping**; **no prostration**; **50–60% of people with HIV react to sulfa** |
| **Epidemic typhus** | Body lice (war, disaster), starts in the **axillae**, spares face, palms and soles; recrudescence = Brill-Zinsser |
| Endemic typhus / **leptospirosis** | Urban rodents; lepto: conjunctival suffusion, water contaminated with animal urine, Weil disease |
| **Lyme** (erythema migrans, average 15 cm) vs **STARI** (Lone Star tick, ~8 cm, southern U.S., milder) | Annular with central clearing |
| **Typhoid** | Rose spots (2–4 mm, trunk), travel |
| **Dengue** | Flushing → trunk → extremities; petechiae after defervescence; "breakbone fever"; leukopenia |
| **Still disease** | Evanescent **salmon** rash **at fever spikes**; ESR >100 |
| **Erythema marginatum** | Shifting annular waves (rheumatic fever) |
| **Zika** | Pruritic rash + conjunctivitis; birth defects (microcephaly), GBS |

## Peripheral (acral → central)
- **RMSF:** begins at the **wrists and ankles**, spreads centripetally, reaches **palms and soles late**, macules → petechiae. **Mortality up to 40% untreated → treat early.**
- **Secondary syphilis:** copper, scaly papules, prominent on **palms and soles**, never vesicular in adults. Consider it in any sexually active patient with a pityriasis-rosea-like rash.
- **Others:**
  - hand-foot-and-mouth (coxsackie A16, EV71; A6 atypical; later nail shedding);
  - **erythema multiforme** (targets on the extremities; **HSV, Mycoplasma**, sulfa, phenytoin, penicillin);
  - endocarditis (Osler = tender; Janeway = painless palms and soles; splinters);
  - chikungunya (severe small-joint arthralgia);
  - COVID pernio.

## Confluent desquamative erythema
| Disease | Clue |
|---|---|
| Scarlet fever | After strep pharyngitis; sandpaper rash, **Pastia lines**, strawberry tongue, circumoral pallor |
| Kawasaki | Child <8; cracked lips, conjunctivitis, nodes, hand and foot edema → **coronary vasculitis** |
| **Strep TSS** | Severe GAS **infection** (e.g., necrotizing fasciitis); mortality 30% |
| **Staph TSS** | Usually only **colonization**; T >39°C, hypotension, palm erythema, desquamation at day 7–10 |
| **SSSS** | Children <10, adults with renal failure; **exquisitely tender skin**, Nikolsky sign; **superficial split**, no strawberry tongue |
| **SJS/TEN** | **80% drugs** (allopurinol, anticonvulsants, antibiotics); **mucosa involved**; full-thickness epidermal loss. SJS <10%, overlap 10–30%, **TEN >30%**; mortality up to 30%; ↑ in HIV and SLE |
| **DRESS/DIHS** | Aromatic anticonvulsants, sulfa, minocycline; **facial edema, eosinophilia, atypical lymphocytes, hepatitis**, HHV-6; mortality ~10% |
| Exfoliative erythroderma | Men >50: psoriasis, eczema, drug, mycosis fungoides |

## Vesiculobullous / pustular
- **Varicella:** lesions in **different stages** in one area, mostly truncal, "dewdrops on a rose petal."
- **Smallpox:** lesions at the **same stage**, mostly face and extremities incl. palms and soles.
- **Mpox (2022):** fewer than 20 lesions, painful, anogenital or oral, mostly MSM.
- **Hot-tub folliculitis** (*Pseudomonas*): areas under the bathing suit, outbreaks, **self-limited**.
- **Rickettsialpox:** urban mouse mite, **eschar**, vesicles on papules.
- **AGEP:** sterile pustules 2–21 days after a drug (anticonvulsants, antimicrobials).
- **Sepsis with hemorrhagic bullae in the immunocompromised:**
  - **Vibrio vulnificus** (cirrhosis, diabetes or CKD + raw seafood or saltwater; mortality 50%);
  - **ecthyma gangrenosum** (*Pseudomonas*, neutropenia; axilla, groin, perianal).
- **RIME/MIRM:** children, severe mucositis with sparse skin rash, *Mycoplasma*, good prognosis.

## Urticaria-like
Classic hives = no fever, lesions <24 h. **Fever + lesions lasting >24 h (up to 5 days) that don't fully blanch = urticarial vasculitis.** Causes: serum sickness (penicillins, sulfa), **acute hepatitis B**, enterovirus, parasites, SLE, Sjögren, lymphoma.

## Nodular
- Immunocompromised host → **disseminated infection**:
  - *Candida* (often *C. tropicalis*): fever, myalgias, nodules;
  - *Cryptococcus* looks like molluscum;
  - **necrotic nodules → Aspergillus or Mucor**.
- **Erythema nodosum:** exquisitely tender shin nodules, women 15–30 (strep, TB, fungi, *Yersinia*, sulfa, OCPs, sarcoid).
- **Sweet syndrome:** edematous plaques that look vesicular; **20% have malignancy (usually hematologic)**; also IBD, pregnancy, G-CSF.
- **Bacillary angiomatosis** (*Bartonella*): advanced HIV.

## Purpuric
- **Acute meningococcemia:** children, **asplenia, C5–C8 deficiency**; macules → petechiae → purpura fulminans (DIC).
- **Echovirus 9 can mimic meningococcemia → treat as bacterial sepsis.**
- **DGI:** fewer than 40 **hemorrhagic pustules near joints**, tenosynovitis, arthritis; women; complement deficiency. Looks like chronic meningococcemia.
- **Others:**
  - viral hemorrhagic fever (travel; fever, shock, bleeding);
  - **TTP/HUS** (fever, MAHA, low platelets, kidney and neuro dysfunction, **normal coagulation tests**);
  - **leukocytoclastic vasculitis = palpable purpura** on the legs (HSP, hepatitis B or C, strep, drugs).

## Ulcer or eschar
Scrub typhus (mite, Asia-Pacific), rickettsialpox, spotted fevers (African tick-bite fever), **tularemia** (ulceroglandular), **anthrax** (painless ulcer with vesicles and edema).

## Next best step
| Scenario | Next step |
|---|---|
| Febrile petechial rash, can't tell meningococcus from echovirus | Treat as bacterial sepsis |
| Tick exposure, rash from wrists and ankles inward | Treat for RMSF now |
| New drug, pruritic rash, not prostrate | Stop the drug |
| Recurrent *Neisseria* infection | Check terminal complement (C5–C8) |
| Febrile urticaria lasting >24 h | Look for serum sickness (hepatitis B, drugs) |
| Sweet syndrome | Evaluate for hematologic malignancy |
| Pityriasis-like rash, sexually active | Syphilis serology |
| Rubella case | Keep pregnant contacts away |
| Hot-tub folliculitis | Reassure (self-limited) |
