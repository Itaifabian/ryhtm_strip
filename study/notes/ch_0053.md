# Ch 53 — Abdominal Swelling and Ascites (high-yield)

## Abdominal swelling: the six Fs
| F | Clues |
|---|---|
| **Flatus** | Aerophagia (gum, smoking, gulping), celiac disease, gastroparesis, FODMAPs, bacterial overgrowth, IBS (visceral hypersensitivity); **abdominophrenic dyssynergia** |
| **Fat** | Obesity, **Cushing syndrome** |
| **Fluid** | Ascites. Grade 1: ultrasound only. Grade 2: found on exam. Grade 3: marked distention |
| **Fetus** | Abdomen enlarges from **12–14 weeks** |
| **Feces** | Constipation, obstruction |
| **Fatal growth** | Tumor (ovarian), abscess, cyst, organomegaly, AAA, distended bladder |

## Evaluation
- **History:** no stool or flatus + vomiting → obstruction or ileus. Alcohol or jaundice → liver disease. Heart failure, TB.
- **Exam:**
  - **Virchow node** (malignancy);
  - **JVP, Kussmaul sign, pericardial knock** (constrictive pericarditis), TR murmur;
  - spider angiomata, palmar erythema, **caput medusae**, gynecomastia;
  - **umbilical venous hum** (portal hypertension); hepatic bruit (HCC, alcoholic hepatitis);
  - **pulsatile liver** (TR).
- **Percussion:** tympany = gas; dullness = fluid or mass.
  - **Exam needs ≥1500 mL of ascites; ultrasound detects ~100 mL.**
- **Tests:**
  - LFTs, albumin, INR, CBC;
  - **amylase and lipase**;
  - **urine protein** if nephrotic syndrome is suspected;
  - **CT** if malignancy or pancreatic disease is suspected (ultrasound misses the retroperitoneum and pancreas).
  - HVPG, elastography or biopsy to confirm cirrhosis.

## Pathogenesis of cirrhotic ascites
- **Portal hypertension:**
  - fibrosis;
  - stellate cell contraction;
  - **↓ intrahepatic nitric oxide** (vasoconstriction).
- **Splanchnic vasodilation:** ↑ systemic NO, VEGF and TNF.
- **Result:** ↓ effective circulating volume → **ADH, SNS and RAAS → Na⁺ and water retention**.

## Causes
- **Cirrhosis: 84%.**
- Cardiac, carcinomatosis, or mixed: 10–15%.
- **Others:** massive liver metastases, TB, *Chlamydia*, pancreatitis, nephrotic syndrome; rarely hypothyroidism, FMF.

## Paracentesis
- **Left lower quadrant is preferred** (deeper fluid, thinner wall).
- **Safe even with coagulopathy.**
- **Gross appearance:**
  - **Turbid:** infection or tumor.
  - **Milky (TG >200 mg/dL): chylous** (trauma, cirrhosis, tumor, TB, congenital).
  - **Dark brown: biliary perforation.**
  - **Black:** pancreatic necrosis, melanoma.
- **Send:** albumin, total protein, cell count and differential; Gram stain and **culture in blood culture bottles at the bedside**; plus **simultaneous serum albumin**.

## SAAG (Fig 53-3)
**SAAG = serum albumin − ascitic albumin.** It doesn't change with diuresis.

| SAAG ≥1.1 (portal hypertension), protein **<2.5** | SAAG ≥1.1, protein **≥2.5** | SAAG **<1.1** |
|---|---|---|
| **Cirrhosis** | **Heart failure, constrictive pericarditis** | **Peritoneal carcinomatosis** |
| Late Budd-Chiari | **Early Budd-Chiari** | **TB** |
| Massive liver metastases | IVC obstruction | **Pancreatitis** |
| | **Sinusoidal obstruction syndrome** | **Nephrotic syndrome** |
| | | Biliary leak |

- **Protein ≥2.5 means the sinusoids are normal** and leak protein. **<2.5 means they're scarred.**
- **BNP** helps identify heart failure as the cause of high-SAAG ascites.
- **Special tests:**
  - **Secondary peritonitis:** **glucose <50, ascitic LDH > serum LDH, multiple organisms**.
  - **Pancreatic:** ascitic amylase **>1000**.
  - **Carcinomatosis:** **cytology** (≥50 mL, processed immediately).
  - **TB peritonitis:**
    - **lymphocytes**;
    - AFB smear 0–3% sensitive, culture 35–50%;
    - **adenosine deaminase >30–45 U/L is >90% sensitive** (noncirrhotic);
    - **gold standard: laparoscopy with peritoneal biopsy**.

## Treatment of cirrhotic ascites
1. **Sodium restriction 2 g/day.**
2. **Spironolactone + furosemide in a 100:40 ratio** (maximum 400 mg and 160 mg).
   - Spironolactone causes hyperkalemia, hyponatremia and **painful gynecomastia** → **amiloride 5–40 mg** as an alternative.
3. **Fluid restriction if Na <125.**
4. **Avoid NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors and ARBs.**
- **Refractory ascites** (diuretic-resistant or diuretic-intractable):
  - add **midodrine ± clonidine**;
  - **β-blockers may lower survival**;
  - **large-volume paracentesis (>5 L) + IV albumin 6–8 g per liter removed** (prevents post-paracentesis circulatory dysfunction and death);
  - **TIPS:** less reaccumulation, **more encephalopathy, same mortality**;
  - Alfapump.
- **Malignant ascites:** **doesn't respond to Na restriction or diuretics** → serial LVP, tunneled drain, peritoneovenous shunt.
- **TB peritonitis:** standard anti-TB therapy.

## Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis
- **Presentation:**
  - tenderness in only **40%**;
  - fever, vomiting;
  - **new or worse encephalopathy**.
- **Paracentesis within 12 h of admission reduces mortality.**
- **Diagnosis: ascitic PMN ≥250/µL.** Usually **one organism**: *E. coli*, *Klebsiella*, streptococci, enterococci.
  - **Multiple organisms + high PMN → secondary peritonitis.**
  - **Multiple organisms + normal PMN → the needle entered bowel.**
- **Treatment:**
  - **cefotaxime 2 g IV q12h**;
  - **5 days** if improving;
  - **repeat paracentesis at 48 h** (PMN should fall ≥25%);
  - **carbapenem** for nosocomial infection or critical illness.
- **Prophylaxis:**
  - **who:** prior SBP, **ascitic protein <1 g/dL**, or **active GI bleeding**;
  - **what:** oral **ciprofloxacin** or norfloxacin daily, or IV ceftriaxone in hospital.

## Other complications
- **Hepatic hydrothorax:**
  - serum-to-pleural albumin gradient ≥1.1;
  - **treatment:** sodium restriction, diuretics, thoracentesis, TIPS. **Avoid chest tubes.**
- Hernias; hepatorenal syndrome.
