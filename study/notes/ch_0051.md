# Ch 51 — Gastrointestinal Bleeding (high-yield)

## Core facts
- **Overt bleeding:**
  - **hematemesis** (red blood or coffee grounds);
  - **melena** (blood in the gut **≥14 h**, up to 3–5 days);
  - **hematochezia**.
- **Occult bleeding:** iron deficiency, positive FOBT, symptoms of anemia.
- **Sites:** upper; lower (colon); small intestine; obscure.
- **GIB is the most common GI reason for hospitalization** (~2% fatality). **Deaths come from decompensation of comorbidities**, not exsanguination.

## Initial assessment
- **Heart rate and BP are the best first assessment.** The sequence is postural changes → tachycardia → supine hypotension.
- **Hemoglobin doesn't fall right away** ("people bleed whole blood"). Equilibration takes **up to 72 h**.
- **Transfuse at Hb <7 g/dL** (restrictive). This lowered rebleeding and death compared with a threshold of 9.
- **Chronic slow bleeding:** very low Hb with normal vitals; **low MCV, high RDW**.
- **Upper vs. lower:**
  - **Hematemesis = upper.**
  - **Melena:** usually upper; the more proximal, the more likely.
  - **Hematochezia from an upper source** = brisk bleeding **with instability and falling Hb**.
  - **↑ BUN** and hyperactive bowel sounds suggest upper.
  - **NG aspirate:** nonbloody in ~15% of upper bleeds with serious hematochezia. "Bile-stained" is wrong ~50% of the time. **Don't occult-test the aspirate.**

## Glasgow-Blatchford score (Table 51-1; range 0–23)
| Item | Points |
|---|---|
| BUN 18.2–22.3 / 22.4–27.9 / 28–69.9 / ≥70 mg/dL | 2 / 3 / 4 / 6 |
| Hb men 12–12.9 (women 10–11.9) / men 10–11.9 / <10 | 1 / 3 / 6 |
| SBP 100–109 / 90–99 / <90 | 1 / 2 / 3 |
| HR ≥100; melena | 1 each |
| Syncope; hepatic disease; cardiac failure | 2 each |

**Score 0–1 → outpatient management from the ED** (only ~1% need intervention or die).

## Upper GI bleeding
| Source | Share of UGIB admissions | Key management |
|---|---|---|
| **Peptic ulcer** | **~50%** | Depends on stigmata (Fig 51-1) |
| **Erosions** (esophagitis, gastritis; NSAIDs) | Up to ~30% | Mild; no arteries or veins in the mucosa |
| **Varices** | 2–40% | Worst outcomes |
| **Mallory-Weiss tear** | 2–10% | Retching then hematemesis; **stops spontaneously in 80–90%** |

**Ulcer stigmata (Fig 51-1):**
| Finding | Endoscopic therapy | PPI | Diet | Stay |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Active bleeding or visible vessel** (~20%; one-third rebleed without treatment) | **Yes** | **High-dose** (80 mg IV bolus + 8 mg/h, or intermittent 40 mg 2–4×/day) for 3 d | Clear liquids ~1 d | ~3 d |
| **Adherent clot** | May consider | High-dose | Clear liquids ~1 d | ~3 d |
| Flat pigmented spot | No | Once daily | Clear liquids ~2 d | Discharge after endoscopy |
| **Clean base** (rebleeding ~0) | No | Once daily | Regular | **Discharge after endoscopy** |

- **Endoscopic methods:** bipolar or heater probe, injection (epinephrine 1:10,000, alcohol), clips, hemostatic powder.
- **Continuous vs. intermittent high-dose PPI:** equivalent.

**Preventing ulcer rebleeding** (10–50% within 1 year without prevention):
- **Eradicate *H. pylori*** → rebleeding <5%.
- **NSAIDs:** stop. If an NSAID is essential, use a **COX-2 inhibitor + PPI**.
- **Aspirin:**
  - **For secondary prevention (established CVD): don't stop it**, or restart once hemostasis is confirmed. 8-week mortality was **1% vs. 13%** with immediate reinstitution.
  - **For primary prevention: stop it.**
- **Ulcer not due to *H. pylori* or NSAIDs → PPI indefinitely** (42% rebleed at 7 years).

**Varices:**
- **Cirrhosis with UGIB at presentation:** **antibiotic (ceftriaxone) + IV vasoactive drug (octreotide)**. Antibiotics reduce infection, rebleeding and **mortality**.
- **Endoscopic band ligation + vasoactive drug for 2–5 days** (octreotide 50 µg bolus, then 50 µg/h). The combination beats either alone.
- **Long term:** **nonselective β-blocker + ligation**.
- **TIPS:**
  - persistent or recurrent bleeding despite therapy;
  - **early TIPS** for Child-Pugh B (score 8–9) with active bleeding, or Child C (10–13). This reduces rebleeding and mortality.
- **Gastric varices:** cyanoacrylate glue, TIPS, or BRTO.

**Stress ulcer prophylaxis:**
- **PPI** reduces clinically important bleeding more than H₂ blockers or nothing, **without raising mortality, pneumonia or C. difficile**.
- **Indicated if bleeding risk is ≥4%:**
  - **ventilated without enteral nutrition**;
  - portal hypertension or cirrhosis;
  - **platelets <50k**;
  - **INR >1.5**;
  - or ≥2 of: ventilated with enteral feeding, AKI, sepsis, shock.

**Other upper sources:**
- **Dieulafoy lesion:** an aberrant vessel bleeding through a pinpoint defect.
- **GAVE**, HHT.
- **Aortoenteric fistula.**
- **Hemobilia**, hemosuccus pancreaticus.

**Pre-endoscopy:**
- PPI infusion may be considered. It **reduces high-risk stigmata but not rebleeding or death**.
- **IV erythromycin 250 mg 30–90 min before** endoscopy improves visualization.
- **Endoscopy within 24 h** for most patients. **Urgent (<6–12 h) doesn't help even high-risk patients.**
- **Early endoscopy in low-risk patients** allows discharge in ≥40%.

## Lower GI bleeding
- **Most common:** hemorrhoids. Excluding anal causes → **diverticulosis**.
- **Others:**
  - **vascular ectasias** (proximal colon, age >70);
  - cancer;
  - colitis (ischemic, infectious, IBD, NSAID);
  - **postpolypectomy**;
  - radiation proctopathy.
- **Children:** IBD and **juvenile polyps** (colon); **Meckel diverticulum** (small bowel).
- **Diverticular bleeding:**
  - **abrupt, painless, sometimes massive, often right colon**; not occult;
  - **stops spontaneously in ≥90%**; rebleeding ~15% over 4–5 years;
  - **angiographic superselective embolization** if found;
  - **segmental resection** if refractory.
- **Vascular ectasias:** chronic, often occult. **Heyde syndrome** (with aortic stenosis) → **aortic valve replacement helps**.

**Algorithm (Fig 51-2):**
1. **Hematochezia + instability → upper endoscopy first.**
2. **Colonoscopy after oral lavage prep = procedure of choice** for most admitted patients.
3. **Too unstable to prep → CT angiography.** If it shows extravasation → angiography with embolization; if not → colonoscopy.
4. Persistent bleeding → angiography or surgery.
5. **Age <35, minor bleeding**, normal vitals, Hb and iron, no family history → **flexible sigmoidoscopy** may suffice.

## Small bowel and obscure bleeding
- **~75% of "obscure" bleeding is small bowel.**
- **Adult causes:** **vascular ectasias**, tumors (GIST, carcinoid, lymphoma), NSAID ulcers. **Children: Meckel.**
- **Workup:**
  - **Severe bleeding → CT angiography or angiography.**
  - **Otherwise → capsule endoscopy** (yield ~55%). Repeat EGD or colonoscopy finds a source in up to ~25%.
  - **Capsule negative + stable → observe + iron.** If bleeding recurs → repeat capsule or **CT enterography** (better for masses; use it first if a **stricture** is suspected).
  - **Deep enteroscopy** (balloon or spiral) for biopsy and therapy.
  - **Meckel scan** (⁹⁹ᵐTc-pertechnetate) in young patients.
  - **Intraoperative endoscopy** if everything is negative and transfusion-dependent.
- **Small bowel ectasias:**
  - endoscopic therapy first; **rebleeding ~45%**;
  - then **thalidomide** (RCT) or **monthly octreotide LAR**.

## Positive FOBT
- Screening from **age 45**. **Positive → colonoscopy.**
- **Negative colon → no further workup** unless iron deficiency or GI symptoms.

## Next best step
| Scenario | Next step |
|---|---|
| Melena, GBS 0 | Outpatient management |
| Hb 7.8, stable UGIB, no CAD | No transfusion (threshold <7) |
| Ulcer with visible vessel | Endoscopic therapy + high-dose PPI × 3 d |
| Clean-based ulcer, stable | Oral PPI, regular diet, discharge |
| Cirrhotic with hematemesis | Ceftriaxone + octreotide now, then banding within 24 h |
| Child C (11) actively bleeding varices | Early TIPS |
| Bleeding ulcer on aspirin for prior MI | Continue or restart aspirin when hemostasis is secure |
| Bleeding ulcer, *H. pylori*+ | Eradicate H. pylori |
| Hematochezia with shock | Upper endoscopy first |
| Brisk hematochezia, too unstable for prep | CT angiography → embolization |
| Negative EGD and colonoscopy, ongoing iron deficiency | Capsule endoscopy |
| AS + recurrent ectasia bleeding | Consider aortic valve replacement |
| ICU, ventilated, no tube feeds | PPI stress prophylaxis |
