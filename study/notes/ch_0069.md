# Ch 69 — Bleeding and Thrombosis (high-yield)

## Hemostasis basics
- **Primary hemostasis (platelet plug):** platelets adhere via **VWF** (to GpIb-IX-V), then aggregate via **GpIIb/IIIa** with fibrinogen.
- **Secondary hemostasis:** fibrin clot formation. **Fibrinolysis** remodels the clot.
- **Vitamin K–dependent proteins:** **factors II, VII, IX, X and proteins C, S, Z**.
- **Liver failure → combined factor deficiencies** plus low platelets and portal hypertension.

## Bleeding history (the best predictor of bleeding risk)
- **Mucosal bleeding** (epistaxis, gums, heavy menses, bleeding after dental cleaning) → **platelet disorders or VWD** (primary hemostasis).
- **Spontaneous hemarthroses or muscle hematomas** → **moderate–severe factor VIII or IX deficiency** (also severe fibrinogen, II, V, VII, X, or severe VWD). Also seen in **acquired factor VIII inhibitor**.
- **ISTH bleeding assessment tool:**
  - most useful for **excluding** a bleeding disorder;
  - recommended for VWD screening in primary care.
- **Heavy menstrual bleeding = >80 mL per cycle.**
  - **Predictors:** iron deficiency or transfusion, **clots >1 inch**, changing pads **more than hourly**.
  - **Onset at menarche** suggests an underlying disorder (VWD, factor XI deficiency, platelet defects, hemophilia carriers).
- **Postpartum hemorrhage** may be **delayed** in type 1 VWD or hemophilia A carriers (levels normalize in pregnancy and then fall).
- **Delayed bleeding about 7 days after tonsillectomy or polypectomy** (eschar loss).
- **Epistaxis** suggesting a disorder: not seasonal, needs medical care or cautery. It's the most common symptom of **HHT** and of VWD in boys.
- **GI bleeding or hematuria:** look for a lesion even with a known disorder. **VWD types 2 and 3 → GI angiodysplasia.**
- **Non-coagulopathic bruising:** Ehlers-Danlos, **Cushing or steroids, senile purpura**.
- **Leading cause of bleeding death in severe factor deficiency: CNS bleeding.**

## Drugs and supplements
- **Aspirin:** irreversible, but function recovers **2–3 days** after the last dose. Other NSAIDs are reversible.
  - Can **unmask mild VWD**.
- **P2Y12 inhibitors** (clopidogrel, prasugrel, ticagrelor): **higher bleeding risk than NSAIDs**.
- **Fish oil and omega-3** impair platelet function. **Stop new supplements in unexplained bleeding.**
- **Systemic contributors:** liver disease, **uremia**, hypothyroidism, paraproteins or amyloid, marrow failure.

## Platelets
- **Normal 150–450k.**
- **Bleeding risk:**
  - rarely bleeds above **50k**;
  - spontaneous bleeding usually only **below 10–20k**;
  - **most procedures are OK at ≥50k**.
- Risk is higher with coexisting coagulopathy, infection or antiplatelet drugs.

## Thrombosis risk (Table 69-2)
- **Venous only:**
  - **inherited:** factor V Leiden, prothrombin G20210A, antithrombin, protein C and S deficiency;
  - **acquired:** age, prior VTE, immobility, surgery, pregnancy or puerperium, hospitalization, obesity, infection, smoking.
- **Venous and arterial:**
  - **antiphospholipid syndrome**, **malignancy**, hormonal therapy;
  - **PV, ET, PNH**;
  - **TTP, HIT, DIC**;
  - **homocystinuria**, dysfibrinogenemia.
- **Arterial:** atherosclerosis is the main risk factor.
- **Key history question: was the VTE idiopathic (unprovoked) or provoked?** Unprovoked is the **strongest predictor of recurrence** (without cancer).
- **DVT risk climbs with age:** 1/100,000 per year in early childhood → **1/200 per year in octogenarians**.
- **Thrombophilia testing in adults only when results would change care (rare):**
  - strong family history;
  - **in steady state, ≥3 weeks after stopping anticoagulation** (after 3–6 months of treatment);
  - **acute thrombosis and heparin lower antithrombin**; **warfarin lowers proteins C and S**; DOACs interfere with functional assays;
  - **APL antibodies are often transiently positive in acute illness**;
  - genetic assays aren't affected by anticoagulants.

## Laboratory evaluation
- **Labs complement but never replace history.** **Bleeding time doesn't predict bleeding**, so it isn't recommended. Thromboelastography is used intraoperatively.
- **Preoperative testing:** PT may reveal liver disease or vitamin K deficiency. **aPTT isn't useful with a negative bleeding history.**
- **Sample artifacts:**
  - **underfilled tubes (<90%)**;
  - **Hct >55%** (less plasma for the citrate) → falsely prolonged times;
  - repeat abnormal tests in people who don't bleed.
- **PT:** factors **I, II, V, VII, X**.
  - **INR** was designed for **warfarin**. In liver disease it only roughly predicts bleeding and **overestimates risk** when mildly elevated.
  - **PT is often normal on apixaban.**
- **aPTT:** XII, XI, IX, VIII, X, V, II, fibrinogen, prekallikrein, HMWK. Prolonged when a factor falls to ≤30–50%.

## Table 69-3 — test patterns
| Pattern | Causes |
|---|---|
| **Isolated ↑ aPTT** | **No bleeding: XII, prekallikrein, HMWK.** Severe bleeding: **VIII, IX**. Mild: XI. **Heparin**, direct thrombin inhibitors |
| **Isolated ↑ PT** | **Factor VII deficiency, early vitamin K deficiency, warfarin** |
| **↑ PT and aPTT** | II, V, X, fibrinogen deficiency; **late vitamin K deficiency**; direct thrombin inhibitors; Xa inhibitors (PT may be normal) |
| **↑ Thrombin time** | **Heparin, dabigatran**, dysfibrinogenemia, afibrinogenemia, fibrin degradation products |
| **No correction with mixing** | **Bleeding → specific inhibitor (acquired VIII)**; **no bleeding, clotting or pregnancy loss → lupus anticoagulant**; heparin; DIC |
| Abnormal clot solubility | **Factor XIII deficiency** (normal PT and aPTT) |
| Rapid clot lysis | α₂-antiplasmin or PAI-1 deficiency; fibrinolytics |

- **Mixing study (1:1 with normal plasma):**
  - **factor deficiency → corrects and stays corrected**;
  - **lupus anticoagulant → doesn't correct**;
  - **acquired factor VIII inhibitor → corrects initially, then prolongs after 37°C incubation**.
- **Lupus anticoagulant confirmation:** prolonged phospholipid-dependent test (LA-PTT, **dilute Russell viper venom time**) + no correction on mixing + **correction with added phospholipid**.
- **Thrombin time vs. reptilase time:** both prolonged with low fibrinogen (<80–100) or dysfibrinogenemia. **Heparin prolongs the thrombin time only.**
- **Anti-Xa assays:** measure LMWH, UFH, rivaroxaban, apixaban, edoxaban levels. **Dilute thrombin time** for dabigatran.
- **Platelet function:**
  - PFA-100 is more sensitive than the bleeding time but **can miss mild VWD**; abnormal results need VWF assays or aggregation;
  - **platelet aggregometry = gold standard** (affected by many drugs).

## Next best step
| Scenario | Next step |
|---|---|
| Heavy menses since menarche, gum bleeding, bleeding after extraction | VWF testing (antigen, activity, factor VIII) |
| Preoperative patient with no bleeding history | No routine aPTT |
| Isolated prolonged aPTT, no bleeding history ever | Factor XII, prekallikrein or HMWK deficiency — not a bleeding risk |
| Isolated prolonged aPTT, recurrent miscarriage, no correction on mixing | Lupus anticoagulant: dRVVT ± phospholipid correction; APL antibodies |
| New extensive bruising in an older adult, prolonged aPTT, corrects then prolongs on incubation | Acquired factor VIII inhibitor |
| Isolated prolonged PT, poor oral intake on antibiotics | Early vitamin K deficiency |
| Prolonged thrombin time, normal reptilase time | Heparin contamination |
| Bleeding history, normal PT, aPTT and platelets | Consider factor XIII (clot solubility), VWF assays, platelet aggregation |
| Unprovoked DVT, considering thrombophilia testing | Test only if it changes management; ≥3 weeks off anticoagulation |
| Platelets 60k, planned procedure | Generally safe (≥50k) |
| Unexplained bruising, new fish oil or other supplements | Stop supplements |
