# Ch 15 — Chest Discomfort (high-yield)

## Big picture
- 6–7 million ED visits per year. **<15% are ACS** (10–20%). **GI causes are the most common** (Fig 15-1: GI 42%, ischemic heart disease 31%, chest wall 28%, pericarditis 4%, pleuritis 2%, PE 2%).
- Historically **2–6% of discharged "noncardiac" chest pain was missed MI**.
- Triage order (Table 15-2):
  1. Acute life-threatening: unstable ischemia, **aortic dissection, pneumothorax, PE**
  2. Chronic but dangerous: stable angina, aortic stenosis, pulmonary hypertension
  3. Acute and treatable: pericarditis, pneumonia/pleuritis, zoster
  4. Other chronic: GERD, spasm, PUD, gallbladder, MSK, anxiety
- **Esophageal rupture** is the most urgent noncardiopulmonary cause.

## Most likely diagnosis (Table 15-1)
| Clue | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| Pressure/squeezing retrosternal pain → neck/jaw/arms, exertional, 2–10 min, relieved by rest | Stable angina |
| Same pain at rest or with less effort | Unstable angina |
| Same pain >30 min, not relieved by rest | MI |
| Sharp pleuritic pain, worse supine, **better sitting forward**, radiates to **trapezius ridge**, friction rub | Pericarditis |
| **Sudden, tearing** pain → back between the scapulae; HTN, Marfan/EDS, bicuspid valve, pregnancy; **AI murmur, pulse deficit** | Acute aortic syndrome |
| Sudden pleuritic lateral pain + dyspnea, tachycardia; massive PE → substernal pain, syncope, hypotension | PE |
| **Tall thin young man** (or COPD/CF), sudden pleuritic pain, ↓ breath sounds | Pneumothorax |
| Burning pain, worse lying down after meals, relieved by antacids | GERD |
| Squeezing pain identical to angina, **relieved by nitroglycerin or a CCB** | Esophageal spasm |
| Pain after forceful vomiting | Esophageal rupture (Boerhaave) or Mallory-Weiss tear |
| Epigastric burning 60–90 min after meals, relieved by food/antacids | PUD |
| Localized sternal tenderness reproduced by pressure | Costochondritis (Tietze) |
| Dermatomal burning ± vesicles | Herpes zoster |
| Abrupt chest pain after emotional stress, ST elevation, ↑ troponin | Takotsubo |
| Tightness, dyspnea, sense of doom, panic history (~10% of ED chest pain) | Panic disorder |

## History features: likelihood ratio for MI (Fig 15-2)
- **↑ likelihood:** radiation to the right arm/shoulder or **both arms** (strongest), exertional, left-arm radiation, diaphoresis, nausea/vomiting, worse than or like prior angina/MI, "pressure."
- **↓ likelihood:** **pleuritic, positional, sharp, reproducible with palpation**, inframammary.
- Other rules:
  - Pain localized to a fingertip, lasting seconds, or constant for days without ECG or troponin change → rarely ischemic.
  - Pain only above the jaw or below the epigastrium → rarely angina.
  - Peak intensity at onset → dissection, PE, pneumothorax.
- **Nitroglycerin response isn't diagnostic** (spasm responds too). Relief taking >10 min → not ischemia, or severe MI.
- **Chest-wall tenderness doesn't exclude ischemia.** A normal exam doesn't reassure.
- Women and the elderly: anginal equivalents (dyspnea, fatigue, nausea, faintness). Nausea is more common with **inferior MI** (Bezold-Jarisch reflex).

## Exam clues
- Hypotension + tachycardia → MI with shock, massive PE, tamponade, tension pneumothorax.
- Dissection is usually **hypertensive**; hypotension suggests coronary or pericardial involvement.
- New MR or VSD murmur → mechanical complication of STEMI. AI murmur → ascending dissection.
- Unilateral leg swelling → VTE. Upper-limb pulse loss → dissection.

## Next best step
| Situation | Next step |
|---|---|
| Any acute chest pain in the ED | **12-lead ECG within 10 min** |
| Nondiagnostic ECG, ongoing suspicion | **Serial ECGs q30–60 min**; consider right-sided leads |
| Suspected ACS | **High-sensitivity troponin** at arrival and repeat at **1–3 h** (conventional assay: 3–6 h) |
| Symptom onset >2–3 h before arrival, very low hs-troponin | MI excluded (NPV >99%) |
| Low risk by pathway (e.g., **HEART ≤3**) + negative serial hs-troponin | **Discharge**; stress testing hasn't been shown to improve outcomes |
| Intermediate risk, **no known CAD** | **Coronary CT angiography** (high NPV; also excludes dissection, PE, effusion) |
| Intermediate risk, **known obstructive CAD** | **Stress imaging** (nuclear or echo); exercise preferred over pharmacologic |
| Suspected dissection | **CT** (or TEE). **TTE is poorly sensitive**; MRI is accurate but rarely first |
| Suspected pneumothorax or pneumonia | Chest x-ray |
| Clinic patient worrying enough to want a troponin | **Send to the ED** |
| Troponin in a patient without suspected ACS | Don't order (except for risk stratification in PE or HF) |

## ECG pearls
- ST elevation → immediate reperfusion candidate.
- ST depression or symmetric T inversion ≥0.2 mV → ischemia, higher risk.
- **Pericarditis:** diffuse ST elevation not matching one coronary territory + **PR depression**.
- **PE:** sinus tachycardia (most common); S1Q3T3 / right axis shift.
- Resting ECG sensitivity for ischemia can be as low as **20%**. Hyperventilation can cause nonspecific ST/T changes.

## Chest x-ray pearls
Wide mediastinum (dissection); **Hampton hump / Westermark sign** (PE); pericardial calcification (constrictive pericarditis); often normal in ACS.

## Troponin classification (Fig 15-3)
Elevated cTn → **stable** = chronic myocardial injury (e.g., ESRD, structural heart disease). **Rising/falling** = acute injury → with ischemia = **MI** (type 1 = plaque rupture/thrombosis; type 2 = supply-demand mismatch); without ischemia = acute nonischemic injury (myocarditis). **MI requires a rise/fall with ≥1 value >99th percentile, caused by ischemia.**

## Decision aids (Fig 15-4)
- **HEART (without troponin):** History, ECG, Age (≥65 = 2; 45–64 = 1), Risk factors (≥3 = 2). **Low risk 0–3**, combined with troponin below the limit of quantification.
- **EDACS:** low risk <16.
- Both NPV ≈99.5%. These are **diagnostic** pathways. **TIMI/GRACE are prognostic scores for established ACS**, not diagnostic.

## Classic traps
- Relief with GI cocktail or nitroglycerin → doesn't settle the diagnosis.
- Reproducible tenderness → doesn't rule out MI.
- Ordering a stress test for every low-risk patient with negative troponins → no outcome benefit.
- TTE to rule out dissection → wrong test.
