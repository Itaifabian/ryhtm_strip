# Ch 44 — Approach to the Patient with a Heart Murmur (high-yield)

## Basics
- **Timing first.** Systolic: S1 to S2. Diastolic: S2 to S1. **Continuous:** through S2 into diastole. In tachycardia, time with the **carotid upstroke** (it follows S1).
- **Intensity grades:**
  - 1: very soft, heard only with effort
  - 2: easily heard
  - **3: loud, no thrill**
  - **4: loud with a palpable thrill**
  - 5: heard with the stethoscope edge
  - 6: heard with the stethoscope off the chest
- **Grade ≥3 usually means structural disease.**
- **Murmurs are softer with:** obesity, COPD, effusion, low cardiac output.
- **Loudness tracks velocity, not size:** a small restrictive VSD is loud (grade ≥4); ASD flow itself is silent.
- **Configuration:**
  - AS: crescendo-decrescendo
  - Chronic MR: plateau, holosystolic
  - Chronic AR: decrescendo
  - MS: low-pitched rumble (use the bell)

## Systolic murmurs (Table 44-1)
| Timing | Causes and key features |
|---|---|
| **Early** | **Acute severe MR** (decrescendo, noncompliant LA): papillary muscle rupture, chordal rupture, endocarditis, trauma. **Small muscular VSD** (grade 4–5, LSB). **Large VSD with pulmonary hypertension** (murmur shortens, loud P₂). **TR with normal PA pressure** (endocarditis; soft, LLSB, **Carvallo sign**) |
| **Mid (ejection)** | **AS** (most common in adults). **HOCM**. Pulmonic stenosis. **ASD** (flow murmur + **fixed split S₂**). Flow murmurs (pregnancy, anemia, hyperthyroidism). **Aortic sclerosis**. **Still murmur** (children) |
| **Late** | **MVP** (click then murmur). Acute ischemia (tethering) |
| **Holosystolic** | **Chronic MR** (apex → axilla). **TR** (LLSB, ↑ with inspiration, c-v waves). **VSD** (mid–lower LSB, **thrill**, no respiratory change) |

**Post-MI murmurs:**
- **Papillary muscle rupture:**
  - **2–7 days** after an inferior, posterior or lateral MI;
  - **posteromedial muscle 6–10× more often** than anterolateral;
  - **the murmur is absent in up to 50%**;
  - pulmonary edema, hypotension; tall **v wave** on wedge tracing.
- **VSR:** holosystolic with a **thrill at the LSB in nearly all**; O₂ step-up on right heart catheterization.
- **Any new post-MI murmur → TTE.** Then stabilize and refer urgently for surgery.

**Aortic stenosis:**
- Murmur at the 2nd right ICS radiating to the carotids. **Gallavardin effect:** higher-pitched at the apex.
- **Severe AS signs:**
  - **soft or absent A₂, paradoxical split S₂**, S₄;
  - **late-peaking** murmur;
  - **parvus et tardus** carotid pulse (less reliable in the elderly);
  - with normal output: a thrill or grade ≥4.
- **Murmur is softer in HF or low output.**
- **Ejection click in a young patient** = mobile **bicuspid valve** (valvular level).
- **Gets louder in the beat after a PVC** (MR doesn't).

**HOCM:**
- LLSB to apex, usually ≤ grade 3. **Brisk carotid upstroke** (occasionally bisferiens). LVH on ECG; **TTE confirms**.
- **Louder with:** Valsalva, standing, vasodilators, inotropes.
- **Softer with:** squatting, leg raise, volume, vasopressors, **β-blockers**.

**MVP:**
- Midsystolic click + late murmur.
- **Standing or Valsalva → click earlier, murmur longer and louder. Squatting → later, shorter, softer** (Fig 44-3).
- Same direction as HOCM. Distinguish them by **LVH in HOCM vs. the click in MVP**.
- **Radiation:**
  - posterior leaflet → anterior jet radiating to the **base (mimics AS)**;
  - anterior leaflet → **axilla or back**.
- **S₃ or a diastolic rumble = severe MR.**

**ASD:**
- **Secundum** is most common in adults.
- **Primum:** cleft mitral leaflet (MR) + **left axis deviation**.
- **Sinus venosus:** sinus node abnormalities.

**Benign murmur:** grade 1–2 midsystolic, asymptomatic, otherwise normal → **no TTE**. Aortic sclerosis: normal carotids, no LVH.

## Diastolic murmurs
- **Chronic AR:**
  - high-pitched, blowing, decrescendo;
  - heard best **leaning forward at end expiration**;
  - **LSB radiation = valve disease** (bicuspid, endocarditis); **RSB = aortic root disease** (Marfan, ectasia, ankylosing spondylitis, dissection).
  - **Peripheral signs:** wide pulse pressure, **Corrigan (water-hammer) pulse**, **Quincke** nail-bed pulsations.
  - **Austin Flint:** apical mid–late rumble with no opening snap. It **softens with amyl nitrite** (the MS rumble gets louder).
- **Acute AR:** short, low-pitched, **no peripheral signs**, soft S₁ (early mitral closure).
- **PR (Graham Steell):**
  - LUSB, ↑ with inspiration, with signs of pulmonary hypertension;
  - also common after **tetralogy of Fallot repair**.
- **MS:**
  - **usually rheumatic**;
  - loud S₁, **opening snap**, low rumble at the apex in the **left lateral decubitus** position;
  - **shorter A₂ (or P₂)-to-OS interval = more severe**;
  - **presystolic accentuation is lost in AF**;
  - louder with exercise.
- **Other mid-diastolic causes:**
  - **Atrial myxoma:** positional, no opening snap.
  - Flow rumble: severe MR, VSD, ASD (tricuspid).
  - **Carey-Coombs:** acute rheumatic fever.
  - Complete heart block.
  - **Tricuspid stenosis:** LLSB, ↑ with inspiration.

## Continuous murmurs
- **PDA:** lateral to the upper left sternal border.
  - Large PDA → Eisenmenger with **differential cyanosis of the legs**.
- **Ruptured sinus of Valsalva aneurysm:** abrupt, upper RSB.
- **Coronary AV fistula**, dialysis fistula.
- **Coarctation:** collateral murmurs along the ribs.
- **To-and-fro (systolic + diastolic) carotid bruit:** high-grade carotid stenosis.
- **Benign:**
  - **venous hum:** right supraclavicular, abolished by jugular pressure or turning the head;
  - **mammary souffle:** late pregnancy and postpartum.

## Dynamic auscultation (Table 44-2)
| Maneuver | Effect |
|---|---|
| Inspiration | **Right-sided murmurs louder** (TR, PR, TS) |
| Handgrip, transient arterial occlusion, vasopressor (↑ afterload) | **MR, VSD, AR louder**; AS and HOCM softer or unchanged |
| Amyl nitrite (↓ afterload) | **AS and HOCM louder**; MR, VSD and the Austin Flint murmur softer; MS louder |
| Valsalva, standing (↓ preload) | Most murmurs softer; **HOCM and MVP louder** |
| Squatting, leg raise | HOCM and MVP softer |
| Beat after a PVC | **AS louder; MR unchanged** |

**S₂ splitting:**
- **Reversed:** severe AS, HOCM, **LBBB**, RV pacing, ischemia.
- **Fixed:** ASD.
- **Wide physiologic:** RBBB, PS, severe MR.
- **Narrow with a loud P₂:** pulmonary hypertension.

## Echo indications
- **TTE indicated:**
  - early, late or holosystolic murmurs;
  - **midsystolic grade ≥3**;
  - grade 1–2 **with** symptoms or signs (including ECG or CXR findings);
  - **any diastolic murmur**;
  - continuous murmurs other than a venous hum or mammary souffle.
- **Not indicated:** asymptomatic grade 1–2 midsystolic murmur with no other findings.
- **Trace regurgitation on Doppler** in normal valves is common and inaudible.
- **Beyond TTE:**
  - **TEE** for poor windows.
  - **CMR** quantifies regurgitation and has largely replaced catheterization when clinical and echo findings disagree.
  - CMR or CT counts aortic leaflets.
  - **Coronary CTA** before valve surgery when CAD probability is low.

| Scenario | Next step |
|---|---|
| New murmur day 4 after inferior MI, pulmonary edema | Urgent TTE, then surgery |
| Asymptomatic young adult, grade 2 midsystolic, normal exam | No testing |
| Grade 3 midsystolic | TTE |
| Any diastolic murmur | TTE |
| Systolic murmur louder with Valsalva, with LVH | TTE for HOCM; avoid volume depletion and vasodilators |

| Continuous murmur abolished by jugular pressure | Reassure (venous hum) |
