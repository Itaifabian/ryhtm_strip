# Ch 6 — Screening and Prevention of Disease (high-yield)

## Core principles
- Screening targets **asymptomatic** people → needs a higher evidence bar than treatment.
- Screen when: disease is common/burdensome, has a **detectable preclinical phase**, and early treatment changes outcome (e.g., polypectomy prevents colon cancer). Diagnosis/treatment facilities must exist.
- Poor screening targets: no effective early therapy (Alzheimer disease), or indolent disease where competing mortality dominates (much prostate cancer).
- Gold-standard evidence = **RCT with mortality (or incidence) endpoint**, *not* survival after diagnosis.

## Biases (classic exam trap)
| Bias | What happens | Why survival looks better |
|---|---|---|
| **Lead-time** | Diagnosis moved earlier; death date unchanged | Clock starts sooner |
| **Length-time** | Screening preferentially catches slow-growing disease | Screened group enriched with indolent cases |
| **Overdiagnosis** | Detects disease that would never present clinically | ~15–40% of mammography-detected breast cancers, ~15–37% of PSA-detected prostate cancers |
→ Fix: compare **disease-specific mortality**, not survival time.

## Measuring benefit
- Absolute vs relative: mammography ~14–32% relative reduction in breast cancer death but only ~1.2/1000 fewer deaths over 12 y; annual FOBT ~30% relative reduction, ≥3/1000 fewer colon cancer deaths over 13 y.
- **NNS = 1 / absolute risk reduction** (e.g., 731 women aged 65–69 screened with DEXA + treated to prevent one hip fracture).
- Life-expectancy gain is a population average: Pap smears add nothing for the 98% who never get cervical cancer, up to ~25 y for the 2% who would.
- Quitting smoking at 35: +3–5 y — the single biggest preventive gain.
- Cost-effective threshold ≈ **$50,000–100,000 per QALY**.

## Harms
False positives → biopsy/follow-up; overdiagnosis; anxiety (rarely long-term); cumulative radiation (mammography, CT); procedure discomfort; downstream "incidentalomas."

## Chemoprevention [verify]
- Aspirin: age 40–59 with ≥10% 10-y CVD risk; bleeding risk may cancel benefit.
- Folic acid: anyone planning or capable of pregnancy (neural tube defects).
- Tamoxifen/raloxifene: women at high breast cancer risk.
- Vitamin D: >64 at increased fall risk.
- Vaccines: influenza yearly (>18); zoster (>60 in this table); pneumococcal >64; HPV up to 27 if not done; MMR for susceptible women of childbearing age; varicella 2 doses if susceptible.

## Risk-adapted screening
- **Family history of colon cancer → start 10 y before the youngest affected relative's age at diagnosis.**
- BRCA1/2 (5–20× breast/ovarian risk): genetic counseling, **breast MRI**, prophylactic oophorectomy ± mastectomy — interventions too harmful for average-risk women.
- Coronary CT calcium can break ties on statin decisions when risk is uncertain.

## Shared decision-making
Use it when the benefit/harm balance is uncertain: **PSA screening** (individualized discussion), **choice of CRC test** (similar lives saved per model; FOBT 15–30%, flex sig ~40–60%, colonoscopy ~50–70% mortality reduction).

## When to stop
Benefit usually accrues after 5–10 y → few data supporting most screening past **75**; with advanced disease/limited life expectancy, shift focus to interventions that affect quality and length of life.

## From the tables (added after table extraction fix)
**WHO screening principles (Table 6-1):** an important health problem; an accepted treatment; facilities to diagnose and treat; a **latent/preclinical stage**; a suitable test that is acceptable to the population; natural history understood; an agreed policy on whom to treat; case-finding cost balanced against overall spending.

**Lifetime risk in an unscreened population (Table 6-2):** breast cancer (women) 10% · hip fracture (white women) 16% · domestic violence (women) up to 15% · colon cancer 6% · cervical cancer 2%.

**Average life-expectancy gain (Table 6-3):** quitting smoking at 35 → **3–5 y** · regular exercise for a 40-year-old man → **9 mo–2 y** · Pap smears (18–65) → 2–3 mo · mammography at 50–70 → 1 mo · mammography at 40–50 → 0–5 days.

| Age | Top causes | High-yield prevention |
|---|---|---|
| 15–24 | **Accident**, homicide, suicide, malignancy, heart disease | Seat belt/helmet counseling, alcohol and driving or swimming, ask about guns, screen for depression/suicidality and domestic violence, STI screening, HPV and other vaccines |

## Classic traps
- "Screened patients survive longer after diagnosis" ≠ screening works (lead/length bias).
- Quitting smoking outranks any screening test.
- AAA screen is for men who **ever** smoked — not never-smokers, not women (per this table).
- A normal Pap at 21 → next in 3 y, not yearly.
