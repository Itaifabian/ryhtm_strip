# Ch 52 — Jaundice (high-yield)

## Recognition
- **Scleral icterus means bilirubin ≥3 mg/dL** (sclera is rich in elastin). It's harder to see under fluorescent light; also check under the tongue.
- **Long-standing jaundice turns green** (biliverdin).
- **Carotenoderma:**
  - palms, soles, forehead, nasolabial folds;
  - **sclerae spared**;
  - from carrots, squash and oranges; also diabetes, hypothyroidism, anorexia.
- **Other yellow skin:** quinacrine, sunitinib, sorafenib, phenols.
- **Dark (tea-colored) urine = conjugated bilirubin.** It means hepatobiliary disease.

## Bilirubin metabolism
- **Source:** ~80–85% comes from senescent RBCs (~4 mg/kg/day).
- **Steps:**
  1. Heme oxygenase converts heme to biliverdin (releasing CO and iron).
  2. Biliverdin reductase converts biliverdin to bilirubin.
  3. Bilirubin travels **bound to albumin**.
  4. In the hepatocyte, **UDPGT** conjugates it.
  5. **MRP2** secretes it into bile.
- **In the gut:** bacteria make urobilinogen. 10–20% undergoes enterohepatic cycling.
- **Unconjugated bilirubin is never in urine** (it's albumin-bound).
- **Unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia** = direct fraction **<15% of total**.
- **Delta bilirubin:**
  - conjugated bilirubin covalently bound to albumin, so its **half-life is 18–20 days**;
  - explains **slow bilirubin fall during recovery**;
  - explains a **negative urine bilirubin despite conjugated hyperbilirubinemia** in prolonged cholestasis.

## Hepatocellular vs. cholestatic
- **Initial labs (Fig 52-1):** total and direct bilirubin, ALT, AST, ALP, **albumin, PT**.
- **Pattern:**
  - **Hepatocellular:** ALT/AST out of proportion to ALP.
  - **Cholestatic:** ALP out of proportion to ALT/AST.
  - **Bilirubin level doesn't distinguish** the two.
- **Albumin:** low = chronic (cirrhosis, cancer); normal = acute.
- **PT:**
  - prolonged = vitamin K deficiency or hepatocellular dysfunction;
  - **fails to correct with parenteral vitamin K = severe hepatocellular injury**.
- **Aminotransferase clues:**
  - **Alcoholic hepatitis: AST:ALT ≥2:1, AST rarely >300.**
  - **Viral or toxic hepatitis with jaundice:** **>500, ALT ≥ AST**.
  - **≥25× normal = acute hepatocellular** disease. Under 8× can be either pattern.
  - Cirrhosis can have normal aminotransferases.
- **Exam clues:**
  - **Arthralgias or myalgias before jaundice:** viral or drug hepatitis.
  - **Sudden severe RUQ pain + rigors:** choledocholithiasis or **cholangitis**.
  - Muscle wasting: pancreatic cancer or cirrhosis.
  - **Virchow node, Sister Mary Joseph nodule:** abdominal malignancy.
  - **JVD or pulsatile liver:** congestion.
  - **Murphy sign:** cholecystitis.
  - **Ascites + jaundice:** cirrhosis or peritoneal malignancy.

## Hepatocellular causes (Table 52-2)
- **Viral:** HAV, HBV, HCV, HDV, HEV; EBV, CMV, HSV.
- **Alcohol**, cirrhosis.
- **Drugs:** **predictable, dose-dependent (acetaminophen)** vs. **idiosyncratic (isoniazid)**.
- **Toxins:** **vinyl chloride**, **Jamaica bush tea (pyrrolizidine alkaloids)**, kava, ***Amanita* mushrooms**.
- **Wilson disease** (age 3–55; **ceruloplasmin**). **Autoimmune hepatitis** (young or middle-aged women; **ANA, anti-smooth muscle antibody, IgG**).

**Hepatocellular workup:**
- **HAV IgM; HBsAg + anti-HBc IgM; HCV RNA** (antibody can take **6 weeks** to appear); ± HEV IgM;
- acetaminophen level, toxicology;
- **ceruloplasmin if <40**;
- ANA, SMA, SPEP;
- then CMV, EBV, HDV.

## Cholestatic causes (Table 52-3)
- **First test: right upper quadrant ultrasound.**
  - **Dilated ducts → extrahepatic.**
  - Not dilated → intrahepatic.
  - **False negatives:** partial obstruction, cirrhosis, PSC.
- **Extrahepatic, dilated ducts → MRCP** (has replaced ERCP as the initial test), CT, or **EUS** (allows biopsy).
  - **ERCP:** gold standard for stones and allows **therapy** (stone removal, stents).
  - **PTC** if ERCP fails.
- **Extrahepatic causes:**
  - **Choledocholithiasis is the most common** (mild pain up to cholangitis with sepsis).
  - **Malignant:** pancreas, gallbladder, **ampulla (highest surgical cure rate of painless-jaundice tumors)**, **cholangiocarcinoma (associated with PSC)**, porta hepatis nodes.
  - **IgG4 cholangitis** (**steroid-responsive**; must be told apart from PSC).
  - Chronic pancreatitis stricture.
  - **AIDS cholangiopathy:** CMV or Cryptosporidium; **ALP ~800 with near-normal bilirubin**.
- **Intrahepatic causes:**
  - **Viral:** fibrosing cholestatic hepatitis B or C after transplant; HAV, HEV, EBV, CMV.
  - **Drugs:**
    - **anabolic and contraceptive steroids** (pure cholestasis);
    - cholestatic hepatitis: chlorpromazine, erythromycin estolate, TMP-SMX, **amoxicillin-clavulanate**;
    - chronic cholestasis: chlorpromazine, prochlorperazine.
  - **Primary biliary cholangitis:** women; **AMA positive in 95%**.
  - **PSC:**
    - **MRCP shows segmental strictures**;
    - **~75% have IBD**.
  - **Vanishing bile duct syndrome:** chronic rejection, GVHD, sarcoid, chlorpromazine.
  - **BRIC:** episodic pruritus and jaundice, **normal GGT**, no chronic disease. **PFIC:** progressive from childhood; only type 3 has a **high GGT**.
  - **Cholestasis of pregnancy:** 2nd–3rd trimester; resolves after delivery.
  - **ICU:** **sepsis, shock liver, TPN**. **After marrow transplant:** **veno-occlusive disease, GVHD**.
  - **Ischemic hepatitis:** dramatic aminotransferase rise, then a delayed bilirubin peak.
  - **Weil disease** (leptospirosis): jaundice + renal failure + fever + myalgia. **Falciparum malaria.**
  - Paraneoplastic: Hodgkin, RCC, medullary thyroid cancer.
- **Intrahepatic workup:** **AMA**, hepatitis serologies, drug review → MRCP or liver biopsy.
- **Jaundice + encephalopathy + coagulopathy without prior liver disease = acute liver failure** → urgent transplant evaluation.
