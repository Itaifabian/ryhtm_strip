# Ch 39 — Dyspnea (high-yield)

## Core facts
- **Dyspnea is a subjective symptom** and must be self-reported. Tachypnea, accessory muscle use and retractions are signs.
- **Prevalence:** up to half of inpatients, a quarter of outpatients, and up to **37% of adults ≥70**.
- **Dyspnea predicts COPD outcomes better than FEV₁.** The mMRC scale is built into GOLD.
- **Chronic dyspnea** is >1 month. **Pulmonary or cardiac causes account for ~85%**, and about one-third are multifactorial. Up to two-thirds need testing beyond the initial assessment.

## Mechanisms (Fig 39-1)
- **Chemoreceptors:**
  - carotid body and aortic arch (peripheral), medulla (central);
  - triggered by hypoxemia, hypercapnia, acidemia → **air hunger**.
- **Mechanoreceptors:**
  - airways and lungs (stretch, irritant, **J receptors**), chest wall;
  - triggered by ↑ resistance (**chest tightness**: asthma, COPD) or ↓ compliance (fibrosis).
- **Metaboreceptors** in muscle respond to anemia and deconditioning.
- **Efferent–reafferent mismatch:** motor output that doesn't match the afferent feedback.
- **Anxiety** amplifies the sensation.

## mMRC scale (Table 39-1)
| Grade | Breathless when |
|---|---|
| 0 | Only with strenuous exercise |
| 1 | Hurrying on level ground or walking up a slight hill |
| 2 | Walks slower than peers on level ground, or stops at own pace |
| 3 | Stops after **100 m** or a few minutes on level ground |
| 4 | Too breathless to leave the house, or breathless dressing |

## Table 39-2 — differential with first tests
| Process | Clues | Initial tests |
|---|---|---|
| **Airways** (asthma, COPD, upper airway obstruction) | Chest tightness, wheeze, exertional hypoxemia | Peak flow, **spirometry (obstructive)**, CXR hyperinflation; CT and airway exam for upper airway |
| **Parenchymal** (ILD) | **Dry end-inspiratory crackles, clubbing**, exertional hypoxemia | Spirometry and lung volumes (**restrictive**), CXR and CT |
| **Chest wall or neuromuscular** | ↓ diaphragm excursion, atelectasis | Lung volumes (restrictive), **MIP and MEP (low in neuromuscular disease)** |
| **Pulmonary hypertension** | Tachypnea, raised right heart pressures | **↓ DLCO**, ECG, echo, RHC; CTA and V/Q for chronic thromboembolism |
| **Left heart failure, pericardial disease** | Wet crackles, **pulsus paradoxus** (pericardial) | **BNP** (especially acute), ECG, echo, ± stress test or LHC |
| **Other** (anemia, deconditioning, anxiety, metabolic, GERD or aspiration, post-COVID) | Variable | Hematocrit, metabolic panel, thyroid tests, ± endoscopy or pH probe |

## History clues
- **Chest tightness → bronchoconstriction.** **Can't take a deep breath → dynamic hyperinflation (COPD).**
- **Orthopnea:** CHF, obesity-related diaphragm limitation, reflux-triggered asthma.
- **Nocturnal dyspnea:** CHF or asthma.
- **Acute intermittent episodes:** ischemia, bronchospasm, PE. **Chronic persistent:** COPD, ILD, chronic thromboembolic disease.
- **Platypnea** (worse upright, better supine): **left atrial myxoma, hepatopulmonary syndrome**.

## Exam clues
- **Pulsus paradoxus >10 mmHg:** COPD, acute asthma, pericardial disease.
- **Percussion:** dull = effusion; hyperresonant = pneumothorax or emphysema.
- **Inward abdominal motion on inspiration while supine = diaphragmatic weakness.** Abdominal rounding on exhalation suggests pulmonary edema.
- **Clubbing:** ILD or bronchiectasis. Joint changes or Raynaud: collagen vascular disease.
- **Right heart signs:** JVD, edema, loud P₂. **LV dysfunction:** S₃, S₄.
- **Walk with oximetry** to reproduce symptoms and look for exertional desaturation or wheeze.

## CXR clues
- **Hyperinflation:** obstruction. **Low volumes:** fibrosis, edema, diaphragm or chest wall problems.
- **Upper-zone vascular prominence:** pulmonary *venous* hypertension. **Enlarged central PAs:** pulmonary *arterial* hypertension.
- **Bilateral effusions:** CHF or collagen vascular disease. **Unilateral:** carcinoma, PE (also HF, parapneumonic).
- **Chest CT** is reserved for parenchyma (ILD) or **CTA for PE**.

## Labs
- **Hematocrit** for anemia.
- **BMP:** metabolic acidosis, or **high bicarbonate suggesting CO₂ retention → ABG**.
- **ECG:** hypertrophy, prior MI. **Spirometry:** obstruction or possible restriction, then lung volumes, DLCO and neuromuscular tests.
- **Echo:** suspected systolic dysfunction, pulmonary hypertension or valve disease.
- **Normal exam and spirometry with intermittent symptoms → bronchoprovocation (methacholine) or home peak flow.** Up to one-third of patients diagnosed with asthma don't have reactive airways on testing.
- **BNP:** useful in acute dyspnea, but also rises with **RV strain**.

## Stepwise algorithm (Fig 39-2)
1. **H&P + walking oximetry + peak flow**
2. **Phase 1:** CXR, spirometry, ECG, CBC, BMP
3. **Phase 2:** chest CT (± CTA), lung volumes, DLCO, neuromuscular tests, echo, stress test
4. **Phase 3:** **cardiopulmonary exercise test (CPET)** and specialist referral

## CPET: heart vs. lungs
| Pulmonary limitation | Cardiac limitation |
|---|---|
| Reaches predicted max ventilation | **HR >85% of predicted max** |
| ↑ Dead space or hypoxemia | **Early anaerobic threshold** |
| Bronchospasm | BP too high or **falls** with exercise |
| | **O₂ pulse falls** (↓ stroke volume); ischemic ECG |

## Treatment
- **Treat the underlying cause.** It is often multifactorial.
- **Chronic breathlessness syndrome:** breathlessness persisting despite optimal treatment, causing disability.
- **O₂ if resting SpO₂ ≤88%** or desaturation to that level with activity or sleep. **It improves mortality in hypoxemic COPD.**
- **Pulmonary rehabilitation** (including yoga or tai chi) improves dyspnea, exercise capacity and hospitalizations. Supervised exercise also helps post-COVID.
- **Opioids:** reduce air hunger. However, a recent trial showed **no benefit of low-dose ER morphine in COPD** → individualize for respiratory depression risk.
- **Anxiolytics:** no consistent benefit.

## Next best step
| Scenario | Next step |
|---|---|
| Chronic dyspnea, unclear cause | Walking oximetry + peak flow, then CXR, spirometry, ECG, CBC, BMP |
| Intermittent wheeze, normal exam and spirometry | Methacholine challenge or home peak flow |
| Restrictive spirometry | Lung volumes, DLCO; MIP/MEP if neuromuscular disease suspected |
| ↓ DLCO, clear lungs, loud P₂ | Echo → RHC; V/Q for chronic thromboembolism |
| Elevated bicarbonate | ABG for CO₂ retention |
| Dyspnea worse upright, better supine | Evaluate for hepatopulmonary syndrome or atrial myxoma |
| Both heart and lung disease, unclear driver | CPET |
| COPD with resting SpO₂ ≤88% | Long-term oxygen |
| COPD with persistent dyspnea | Pulmonary rehabilitation |
