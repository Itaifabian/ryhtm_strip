# Ch 22 — Fever of Unknown Origin (high-yield)

## Definition (current)
1. Fever **≥38.3°C (101°F) on at least 2 occasions**
2. Illness of **≥3 weeks**
3. **Not immunocompromised** (that group needs a different, more aggressive approach)
4. Diagnosis still unclear after a thorough history, exam and the **obligatory tests**:
   - ESR, CRP, CBC with differential, platelets;
   - electrolytes, creatinine, total protein, **protein electrophoresis**;
   - ALP, AST, ALT, LDH, CK, **ferritin**, ANA, RF;
   - UA, **3 blood cultures**, urine culture;
   - **CXR, abdominal ultrasound**, **TST or IGRA**.

(The original Petersdorf-Beeson criteria were 3 weeks + 1 week of inpatient workup.)

**Inflammation of unknown origin (IUO)** = the same, but with raised CRP or ESR and a normal temperature. Same causes, same workup.

## Causes (Table 22-1, Table 22-2)
| Region | Infection | NIID | Malignancy | No diagnosis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Western Europe | 15.5% | **25%** | 11% | **39.5%** |
| Other Europe/Turkey | 42% | 23% | 14% | 16% |
| Middle East | 66% | 15% | 7% | 8% |
| Asia | 42% | 19% | 13% | 17% |

- **Western cohorts: noninfectious inflammatory diseases (NIID: autoimmune, autoinflammatory, granulomatous, vasculitis) are the leading cause.** Non-Western cohorts: infection, with **TB causing up to half** of infections.
- **FUO is usually an atypical presentation of a common disease**, not a rare one.
- **"FUO paradox":** undiagnosed cases have increased (>50% in some Western cohorts) because easy cases are diagnosed before 3 weeks. Most undiagnosed patients do well.
- Lower diagnostic yield: recurrent episodes, symptoms >12 months, normal PET/CT. But TB, endocarditis, spondylodiscitis and lymphoma still occur even with long durations.

**Specific causes:**
- **Infections:** endocarditis, diverticulitis, vertebral osteomyelitis, extrapulmonary TB.
  - **Q fever:** rural living, valve disease, aortic aneurysm or vascular prosthesis → IFA serology.
  - **Whipple:** unexplained CNS, GI or joint symptoms → *T. whipplei* PCR on stool and blood.
  - **Travel or residence** (tropics, American Southwest): malaria, leishmaniasis, histoplasmosis, coccidioidomycosis.
- **Culture-negative endocarditis:**
  - nutritionally variant strep, **HACEK**, *Coxiella*, *T. whipplei*, *Bartonella*;
  - marantic (adenocarcinoma);
  - SLE / antiphospholipid.
- **NIID:** adult-onset Still, large-vessel vasculitis, PMR, SLE, sarcoid.
  - **GCA in up to 17% of elderly FUO.**
  - **Schnitzler:** urticaria + bone pain + monoclonal gammopathy.
  - **VEXAS:** older men with chondritis, skin lesions, lung disease, VTE, MDS; **vacuoles in myeloid precursors**; somatic *UBA1* variant.
  - FMF in endemic regions.
- **Malignancy:** **lymphoma by far the most common** (fever can come before palpable nodes). Solid tumors: renal cell, hepatocellular, colon, lung, pancreas, breast.
- **Drug fever** (± DRESS with eosinophilia and lymphadenopathy), even after long-term use:
  - allopurinol, carbamazepine, lamotrigine, phenytoin, sulfasalazine, furosemide;
  - antimicrobials: sulfonamides, minocycline, vancomycin, β-lactams, INH.
- **Benign or exercise-induced hyperthermia:** high temperature with **normal CRP and ESR**.
- **Factitious fever:** self-injection, health care workers. **Fraudulent fever:** thermometer manipulation → **measure at several sites at once**; temp–pulse dissociation is a clue.

## Structured approach (Fig 22-1)
1. **Find potentially diagnostic clues (PDCs):** repeated full history and exam. Ask about the fever pattern, drugs, family history, sex, origin, travel, hobbies, animals. Examine the eyes, nodes, temporal arteries, liver, spleen, surgical scars, entire skin, mucosa.
2. **Stop antibiotics and glucocorticoids** first. They mask disease (falsely sterile cultures, shrinking nodes).
3. **Exclude fraudulent fever** and benign hyperthermia (especially if inflammatory markers are normal).
4. **Stop all drugs** (including OTC and supplements). **Fever persisting >72 h after stopping → that drug is unlikely.**
5. **PDCs present → targeted tests** only. Low-yield without PDCs: extra serologies (more false than true positives), echo, sinus films, GI studies, marrow or liver biopsy, bronchoscopy.
6. **No PDCs → cryoglobulins + fundoscopy** (retinal vasculitis) early.
7. **Cultures:** no more than 3 blood and 1 urine culture without PDCs. Repeat only if prior samples were drawn on antibiotics or within 1 week after. Tell the lab to hold for fastidious organisms (HACEK). **FUO + headache → CSF** (HSV-2, *Cryptococcus*, TB).
8. **Still no diagnosis → ¹⁸F-FDG-PET/CT**, ideally **when CRP or ESR is elevated** (or during an episode). Total diagnostic yield ~50%, better than gallium (21–54%) or leukocyte scans (8–31%), which are alternatives if PET isn't available.
   - PET can't tell infection from inflammation from cancer, but all three cause FUO.
   - Physiologic uptake hides the brain, heart, bowel, kidney and bladder. A **low-carb diet** before the scan unmasks endocarditis.
   - **Steroids quickly abolish uptake → stop or postpone them until after PET.**
   - Don't repeat a well-timed negative PET unless new signs appear.
9. **Then:** PDC-driven biopsy and culture. If the patient is stable with no new PDCs, **watch and wait** rather than order more screening. Investigate further only if the patient deteriorates. Consider a **second opinion at an expert center** (finds a diagnosis in >50%).

**Recurrent fever:** same workup, but test **during an episode** (have the patient return while febrile).

## Specific test pearls
- **TST or IGRA:** a negative result **doesn't exclude TB** (indeterminate in miliary TB, malnutrition, immunosuppression). IGRA is less affected by BCG.
- **Miliary TB:** **liver biopsy** (AFB smear, culture, PCR) has the highest yield. Marrow and nodes are alternatives. Granulomas → reconsider TB.
- **Suspected GCA ≥55:** **temporal artery biopsy is still recommended.** PET can miss temporal-limited disease (small vessels next to high brain uptake).

## Treatment
- Treat the diagnosis. **Avoid empiric trials** unless the patient is deteriorating rapidly. Most unexplained FUO resolves on its own.
- **Empiric antibiotics:** hemodynamic instability or **neutropenia**.
- **Anti-TB trial:** positive TST/IGRA, granulomas with anergy (sarcoid unlikely), or from an endemic area. Only after mycobacterial cultures and PCR are sent. **No response in 6 wk → rethink the diagnosis.**
- **NSAIDs or acetaminophen** for symptoms (Still disease may respond dramatically).
- **Colchicine** for suspected FMF (esp. from a high-prevalence region), recurrent pericarditis, Behçet. It prevents attacks but doesn't reliably abort one.
- **Glucocorticoids:** avoid until **infection and lymphoma are excluded** and inflammatory disease is probable and dangerous. Dramatic in GCA and PMR (Fig 22-2: PET showed large-vessel vasculitis and PMR, and the patient recovered within a week on prednisone 60 mg).
- **Anakinra** (IL-1Ra): trial for undiagnosed FUO with an **IL-1 phenotype (serositis, high CRP, high ferritin)**. Preferred over steroids when autoinflammation is suspected.
- **Prognosis:** favorable if unexplained after a full workup (≤8% mortality over years). Risk is highest early, in the index admission (e.g., intravascular lymphoma found at autopsy). A normal PET predicts spontaneous resolution.

## Next best step
| Scenario | Next step |
|---|---|
| FUO on antibiotics or prednisone | Stop them before testing |
| FUO on multiple drugs | Stop all; reassess at 72 h |
| Normal inflammatory markers, high temps, health care worker | Simultaneous multi-site temperatures |
| No PDCs after obligatory tests | Cryoglobulins + fundoscopy, then FDG-PET/CT |
| Episodic fever | Repeat workup and PET during an episode |
| Suspected GCA, age ≥55 | Temporal artery biopsy |
| Suspected miliary TB | Liver biopsy (AFB, culture, PCR) |
| Rural living + valve or vascular graft | Q fever IFA serology |
| CNS, GI or joint symptoms | *T. whipplei* PCR (stool and blood) |
| Stable, full workup negative | Watchful waiting for new PDCs; avoid empiric steroids |
| Unstable or neutropenic | Empiric antibiotics |
