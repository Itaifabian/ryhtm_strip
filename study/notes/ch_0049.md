# Ch 49 — Diarrhea and Constipation (high-yield)

## Physiology snapshot
- **Fluid:** 9 L/d enters the gut, ~1 L reaches the colon, 0.2 L leaves in stool. The colon can absorb up to 4× its usual load.
- **Na⁺-glucose cotransport (SGLT1)** is the basis of oral rehydration.
- **Migrating motor complex:** every 60–90 min while fasting (the "housekeeper").
- **Continence:** puborectalis sling plus internal and external sphincters. **The anorectal angle opens >15° during defecation.**

## Diarrhea definitions
- **Stool >200 g/d.**
- **Duration:** acute <2 wk; persistent 2–4 wk; **chronic >4 wk**.
- **Look-alikes:**
  - **Pseudodiarrhea:** frequent small stools with urgency or tenesmus (IBS, proctitis).
  - **Fecal incontinence.**
  - **Overflow** around an impaction in nursing home patients (**rectal exam**).

## Acute diarrhea
- **>90% infectious.** The rest: drugs (the most common noninfectious cause), toxins, ischemia.
- **High-risk groups:**
  - **Travelers:** ETEC and enteroaggregative *E. coli*; also Campylobacter, Shigella, Salmonella, norovirus. **Giardia and Cyclospora** in campers.
  - **Food:**
    - chicken → Salmonella, Campylobacter, Shigella;
    - **undercooked hamburger → EHEC O157:H7**;
    - **fried rice → *B. cereus***;
    - **mayonnaise or cream → *S. aureus***, Salmonella;
    - eggs → Salmonella;
    - deli or dairy → **Listeria**;
    - raw seafood → Vibrio, hepatitis A.
  - **Immunodeficient:** severe or protracted illness. **Hemochromatosis → Vibrio and Yersinia** (avoid raw fish and seawater on wounds).
  - **Daycare:** Shigella, Giardia, Cryptosporidium, rotavirus.
  - **Institutions:** ***C. difficile*** (also community-acquired without antibiotics).

**Table 49-2 — clinical patterns**
| Mechanism | Incubation | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Preformed toxin** (*B. cereus, S. aureus, C. perfringens*) | **1–8 h** (8–24 h for *C. perfringens*) | **Prominent vomiting**, little fever, watery |
| **Enterotoxin** (cholera, ETEC) | 8–72 h | Profuse watery diarrhea |
| **Enteroadherent** (EPEC, Giardia, Cryptosporidium) | 1–8 d | Cramping or bloating, mushy stools |
| **Cytotoxin** (*C. difficile*, EHEC) | 1–3 d | Severe pain; **EHEC turns bloody quickly** |
| **Invasive** (Shigella, EIEC, *E. histolytica*) | 12 h–8 d | **High fever, pain, bloody (dysentery)** |
| **Yersinia** | — | **Mimics appendicitis** (terminal ileum) |

**Complications:**
- **Reactive arthritis:** Salmonella, Campylobacter, Shigella, Yersinia.
- **HUS:** EHEC O157:H7, Shigella.
- **Post-infectious IBS:** Campylobacter.

**Noninfectious acute causes:**
- **Ischemic colitis:** age >50; acute lower abdominal pain → watery then bloody diarrhea; **left colon, rectum spared**.
- **Toxins:** scombroid (histamine), ciguatera, mushrooms, organophosphates, arsenic.
- **Alpha-gal** allergy.

**When to test stool (Fig 49-3):**
- profuse diarrhea with dehydration;
- **bloody stools**;
- **fever ≥38.5°C**;
- **>48 h without improvement**;
- recent antibiotics;
- outbreaks;
- severe pain at age >50;
- **elderly (≥70) or immunocompromised**.

**Testing:**
- **Culture-independent multiplex PCR first.** Culture is for outbreaks.
- **No serology or endoscopy during acute enteritis.** Celiac and Crohn testing early after enteritis gives false positives.

**Persistent (2–4 wk):** **Giardia** most common; also *C. difficile*, *E. histolytica*, Cryptosporidium. **Brainerd diarrhea:** abrupt onset, lasts ≥4 wk up to 1–3 years.

**Treatment:**
- **Fluids and electrolytes for everyone.** Oral sugar-electrolyte solution; **IV if profoundly dehydrated**.
- **Loperamide:** moderate diarrhea that is **nonfebrile and nonbloody**. **Avoid in febrile dysentery.** Caution with QT drugs.
- **Bismuth:** avoid in immunocompromise or renal impairment (encephalopathy).
- **Empiric antibiotics** for moderate–severe febrile illness:
  - **azithromycin 500 mg × 1** or ciprofloxacin 500 mg BID × 3–5 d;
  - **suspected Giardia → metronidazole 250 mg QID × 7 d**;
  - nitazoxanide for resistant Giardia or Cryptosporidium.
- **Treat regardless of organism** if immunocompromised, **mechanical valve or recent vascular graft**, or elderly.
- **Travel prophylaxis only for high-risk travelers:** immunocompromise, IBD, hemochromatosis, achlorhydria. Cipro, azithro or **rifaximin** (not for invasive disease).
- **Report outbreaks** to public health.

## Chronic diarrhea (Table 49-3)
| Type | Clues | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Secretory** | Watery, large volume, painless, **persists with fasting**, **no osmotic gap** | **Drugs or stimulant laxatives** (most common), alcohol, **bile acid diarrhea**, ileal resection under 100 cm, hormones (carcinoid, **VIPoma**, gastrinoma, medullary thyroid cancer, mastocytosis, villous adenoma), Addison, **olmesartan** sprue-like enteropathy |
| **Osmotic** | **Stops with fasting**, **osmotic gap >50** | Mg²⁺ laxatives, **lactase deficiency** (low stool pH), sorbitol, fructose, FODMAPs |
| **Steatorrheal** | Greasy, foul, weight loss | Pancreatic insufficiency (**>32 g/d** fat), celiac or small bowel (15–25 g/d), bacterial overgrowth, Whipple, lymphatic obstruction |
| **Inflammatory** | Pain, fever, blood, **calprotectin** | IBD, **microscopic colitis**, infection, radiation, eosinophilic gastroenteritis |
| **Dysmotile** | — | IBS, hyperthyroidism, diabetic neuropathy |
| **Factitial** | Women, health care workers; **hypokalemia** | Surreptitious laxatives; **very low stool osmolality = added water, high = added urine** |

- **Stool osmotic gap = 290 − 2 × (stool Na⁺ + K⁺).**
- **Bile acid diarrhea:**
  - ~40% of unexplained chronic diarrhea; ~1% of the population;
  - ↓ FGF-19;
  - **diagnosis:** scintigraphic radiolabeled bile acid retention test, **serum 7αC4**, or fecal bile acids;
  - **treatment: bile acid sequestrants** (cholestyramine, colestipol, colesevelam).
- **Ileal resection:** under 100 cm → bile acid diarrhea.
- **Hormonal causes:**
  - **VIPoma:** >3 L/d stool, **hypokalemia, achlorhydria**.
  - **Carcinoid:** flushing, wheezing, right-sided valve disease; **octreotide**.
  - **Gastrinoma:** diarrhea in one-third; PPI.
  - **Medullary thyroid cancer:** treated with **indomethacin**.
- **Microscopic colitis:**
  - **middle-aged women**;
  - **NSAIDs, statins, PPIs, SSRIs**;
  - **normal-looking colon → biopsy**;
  - treat with budesonide, bismuth, loperamide or bile acid sequestrants.
- **Celiac:** ~1%, often **without steatorrhea**, mimics IBS. **TTG-IgA while eating gluten.**
- **Whipple disease:** middle-aged men; arthralgia, fever, nodes, CNS and endocardial involvement.
- **Abetalipoproteinemia:** acanthocytes, ataxia, retinitis pigmentosa.
- **Pancreatic insufficiency:** occurs after **>90% loss of function**. Direct secretin test is best; fecal elastase has low sensitivity and specificity.
- **IgA deficiency or CVID:** Giardia, bacterial overgrowth, Strongyloides.
- **Eosinophilic gastroenteritis:** atopy, Charcot-Leyden crystals, peripheral eosinophilia in 50–75%.
- **IBS:**
  - **10% prevalence**;
  - pain relieved by defecation, alternating bowel habit;
  - **symptoms stop at night**; weight loss is rare.

**Chronic diarrhea approach (Fig 49-4):**
- **History:** nocturnal stools, fasting response, drugs, travel, weight loss, extraintestinal signs.
- **Screen:** CBC, chemistries, CRP or ESR, iron, folate, B12, **TTG-IgA, C4**, stool fat, **calprotectin**.
- **Therapeutic trials are cost-effective:**
  - **lactose restriction** for fasting-responsive watery diarrhea in a young adult;
  - **metronidazole** after backpacking;
  - **sequestrant** after ileal resection.
- **Suspected IBS:** **flexible sigmoidoscopy with biopsies** to exclude IBD or microscopic colitis.
- **Chronic diarrhea + hematochezia:** stool studies + **colonoscopy**. Middle-aged or older with inflammatory diarrhea: colonoscopy (tumor).
- **Secretory:** recheck drugs and laxatives; stool PCR, cultures, **Giardia antigen (most sensitive)**; breath test for bacterial overgrowth; EGD and colonoscopy with biopsies; hormone screens (gastrin, VIP, calcitonin, TSH, **urinary 5-HIAA**, histamine) when suggested.
- **Fatty:** small bowel biopsy and aspirate → imaging → pancreatic function testing.

**Empiric treatment:**
- **Loperamide or diphenoxylate.** Codeine or opium tincture if severe. **Avoid antimotility drugs in severe IBD (toxic megacolon).**
- **Clonidine** for diabetic diarrhea (watch for orthostasis).
- **IBS-D:** **alosetron or ondansetron**, rifaximin, **eluxadoline** (pancreatitis risk, **especially after cholecystectomy**).
- Crofelemer for AIDS diarrhea.
- Replace fat-soluble vitamins in steatorrhea.

## Constipation
- **Symptoms:** <3 stools a week, hard or lumpy stools, straining, incomplete evacuation, **digital maneuvers or perineal support** (the last suggests an evacuation disorder).
- **>90% have no underlying cause.** Treat with **fluid, exercise, fiber 15–25 g/d**.
- **Causes (Table 49-5):**
  - **Recent onset:** colonic obstruction (cancer, stricture), **anal fissure** or painful hemorrhoids, drugs.
  - **Chronic:**
    - IBS-C;
    - **CCBs, antidepressants**;
    - slow transit, megacolon (Hirschsprung, Chagas);
    - **evacuation disorders** (pelvic floor dyssynergia, rectocele, prolapse);
    - **hypothyroidism, hypercalcemia**, pregnancy;
    - depression;
    - **Parkinson, MS, spinal cord injury**;
    - scleroderma.
- **Evaluation:**
  - **Rectal exam is mandatory:** impaction, high sphincter tone, paradoxical puborectalis contraction, poor perineal descent.
  - **Alarm features (weight loss, bleeding, anemia) → colonoscopy.**
  - **Labs: calcium, potassium, TSH.**
  - **Melanosis coli** = anthraquinone laxatives (senna, cascara).
- **Treatment ladder:**
  1. Fiber
  2. **Osmotic** (PEG, lactulose, Mg) with **bowel training** (toilet 15–20 min after breakfast)
  3. **Stimulant** (bisacodyl, senna); **prucalopride** (5-HT₄)
  4. **Secretagogues:** **lubiprostone** (ClC-2), **linaclotide or plecanatide** (GC-C agonists), **tenapanor** (NHE3 inhibitor)
- **Refractory:**
  - **Anorectal manometry + balloon expulsion before transit testing** if an evacuation disorder is suspected. **Abnormal: balloon expulsion >60 s.** **Absent rectoanal inhibitory reflex = Hirschsprung.**
  - **Radiopaque markers:** ≥80% should pass by day 5.
- **Evacuation disorder → biofeedback** (3 months, **70–80% success**). Surgery and botulinum have been largely abandoned.
- **Slow transit refractory to 3–6 months of treatment → colectomy with ileorectostomy**, only if there's no evacuation disorder or generalized dysmotility, and **not for pain**.
- **Spinal cord injury:** scheduled bowel regimen.
