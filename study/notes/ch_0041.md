# Ch 41 — Hemoptysis (high-yield)

## Core facts
- **Hemoptysis:** expectorated blood from the **lower respiratory tract** (glottis to alveolus).
- **Distinguish from:** hematemesis and epistaxis. Look for **pseudohemoptysis** from the upper airway or GI tract.
- **Most important information: volume and frequency.**
- **Massive hemoptysis** (variable definitions):
  - >150 mL in 24 h, or a rate ≥100 mL/h;
  - **life-threatening** = abnormal gas exchange, hemodynamic compromise, or threatened airway.
- **Patients die of asphyxiation, not exsanguination.** The risk is with >400 mL in 24 h or >150 mL at once. Life-threatening cases are **5–15%** of hemoptysis.
- **Absence of anemia doesn't exclude life-threatening bleeding.**
- **Source:** most bleeding comes from the **bronchial circulation** (systemic, from the aorta, high pressure, hard to control). The bronchial arteries neovascularize tumors, bronchiectasis and cavities.

## Causes
| Category | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Infection** | **US: viral bronchitis (most small-volume), bronchiectasis, malignancy are the most common.** Chronic bronchitis superinfection (*S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, M. catarrhalis*). **Bronchiectasis/CF** exacerbations, which can be massive and fatal |
| **TB** | Most common cause in endemic regions. Cavities; **Rasmussen aneurysm** (pulmonary artery aneurysm eroding into a cavity) |
| **Other cavitary** | Endemic fungi, *Nocardia*, NTM; **Aspergillus mycetoma** in an old cavity (neovascularized); abscess or necrotizing pneumonia (*S. aureus, Klebsiella*, oral anaerobes) |
| **Paragonimiasis** | Mimics TB; Southeast Asia and China; **raw crayfish** in North America; consider in immigrants |
| **Cardiac** | Pulmonary edema ("pink frothy," but can be frank blood, especially on anticoagulants or antiplatelets) |
| **Vascular** | PE with infarction (PE doesn't commonly cause hemoptysis); AVM; **aortobronchial fistula** (aneurysm or pseudoaneurysm, herald bleeds before massive hemorrhage) |
| **Diffuse alveolar hemorrhage** | Diffuse ground glass; **often no hemoptysis**. Causes: SLE capillaritis, cocaine and inhalants, stem cell transplant, **vaping**. **Pulmonary-renal syndromes: GPA, anti-GBM** (hemoptysis ± hematuria) |
| **Malignancy** | Any bronchogenic carcinoma. **Carcinoid** (vascular, proximal airways). **Small cell and squamous are central → massive bleeding.** Metastases (melanoma, sarcoma, breast, colon); **Kaposi sarcoma** (AIDS) |
| **Coagulopathy or antithrombotics** | Minor insults can bleed |

## Evaluation
- **History:**
  - character (flecks, pink, frank blood, clots);
  - amount (**1 US cup = 236 mL**);
  - smoking or weight loss (cancer), fever or sputum (infection);
  - CF or bronchiectasis.
- **Exam:**
  - hypoxemia, tachycardia, instability;
  - **nose and mouth** (extrapulmonary source);
  - lung exam for laterality;
  - clubbing, ecchymoses or petechiae, **telangiectasias**, rash.
- **Labs:**
  - **CBC, coagulation studies, BMP/creatinine, urinalysis** (pulmonary-renal);
  - as indicated: sputum bacterial and **AFB culture**, viral panel, **ANA, ANCA, anti-GBM**.
- **Imaging:**
  - **CXR first.** It often doesn't localize and may be normal.
  - **No risk factors + normal CXR → treat for bronchitis, close follow-up.**
  - **Risk factors (age >40, malignancy risk, significant volume) → chest CT.** CT finds the site and cause in most patients.
  - **CT angiography** maps a source for embolization.
- **Bronchoscopy:** complementary. It finds **subtle mucosal lesions CT misses** and localizes the side. Highest yield **during active bleeding**.

## Fig 41-1 algorithm
- **No risk factors → treat the underlying disease (usually infection).** If bleeding persists → risk-factor pathway.
- **Risk factors → CXR, CBC, UA, creatinine, coagulation → CT → bronchoscopy → treat the cause.** Persistent bleeding → embolization or resection.
- **Massive → protect the airway.** If bleeding stops → workup. If it continues → **embolization or resection**.

## Life-threatening hemoptysis: three simultaneous goals
1. **Protect the nonbleeding lung**
   - **Position bleeding side down.**
   - Let a strong cough clear clot. Otherwise **intubate with a large ETT (8.5)** for suction and bronchoscopy. It can be advanced into the **mainstem of the nonbleeding lung**.
   - Double-lumen tubes work, but each lumen is too small for suction or interventions.
2. **Localize**
   - Imaging, **flexible bronchoscopy during bleeding** (identifies side and lobe), or go **straight to CT angiography** (diagnostic and pre-therapeutic).
3. **Control**
   - **Correct any bleeding diathesis first.**
   - **Bronchoscopic:** suction, **balloon catheter or bronchial blocker**, iced saline, epinephrine, **tranexamic acid** (instilled or nebulized). Rigid bronchoscopy allows APC or cautery for central lesions.
   - **Bronchial artery embolization = procedure of choice.** >80% immediate success. Bleeding recurs if the cause isn't treated (e.g., mycetoma).
   - **Surgery:** mortality **15–40%**. Only if other measures fail and bleeding continues. Best candidates have localized disease with otherwise normal lungs.

## Next best step
| Scenario | Next step |
|---|---|
| Blood-streaked sputum, young nonsmoker, URI, normal CXR | Treat bronchitis, close follow-up |
| Hemoptysis in a 55-year-old smoker, normal CXR | Chest CT (then bronchoscopy) |
| Hemoptysis + hematuria | UA, creatinine, ANCA, anti-GBM |
| Massive, right-sided bleeding | Right side down; secure the airway |
| Needs intubation for massive hemoptysis | Large (8.5) single-lumen ETT; may advance into the nonbleeding mainstem |
| Life-threatening bleeding, source localized | Bronchial artery embolization |
| Hemoptysis on warfarin with high INR | Reverse the coagulopathy first |
| Old TB cavity with mycetoma, recurrent bleeding | Embolize; recurrence likely unless the mycetoma is treated |
| Immigrant from Southeast Asia, TB-like picture, negative AFB | Consider paragonimiasis |
| Embolization failed, ongoing bleeding, localized disease | Surgical resection |
