# Ch 55 — Azotemia and Urinary Abnormalities (high-yield)

## Table 55-1 — nephrologic syndromes
| Syndrome | Key clues |
|---|---|
| Acute or rapidly progressive renal failure | Anuria or oliguria, documented recent GFR fall |
| **Acute nephritis** | **Hematuria, RBC casts**, azotemia, oliguria, edema, hypertension |
| **Chronic renal failure** | Azotemia >3 months, **small kidneys bilaterally, broad casts**, renal osteodystrophy |
| **Nephrotic syndrome** | **Proteinuria >3.5 g/24 h**, hypoalbuminemia, edema, hyperlipidemia; lipiduria, hypercoagulability |
| Asymptomatic urinary abnormalities | Hematuria, subnephrotic proteinuria, sterile pyuria |
| UTI or pyelonephritis | Bacteriuria >10⁵ CFU/mL, **WBC casts**, flank tenderness, fever |
| Tubular defects | Electrolyte disorders, polyuria, **"tubular" proteinuria <1 g/day** |
| Obstruction | Anuria or polyuria, weak stream, **large prostate, full bladder after voiding** |

## Estimating GFR
- **Creatinine:**
  - freely filtered, not reabsorbed, **secreted** (especially in advanced CKD, so GFR is overestimated);
  - **rises after eating cooked meat**;
  - **low muscle mass hides GFR loss** (chronic illness, steroids, malnutrition).
- **Urea clearance underestimates GFR** (tubular reabsorption).
- **24-h CrCl = (Uvol × UCr)/(PCr × minutes).**
  - Adequate collection: **men 18.5–25 mg/kg/day, women 16.5–22.4** (an 80-kg man excretes ~1500–2000 mg).
- **Equations:**
  - **Cockcroft-Gault:** (140 − age) × weight / (72 × Cr), × 0.85 if female.
  - MDRD: best when GFR <60.
  - **CKD-EPI:** more accurate.
  - **All assume steady state.**
- **Race modifier:** removed by many centers (it delayed transplant listing in Black patients).
- **Cystatin C:**
  - less affected by muscle mass and race; **more sensitive for early GFR decline**;
  - influenced by sex, diabetes, smoking, inflammation;
  - **creatinine + cystatin C combined is most accurate**;
  - useful in **bodybuilders** and others with unusual muscle mass.
- **CKD trajectory:** 1/PCr vs. time is roughly linear. **A deviation means a superimposed acute process.**
- **Uremic symptoms usually start at GFR <15.**

## Approach to azotemia (Fig 55-1)
- **First steps:** **urinalysis + renal ultrasound**.
- **Hydronephrosis → urology, relieve obstruction.**
- **Small kidneys, thin echogenic cortex, isosthenuria, bland sediment, <3.5 g protein → CKD.** Slow progression, prepare for dialysis if end-stage.
- **Anemia, hypocalcemia and hyperphosphatemia occur in AKI too.** Renal osteodystrophy on imaging is CKD-only (and late).

## Prerenal azotemia (40–80% of AKI)
- **Causes:**
  - **true volume loss:** GI bleeding, burns, diarrhea, diuretics;
  - **sequestration:** pancreatitis, peritonitis, rhabdomyolysis;
  - **low effective volume:** cardiogenic shock, sepsis;
  - **renal vasoconstriction:** severe HF, **hepatorenal syndrome**, NSAIDs.
- **Autoregulation:**
  - **afferent dilation by prostaglandins** + **efferent constriction by angiotensin II**;
  - **GFR falls steeply when MAP <80 mmHg**.
- **NSAIDs** (block prostaglandins) and **ACE inhibitors or ARBs** (block efferent tone) cause hemodynamic AKI, especially with volume depletion or **bilateral renal artery stenosis (or stenosis in a solitary kidney)**.

## Table 55-2 — prerenal vs. ATN
| Index | Prerenal | Oliguric ATN |
|---|---|---|
| BUN:Cr | **>20:1** | 10–15:1 |
| Urine Na | **<20** | >40 |
| Urine osmolality | **>500** | <350 |
| **FENa** | **<1%** | >2% |
| U/P creatinine | >40 | <20 |
| Sediment | Normal or hyaline/granular casts | **Muddy brown granular casts, tubular epithelial cells** |

- **FENa <1% despite ATN:** nonoliguric ATN (**rhabdomyolysis**), and ATN on top of **HF, cirrhosis or hepatorenal syndrome**.
- **Sediment microscopy is key:** dark granular casts or tubular epithelial cells strongly predict ATN.

## Postrenal (<5%, but reversible; rule it out early)
- **Requires** bladder outlet or urethral obstruction, **bilateral ureteral** obstruction, or a **solitary kidney**.
- **Ultrasound may be falsely negative** early on or with encasement (retroperitoneal fibrosis, pelvic tumor) → **MAG3 furosemide renogram**.

## Intrinsic disease
- **ATN = ~90% of intrinsic AKI.**
  - **Ischemic:** surgery, trauma, hypovolemia, sepsis, burns.
  - **Nephrotoxic:** vasoconstriction, tubular toxicity, obstruction. The kidney gets 25% of cardiac output.
  - **Treatment:** stop toxins, support BP. Dialysis often isn't needed.
- **Acute interstitial nephritis:**
  - **drugs: antibiotics, NSAIDs, PPIs**;
  - infection, SLE, sarcoid, Sjögren, lymphoma;
  - **urine:** pyuria (~75%), mild proteinuria, hematuria, **WBC casts**;
  - **urine eosinophils are no longer recommended** (no diagnostic utility);
  - biopsy to confirm.
- **Atheroembolic disease:**
  - **after aortic instrumentation**;
  - cholesterol emboli with an eosinophil-rich reaction;
  - **livedo reticularis, distal infarcts, eosinophilia**;
  - urinalysis often normal; biopsy is often unnecessary.
- **Renal artery thrombosis:** mild proteinuria and hematuria. **Renal vein thrombosis:** **heavy proteinuria** + hematuria. **Angiography** confirms.
- **Glomerular disease or microvasculature** (HUS, TTP, malignant hypertension):
  - **RBC casts → early renal biopsy**;
  - RBC casts are highly specific but insensitive;
  - **dysmorphic RBCs (acanthocytes)** on phase contrast.

## Urine volume
- **Oliguria <400 mL/day** (never normal, since obligate solute excretion needs ≥400 mL). **Anuria <100 mL/day.**
- **Causes of anuria:**
  - **bilateral obstruction**;
  - arterial occlusion or dissection, renal vein thrombosis;
  - **myeloma cast nephropathy**, **cortical necrosis**;
  - severe ATN, severe RPGN;
  - **NSAID + ACE inhibitor/ARB**;
  - shock.
- **Nonoliguric ATN:** milder K⁺ and acid problems, faster recovery.
- **Polyuria (>3 L/day; confirm with a 24-h collection):**
  - **Urine osm <250 → water diuresis:** primary polydipsia (urine ~**50**, low-normal serum osm), **central DI**, **nephrogenic DI**.
  - **Urine osm >300 → solute diuresis:**
    - **uncontrolled diabetes (most common)** → hypernatremia;
    - mannitol, contrast, high-protein feeds (urea);
    - **postobstructive diuresis, resolving ATN** (can be large);
    - salt wasting (cystic disease, Bartter).
  - **Nephrogenic DI causes (Fig 55-4):**
    - **lithium**, demeclocycline, amphotericin, methoxyflurane, ethanol;
    - **hypercalcemia, hypokalemia**;
    - obstruction, sickle cell, Sjögren, myeloma, amyloid, sarcoid.
  - **Distinguishing central from nephrogenic DI:** **plasma AVP or copeptin**. Water deprivation + **DDAVP**; hypertonic saline-stimulated copeptin can replace water deprivation.

## Proteinuria (Fig 55-3)
- **Dipstick:**
  - detects **albumin only**;
  - **false positive** at pH >7, with concentrated urine, or with blood;
  - **misses light chains (Bence Jones)** and dilute urine.
- **Quantify with:**
  - **first-morning ACR** (mg/g ≈ mg/day);
  - or a protein/creatinine ratio (3.0 ≈ 3 g/day);
  - or a 24-h collection;
  - **sulfosalicylic acid** detects all proteins.
- **Normal:** total protein <150 mg/day, albumin <30 mg/day. Even <30 mg/day carries some CV and renal risk.
- **Overflow proteinuria:** **myeloma light chains** (proximal tubule injury, **cast nephropathy**, amyloid, light chain deposition); lysozyme in **CMML**.
- **Nephrotic syndrome:**
  - **>3.5 g/day**, hypoalbuminemia, hyperlipidemia (↑ hepatic synthesis), edema (Na retention including ENaC activation by filtered proteases);
  - **hypercoagulable:** **loss of antithrombin III**, ↓ protein S and C, ↑ fibrinogen;
  - infections (loss of immunoglobulins);
  - **hypertension is uncommon** in primary nephrotic disease;
  - **>3.5 g/day can occur without the full syndrome** (e.g., diabetes).

## Hematuria, pyuria, casts (Fig 55-2)
- **Hematuria = 2–5 RBC/HPF.**
  - **Dipstick positive with no RBCs on microscopy → myoglobin** (rhabdomyolysis).
- **One positive UA is common** (menses, viral illness, exercise, trauma).
- **Significant hematuria** (>3 RBC/HPF on 3 UAs, >100 RBCs on one, or gross) → urologic or renal lesion in **9.1%**. Cancer risk rises with age.
  - Workup: **cytology, imaging (CT), cystoscopy**.
- **Gross hematuria with clots → postrenal source.**
- **Isolated glomerular hematuria:** **IgA nephropathy, hereditary nephritis (family history of renal failure), thin basement membrane disease (family members with microscopic hematuria)**.
- **Dysmorphic RBCs + RBC casts + protein >500 mg/day = glomerulonephritis** → **serologies (ANCA, anti-GBM, complement, cryoglobulins, hepatitis B/C, ASO, HIV) + biopsy, even without azotemia**.
- **Hypercalciuria or hyperuricosuria** cause isolated hematuria. Diet fixes it in 50–60%.
- **Pyuria:**
  - **WBC casts + bacteria = pyelonephritis**;
  - **sterile pyuria → GU TB**;
  - WBC casts also occur in interstitial nephritis, GN, and transplant rejection.
- **Waxy or broad casts:** CKD.

## Next best step
| Scenario | Next step |
|---|---|
| New azotemia | Urinalysis + renal ultrasound |
| Hydronephrosis | Relieve obstruction (catheter, urology) |
| BUN:Cr 30, FENa 0.4%, bland sediment | Volume repletion (prerenal) |
| Muddy brown casts after hypotension | ATN: stop nephrotoxins, support BP |
| AKI after starting an ACE inhibitor, bilateral bruits | Stop it; evaluate for bilateral RAS |
| AKI 2 weeks after cardiac catheterization + livedo + eosinophilia | Atheroembolic disease |
| AKI on a PPI, pyuria, WBC casts | Stop the drug; biopsy if unclear (not urine eosinophils) |
| RBC casts + proteinuria | Serologies + early renal biopsy |
| Dipstick blood positive, 0 RBC/HPF, CK 40,000 | Myoglobinuria |
| Persistent painless hematuria, age 60, smoker | Urine cytology, renal imaging (CT), cystoscopy |
| Myeloma, negative dipstick, AKI | Sulfosalicylic acid test or UPEP |
| Polyuria >3 L/d, urine osm 80 | Water deprivation test / copeptin (DI) |
| Polyuria after relief of obstruction | Postobstructive solute diuresis |
| Muscular bodybuilder, Cr 1.5 | Cystatin C eGFR |
