# Ch 40 — Cough (high-yield)

## Mechanism
- **Receptors:** TRP channels and **P2X3**. Signals travel on Aδ fibers (mechanical) and C fibers (chemical) via the **vagus and superior laryngeal nerves → nucleus tractus solitarius**.
- **Afferent endings** are in the pharynx, larynx and airways down to the terminal bronchioles, plus the **external ear canal (Arnold nerve, auricular branch of the vagus)** and the esophagus.
- **Efferent sequence:** glottis closes → expiratory muscles generate intrathoracic pressure up to **300 mmHg** → glottis opens → flows exceed the normal flow-volume envelope (Fig 40-1).
  - Clearance energy ∝ velocity².
  - Repeated coughs at falling lung volumes push the clearing point further into the periphery.

## Impaired cough (Table 40-1)
- **Causes:**
  - respiratory muscle weakness, **chest wall or abdominal pain**, kyphoscoliosis;
  - impaired glottic closure or tracheostomy;
  - central depression (anesthesia, sedation);
  - abnormal secretions (**CF**), ciliary dysfunction (**primary ciliary dyskinesia**);
  - tracheobronchomalacia, bronchiectasis, stenoses.
- **Assessment:** peak expiratory flow or maximal expiratory pressure as a surrogate for cough strength.
- **Aids:** pillow splinting after surgery; mechanical cough-assist (positive then negative pressure).

## Duration
| Duration | Usual cause |
|---|---|
| **Acute <3 wk** | Respiratory infection, aspiration, inhaled chemicals or smoke |
| **Subacute 3–8 wk** | Post-tracheobronchitis: **pertussis**, postviral tussive syndrome |
| **Chronic >8 wk** | Broad cardiopulmonary differential |

- **Chronic cough with a normal exam and CXR:** **cough-variant asthma, GERD, rhinosinusitis/postnasal drainage, ACE inhibitors.** The source **disputes the claim that these cause >90%**.
- **Smoker with a productive early-morning cough:** chronic bronchitis.
- **Postbronchitic cough:** dry cough lasting >2 months after respiratory infections, especially in winter.
- **Cough hypersensitivity syndrome:**
  - dry cough with a throat tickle, **worse with talking, laughing or exertion**;
  - **more common in women**, lasts years;
  - a diagnosis of exclusion.
- **Timing, pattern and sound rarely help**, except for the sound of excess secretions. Most coughs worsen on first lying down and improve during sleep.

## Assessment
- **Exam:**
  - wheeze, crackles;
  - **ear canals and tympanic membranes (Arnold nerve)**;
  - nose (rhinitis, polyps);
  - nails (clubbing);
  - systemic signs (sarcoid, vasculitis).
- **Chest radiograph in virtually all chronic cough.** Silent serious causes include **sarcoidosis or Hodgkin lymphoma in young adults, lung cancer in older adults, and TB** worldwide.
- **Productive cough:**
  - **purulent sputum → bacterial (± mycobacterial) culture**;
  - mucoid sputum → cytology (malignancy, aspiration, **neutrophilic vs. eosinophilic bronchitis**).
- **Hemoptysis:** see Ch 41.

## Specific causes and treatment
| Cause | Clues | Diagnosis | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ACE inhibitor** | 5–30% of users, **not dose-dependent**, any time after starting | Stop the drug; **no improvement after 1 month argues against it** | Switch to an **ARB (no cough)** |
| **Upper airway cough syndrome** (postnasal drip) | Postnasal drip sensation, throat clearing, sneezing, rhinorrhea; **cobblestoned posterior pharynx**, polyps | Clinical; can't be quantified | Treat the cause: antihistamines, decongestants, antibiotics, saline irrigation, **nasal steroid**, antihistamine or anticholinergic sprays |
| **GERD** | Heartburn, belching, hoarseness; may be silent | 24-h **pH probe** or capsule ± manometry; laryngoscopy findings are nonspecific | PPI, H₂ blocker, antacids; diet; **raise the head of the bed**; alginates, prokinetics |
| **Cough-variant asthma** | Cough only (common in children, not adults); tied to triggers | **Variable or reversible obstruction**; **negative methacholine challenge excludes it**; home peak flow | **Inhaled glucocorticoid + as-needed β₂-agonist** |
| **Eosinophilic bronchitis** | Normal CXR | **Sputum eosinophils >3%, no obstruction or hyperresponsiveness**; ↑ exhaled NO | **Inhaled glucocorticoids** |

- **Mechanism of ACE inhibitor cough:** bradykinin and substance P accumulate and sensitize sensory nerves.
- **Elevated exhaled NO** predicts response to inhaled steroids (asthma or eosinophilic bronchitis).

## Refractory chronic cough
- If treatment of the common causes fails, or they've been excluded → **chest CT**. It finds tumors, early ILD, **bronchiectasis**, and atypical mycobacteria missed on CXR.
- **Normal exam, PFTs, oxygenation and CT → reassure** that there's no serious lung disease.
- **TB-endemic areas:** chronic cough mandates imaging and sputum analysis.
- **Air pollution** (cooking smoke, occupational): remove the exposure, chimney, mask.

## Symptomatic treatment
- **Narcotics** (codeine, hydrocodone, morphine): most potent. They act on the brainstem cough center. Limited by drowsiness, constipation and dependence.
- **Dextromethorphan:** OTC, central, less effective with fewer side effects. It acts at a different site, so it can be combined with narcotics.
- **Benzonatate:** acts on peripheral sensory nerves. Few side effects but unpredictable effect.
- **Inhaled lidocaine:** transient effect, but **aspiration risk** from oropharyngeal anesthesia.
- **Cough hypersensitivity:**
  - off-label **gabapentin, pregabalin, amitriptyline**; speech therapy;
  - P2X3, NK-1 and TRPV1 antagonists are in development.
- **Empiric ICS, inhaled anticholinergics and macrolides** for idiopathic cough: inconsistent benefit.

## Next best step
| Scenario | Next step |
|---|---|
| Any chronic cough | Chest radiograph |
| Chronic cough on lisinopril (even for years) | Stop it, switch to an ARB, reassess at 1 month |
| Cough, throat clearing, cobblestoned pharynx | Treat postnasal drainage (e.g., nasal steroid) |
| Cough with exercise or allergen exposure, normal spirometry | Methacholine challenge |
| Normal spirometry and methacholine, sputum eosinophils >3% | Inhaled glucocorticoid (eosinophilic bronchitis) |
| Purulent sputum, chronic cough | Bacterial ± mycobacterial culture |
| Failed treatment of common causes | Chest CT |
| Exhaustive negative workup, throat tickle | Gabapentin, pregabalin or amitriptyline; speech therapy |
| Chronic cough, immigrant from a TB-endemic area | Imaging + sputum for TB |
