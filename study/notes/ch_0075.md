# Ch 75 — Prevention and Early Detection of Cancer (high-yield)

## Lifestyle
- **Smoking:**
  - ~1 in 3 smokers die prematurely; lifespan is shortened by ~10 years. Tobacco causes **more CV deaths than cancer deaths**.
  - **Tobacco-related cancers:** lung, larynx, oropharynx, esophagus, kidney, bladder, colon, pancreas, stomach, cervix.
  - **Quitting → 30–50% lower 10-year lung cancer mortality.** It saves more lives than any other public health measure.
  - **Light or low-tar cigarettes aren't safer** (smokers inhale more deeply).
  - **>80% of smokers start before 18.** E-cigarette use is rising (~20% of high schoolers in 2020).
  - **Quitting completely beats cutting down.**
  - **Tools:** counseling + **nicotine replacement, bupropion, varenicline**. E-cigarettes are poorly studied.
  - **Cigars:** 1–2 per day doubles oral and esophageal cancer risk. **Smokeless tobacco:** leukoplakia, oral and esophageal cancer.
- **Physical activity:** ↓ colon and breast cancer (observational).
- **Diet:**
  - **Low-fat, high-fiber diets didn't reduce polyps (Polyp Prevention Trial) or breast or colon cancer (WHI).**
  - Supplements beyond a balanced diet have no proven benefit.
- **BMI >25:** relative risk 1–2 for uterine, gallbladder, kidney, cervix and thyroid cancer and leukemia. **Inverse association with prostate and premenopausal breast cancer.**
- **Sun:**
  - nonmelanoma skin cancer comes from **cumulative UV**; the melanoma link is weaker;
  - **sunscreen ↓ actinic keratoses (SCC precursors); melanoma benefit uncertain**;
  - **70–80% of melanomas arise de novo**;
  - appearance-focused messages reduce tanning in young women.

## Chemoprevention
- **Upper aerodigestive tract:**
  - second-cancer risk **up to 5% per year** after a cured head-and-neck, esophageal or lung SCC; quitting doesn't markedly lower it;
  - **HPV-16 → oropharyngeal cancer** (synergy with smoking);
  - isotretinoin regresses leukoplakia only at toxic doses.
- **β-carotene raises lung cancer in smokers (ATBC, CARET).** α-Tocopherol → more hemorrhagic stroke. **Lesson: test chemoprevention in RCTs.**
- **Colon:**
  - **no agent proven** for the general population;
  - **aspirin or NSAIDs: insufficient evidence**, with clear bleeding harm;
  - **COX-2 inhibitors → CV events**;
  - estrogen + progestin ↓ CRC 44% but isn't recommended (breast and CV harm);
  - **statins: no protective effect**.
- **Breast:**
  - **Tamoxifen ↓ breast cancer 49%** (high-risk women).
    - ↓ fractures; ↑ **endometrial cancer, stroke, PE/DVT**.
  - **Raloxifene (postmenopausal only):**
    - **comparable, without endometrial cancer risk**;
    - fewer clots, slightly less effective.
  - **FDA-approved threshold:** **Gail 5-year risk ≥1.66%**.
  - **Exemestane ↓ invasive cancer 65%** (arthralgias, hot flashes).
- **Prostate:**
  - **finasteride (PCPT) ↓ incidence 18.4% vs. 24.4%, but more Gleason ≥7**; dutasteride similar (REDUCE);
  - **no mortality difference; not FDA-approved for prevention**;
  - **vitamin E ↑ prostate cancer (SELECT)**.

## Vaccines
- **HBV vaccine** prevents HBV hepatitis and HCC.
- **Nonavalent HPV vaccine** (6, 11, 16, 18, 31, 33, 45, 52, 58):
  - HPV 16 and 18 cause >70% of cervical cancers;
  - prevents CIN and AIN;
  - **doesn't treat existing infection**.
  - **Schedule:** **2 doses at ages 9–14; 3 doses if started at 15–26.**

## Surgical prevention
- **Cervical dysplasia:** LEEP or conization.
- **Colectomy:** FAP, ulcerative colitis.
- **BRCA:**
  - **prophylactic mastectomy** (observational data show a large reduction);
  - **salpingo-oophorectomy** (↓ ovarian or peritoneal cancer; **↓ breast cancer ~50%, especially if done before 45–50**);
  - high-grade serous cancer often starts in the **fallopian tube fimbriae**;
  - BSO at benign premenopausal hysterectomy → ↑ CV risk.

## Screening principles
- **Sensitivity = a/(a+c). Specificity = d/(b+d). PPV = a/(a+b). NPV = d/(c+d).**
- **PPV depends on prevalence.** In low-prevalence disease, **poor specificity crushes PPV**. Specificity matters at least as much as sensitivity.
- **Biases:**
  - **Lead time:** diagnosis is earlier, so survival looks longer without longer life.
  - **Length bias:** screening finds slow-growing tumors.
  - **Overdiagnosis:** extreme length bias — "pseudo-disease" that would never cause harm.
  - **Selection (healthy volunteer) bias.**
- **Best evidence:** an RCT with **cause-specific mortality** as the endpoint. **Survival, stage shift and incidence of early disease are misleading.** All-cause mortality also matters.
- **Screening reduces cancer-specific deaths for cervical, colon, breast and lung cancer, but not all-cause mortality (meta-analyses).**
- **Harms:** false positives, workup complications, overtreatment, anxiety.

**Evidence details:**
- **Mammography:** breast cancer mortality ↓ **15–30%** after age 50. At 40–49 the benefit is small, and **nearly half get a false positive**. Overdiagnosis 10–50%. **Self-exam doesn't lower mortality** (more biopsies). **BRCA: MRI is more sensitive** (mammography is less sensitive in young women).
- **Cervical:** screening more often than recommended adds harm, not benefit.
- **CRC:**
  - **FOBT ↓ mortality 22%** (annual 32%), but **only 2–10% of positives are cancer**;
  - **FIT** is more sensitive; stool DNA is less specific;
  - **SEPT9 blood test isn't first-line**;
  - **sigmoidoscopy:** ↓ incidence 18%, ↓ mortality 28%; **adenoma → colonoscopy**;
  - **colonoscopy perforation ~4/10,000** (sigmoidoscopy 1/10,000);
  - CT colonography: extracolonic findings in 5–37%, plus radiation.
- **Lung:**
  - **CXR doesn't help (PLCO)**;
  - **NLST: LDCT ~3 fewer deaths per 1000**;
  - harms: false positives, incidental findings, radiation;
  - offer smoking cessation at every screen.
- **Prostate:**
  - **PSA: lead time, length bias, overdiagnosis** (30–50% of men over 50 harbor insignificant cancer);
  - PLCO: no benefit. **ERSPC: 20% ↓ prostate cancer death; 570 invited and 18 diagnosed to prevent 1 death**;
  - treatment harms: impotence, incontinence;
  - multiparametric MRI helps decisions.
- **Ovarian:** TVUS + CA-125 doesn't reduce deaths. **False positives → surgery** (~20 surgeries per cancer found).
