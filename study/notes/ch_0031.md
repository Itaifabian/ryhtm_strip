# Ch 31 — Dementia (high-yield)

## Definitions
- **Dementia:** acquired cognitive decline that **impairs activities of daily living**. Episodic memory is the function most often lost.
- **Mild cognitive impairment (MCI):** decline confirmed on objective testing, but **daily function is preserved**.
- **Subjective cognitive decline:** the patient reports decline, but testing is normal for age and education.
- **Mild behavioral impairment:** new, sustained neuropsychiatric symptoms in older adults (apathy, disinhibition, psychosis). Like MCI, it can be a prodrome.
- **Rapidly progressive dementia:** onset to dementia in **≤1 year**.
  - Classic cause: **CJD (with myoclonus)**.
  - More often: AD or another neurodegenerative disease, or **autoimmune encephalitis**.
- **MCI → AD progression predictors:**
  - prominent memory deficit;
  - family history;
  - **ApoE ε4**;
  - small hippocampi;
  - positive AD biomarkers.

## Epidemiology
- **Strongest risk factor: age.**
- **AD** causes more than half of cases in Western countries. **Vascular dementia** is second.
- **Under age 65:** **FTD rivals AD**.
- **Over age 65:** AD, DLB, vascular.
- **Mixed pathology is the rule in the very old.** In an amnestic cohort with a mean age at death of ~90: AD 39%, vascular 25%, LATE 17%, Lewy body 12%.
- **Memory clinic:** 19% had a potentially reversible cause. The **top 3 were depression, NPH, and alcohol**. Always consider **medications**.
- **Modifiable risks:**
  - low education, **hearing loss**, social isolation, TBI;
  - hypertension, diabetes, obesity;
  - heavy alcohol, smoking, depression;
  - inactivity, air pollution.

## Table 31-4 — clinical differentiation
| Disease | First symptom | Key features | Imaging |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AD** | **Memory loss** | Motor exam normal early; irritability, depression | **Entorhinal and hippocampal atrophy**, posterior cortex; FDG: temporoparietal, **posterior cingulate and precuneus** |
| **Vascular** | Often sudden, **stepwise**; apathy, falls, focal weakness | Executive slowing, may spare memory; spasticity | Infarcts, **confluent white matter disease** |
| **DLB** | **Visual hallucinations, REM sleep behavior disorder, delirium, Capgras**, parkinsonism | Fluctuating cognition and arousal; drawing and executive deficits; memory spared | Hippocampi larger than in AD; FDG: occipital hypometabolism with the **cingulate island sign** |
| **LATE** | Memory loss, age >70–80 | Slow, memory only | Anterior medial temporal atrophy; **amyloid-negative** |
| **FTD** | **Apathy, disinhibition, hyperorality or overeating, compulsions**, loss of empathy, language | Executive and language deficits; **drawing spared**; may have gaze palsy, axial rigidity, ALS | **Frontal, insular and anterior temporal atrophy** |
| **CJD** | Dementia + movement disorder | **Startle myoclonus**, rigidity, akinetic mutism | **DWI cortical ribboning**, basal ganglia or thalamic signal; EEG **periodic complexes** |

**Other clues:**
- **PSP:** falls, vertical gaze palsy, axial rigidity, dysphagia.
- **Corticobasal syndrome:** asymmetric rigidity, alien limb, apraxia.
- **Gait disturbance:** vascular, PD/DLB, **NPH**. NPH MRI: ventriculomegaly out of proportion to atrophy, gaping Sylvian fissures, vertex crowding (Fig 31-3). Treated by **VP shunt**.
- **Myelopathy + neuropathy:** **B12 deficiency**. Causes: pernicious anemia, veganism, gastric surgery, chronic H2 blockers.
- **Autoimmune encephalitis:** subacute amnesia and psychosis with **mesial temporal T2/FLAIR signal**.
  - **LGI1:** faciobrachial dystonic seizures
  - **NMDA receptor:** psychosis, dyskinesias
  - **Caspr2:** insomnia, ataxia, myotonia
- **Risk behaviors:** test for HIV and syphilis. **Repeated head trauma:** subdural, CTE, NPH.

## Workup (Table 31-3)
- **Routine for everyone:**
  - history and exam;
  - **CBC, CMP (electrolytes, glucose, renal, liver), TSH, B12**;
  - **CT or MRI**.
- **Focused, as indicated:**
  - neuropsychological testing;
  - HIV, RPR/VDRL;
  - LP;
  - FDG, amyloid or tau PET;
  - CXR, urine toxicology;
  - ApoE, blood AD biomarkers.
- **Occasional:** EEG, PTH, adrenal tests, heavy metals, ESR, autoantibodies, angiography, brain biopsy (vasculitis, treatable tumor, unusual infection).
- **MMSE and MoCA** aren't sensitive for early disease and don't distinguish the syndromes. They can be **normal in MCI**, so use full neuropsychological testing.
- **Amyloid PET:** positive in ~25% of normal 70-year-olds. Its main value is **ruling out AD** when negative. A negative scan with amnestic decline and hippocampal atrophy points to **LATE**. **Tau PET** better rules AD in.
- **CSF AD profile:** **low Aβ42 (or Aβ42/40 ratio), raised total tau, raised p-tau**. This is enough to select patients for anti-amyloid therapy.
- **α-Synuclein:** CSF seed amplification assay, skin biopsy.
- **Amyloid and tau PET in cognitively normal people:** research only.

## Treatment
- **Treat reversible causes:**
  - thyroid hormone, thiamine, B12;
  - antiretrovirals, antimicrobials;
  - **shunt for NPH**;
  - tumor therapy;
  - **remove cognition-impairing drugs**.
- **Depression:** SSRI or SNRI, e.g., **escitalopram 5 mg, target 5–10 mg** [verify].
- **Seizures:** **levetiracetam or lamotrigine**.
- **Cognitive drugs:**
  - **Cholinesterase inhibitors** (donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine) for AD. **Rivastigmine for PDD.** They may also help hallucinations and apathy in DLB.
  - **Memantine** (NMDA) for moderate–severe AD; it mainly eases caregiver burden. **Avoid or use great caution in DLB** (worsens agitation and confusion).
- **Anti-amyloid antibodies** (lecanemab, donanemab):
  - **Who:** MCI or mild AD with **amyloid confirmed by PET or CSF**.
  - **Benefit:** lecanemab slowed decline by 27% over 18 months.
  - **Risk: ARIA** (edema, or hemorrhage such as microbleeds). Risk is higher with **ApoE ε4**, so **genotype before treatment**.
  - **Thrombolytics are contraindicated** (fatal intracerebral hemorrhages reported).
- **Agitation or psychosis:**
  1. **First look for triggers:** pain (toothache), constipation, UTI, hunger, electrolytes, drug toxicity.
  2. Then **low-dose quetiapine (12.5–25 mg)** [verify] or **brexpiprazole** (first FDA-approved drug for AD agitation). Both carry **↑ mortality in dementia**.
  3. **Avoid escalating doses, anticholinergics (diphenhydramine), and benzodiazepines or barbiturates.**
- **Nondrug care:**
  - lists, calendars, routines, exercise;
  - reassurance and distraction instead of confrontation;
  - home safety; others eventually take over driving and finances.
- **Caregivers:** screen for burnout; offer day care, respite and support groups.
