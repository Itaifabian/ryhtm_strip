# Ch 57 — Hypercalcemia and Hypocalcemia (high-yield)

## Basics
- **Normal calcium:** 8.5–10.5 mg/dL. About **50% is ionized**; the rest is mostly bound to albumin.
- **Regulation:** low Ca → CaSR on parathyroid → **PTH** → bone resorption (via RANKL), distal tubular Ca reabsorption, and renal **1,25(OH)₂D** → gut absorption.
- **Corrected Ca = measured Ca + 0.8 × (4.0 − albumin).** Ionized Ca is affected by tourniquet time.
- **Pseudohypercalcemia:** calcium-binding IgM (Waldenström). **Pseudohypocalcemia:** low albumin, gadoversetamide.

## Hypercalcemia causes (Table 57-1)
- **Most common: primary hyperparathyroidism and malignancy.**
- **PTH high or inappropriately normal:**
  - **Primary hyperparathyroidism:** adenoma or hyperplasia, MEN; rarely carcinoma.
    - **CDC73** mutation (jaw tumor) → ~30% carcinoma risk.
    - Genetic cause in ~30% of cases.
  - **Tertiary** hyperparathyroidism (CKD).
  - **Lithium** (raises the CaSR set point).
  - **FHH:** autosomal dominant inactivating *CASR* mutation; **surgery ineffective**.
- **PTH suppressed:**
  - **Malignancy:**
    - **PTHrP** from solid tumors;
    - **lytic metastases** (breast, **myeloma**);
    - **1,25(OH)₂D from lymphoma**.
  - **Granulomatous disease** (sarcoid, TB, fungal, leprosy): macrophage **CYP27B1** makes 1,25(OH)₂D.
  - **Vitamin D intoxication:** lasts weeks. CYP24A1 deficiency.
  - **Bone resorption:** **hyperthyroidism, Paget disease, immobilization** (look for a hidden pHPT, Paget or myeloma).
  - **Milk-alkali syndrome**, TPN, ketogenic diet.
  - **Drugs:** **thiazides, vitamin A**, lithium, foscarnet, teriparatide; **denosumab discontinuation (rebound)**.
  - Adrenal insufficiency, pheochromocytoma, acromegaly; lactational PTHrP.
- **Mnemonic VITAMINS TRAP:**
  - **V**itamin D/A; **I**mmobilization;
  - **T**hyrotoxicosis/thiazides; **A**ddison/acromegaly;
  - **M**ilk-alkali; **I**nflammation; **N**eoplasia; **S**arcoid;
  - **T**B; **R**habdo recovery/denosumab rebound; **A**IDS;
  - **P**TH/Paget/parenteral/pheo/pregnancy/pseudo.

## Clinical features
- **Mild (10.5–11.9 mg/dL):** often asymptomatic.
  - Vague neuropsychiatric symptoms, fatigue, proximal weakness, **constipation**, hyporeflexia, **stones**, low BMD.
- **Moderate (12–14) to severe (≥14):**
  - lethargy to coma;
  - nausea, vomiting, peptic ulcer, **pancreatitis**;
  - **polyuria (nephrogenic DI from ↓ AQP2) → volume depletion → AKI**.
- **Long-standing:** bone pain, fractures, hypertension. **Osteitis fibrosa cystica** in low-resource settings.
- **ECG:** bradycardia, AV block, QRS widening, **short QT**.

## Diagnostic approach
1. **Confirm true hypercalcemia** (correct for albumin).
2. **Intact PTH** (second most important test) + creatinine.
3. **PTH high or inappropriately normal + low phosphate → primary hyperparathyroidism.** Fractional calcium excretion is >0.02.
   - **Exclude FHH:** **Ca/Cr clearance ratio <0.01**, family history of mild hypercalcemia, failed neck surgery → sequence *CASR, GNA11, AP2S1*.
   - **Chronic, fluctuating course = pHPT or FHH** (occasionally myeloma). **Malignancy is usually acute.**
4. **PTH suppressed:**
   - **PTHrP**;
   - **SPEP/UPEP with immunofixation + serum free light chains** (~3% of myeloma is missed without them);
   - 25(OH)D;
   - **1,25(OH)₂D**: high or inappropriately normal in granulomatous disease or lymphoma, with phosphate high-normal.

## Treatment of hypercalcemia
- **Mild, asymptomatic:** oral hydration, treat the cause.
- **Severe or symptomatic:**
  1. **IV normal saline (4–6 L in 24 h)**, adjusted for HF.
  2. **Loop diuretic only after euvolemia is restored.**
  3. **IV bisphosphonate:**
     - **zoledronic acid 4 mg over ~30 min** or pamidronate 60–90 mg;
     - onset 1–2 days; normalizes Ca in 60–90%;
     - **contraindicated if GFR <35**.
  4. **Calcitonin 4–8 IU/kg IM or SC q6h** for the first 48 h (rapid but transient).
  5. **Denosumab:** for bisphosphonate-refractory hypercalcemia of malignancy, or **low dose in advanced CKD**.
  6. **Dialysis** for anuric AKI or HF.
- **Vitamin D–mediated (granulomatous, lymphoma, vitamin D toxicity):** **glucocorticoids** (hydrocortisone 200–400 mg IV × 3–5 days, or prednisone 40–60 mg × 7–10 days). Ketoconazole for CYP24A1 deficiency.
- **Parathyroid carcinoma, or pHPT not fit for surgery:** **cinacalcet** (CaSR agonist).
- **Thyroid storm:** treat the thyrotoxicosis.

## Hypocalcemia causes (Table 57-2)
- **Low PTH (hypoparathyroidism):**
  - **Post-thyroid or parathyroid surgery (most common in adults).**
  - **Autoimmune** (~25%; APS-1; checkpoint inhibitors).
  - DiGeorge; radiation; **iron or copper overload** (hemochromatosis, thalassemia, Wilson); infiltration (sarcoid, IgG4).
  - **Hypomagnesemia:** alcohol, cisplatin, anti-EGFR antibodies, **PPIs**.
  - **Severe hypermagnesemia** (obstetric Mg infusions).
  - **Activating CaSR mutations** (autosomal dominant hypocalcemia).
- **High PTH (secondary hyperparathyroidism):**
  - **Vitamin D deficiency**;
  - **CKD** (phosphate retention, ↓ 1,25(OH)₂D);
  - malabsorption (celiac, Crohn, **Roux-en-Y**);
  - **pseudohypoparathyroidism (GNAS; PTH resistance)**;
  - IV phosphate;
  - osteoblastic metastases (prostate);
  - drugs: alcohol, chelators (citrate in transfusions, FFP), bisphosphonates, denosumab.
- **Alkalosis** (respiratory or vomiting) ↑ albumin binding. **Tetany with alkalosis → look for a second cause.**
- **Tissue injury:** burns, **rhabdomyolysis, tumor lysis, pancreatitis**.

## Clinical features
- **Paresthesias** (fingers, toes, perioral).
- **Chvostek sign** (present in ~10% of normal people).
- **Trousseau sign:** cuff 20 mmHg above systolic for 3 min → carpal spasm; more specific.
- Proximal myopathy (vitamin D deficiency, low Mg).
- **Severe:** seizures, laryngospasm, bronchospasm, **long QT → torsades**.

## Diagnosis
- Check **albumin, phosphate, Mg, PTH**.
- **Low or inappropriately normal PTH → hypoparathyroidism** (history of neck surgery).
- **High PTH → vitamin D axis or gut disease.** Check **25(OH)D**. **Low 24-h urine Ca** in vitamin D deficiency or malabsorption. 1,25(OH)₂D if nonnutritional rickets.

## Treatment of hypocalcemia
- **Acute symptomatic (tetany, seizures, long QT):**
  - **10 mL 10% calcium gluconate in 50 mL D5W or NS IV over 5–10 min with telemetry**;
  - repeat 1–2 times, then **infusion ~1 mg/min elemental Ca**;
  - check Ca every 4–6 h;
  - **correct Mg**.
- **Chronic hypoparathyroidism:**
  - **oral calcium 1–3 g/day + calcitriol 0.25–1 µg/day**, targeting **low-normal** Ca;
  - higher targets → **hypercalciuria, stones, nephrocalcinosis**;
  - **24-h urine Ca <250 mg (women) or <300 mg (men)**;
  - keep 25(OH)D replete;
  - refractory → **teriparatide** off-label.
- **Vitamin D deficiency:**
  - **ergocalciferol 50,000 IU 2–3×/week**;
  - malabsorption or bypass → up to 100,000 IU/day;
  - **calcium citrate** in gut disease (better absorbed, lowers stone risk).
