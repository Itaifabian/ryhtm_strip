# Ch 66 — Anemia and Polycythemia (high-yield)

## Physiology
- **EPO** comes from renal peritubular cells (a little from the liver).
  - Regulated by **HIF-1α**, which is degraded when O₂ is available.
  - Normal level 10–25 U/L. Rises when Hb falls below 10–12.
- **Marrow output can rise 4–5× in 1–2 weeks**, but only if **iron is adequate**.
- RBCs live 100–120 days.
- **Compensation:**
  1. ↑ cardiac output (minutes)
  2. ↑ **2,3-DPG** → right shift, more O₂ unloading (hours–days)
  3. ↑ plasma volume (weeks)
- **Gradual anemia is well tolerated.** Symptoms predict the Hct poorly.

## Evaluation
- **History:**
  - prior anemia, iron or transfusions, family history, blood donation;
  - **menstrual history**;
  - diarrhea (celiac, IBD).
- **Exam:** pallor, tachycardia, flow murmur, **splenomegaly**, stool blood, angina or TIA in vascular disease, **pica** (iron), **neuropathy** (B12).
- **Indices:** **MCV 85–95 fL**. MCH and MCHC add little.
- **Normal Hb:**
  - men 16 ± 2;
  - menstruating women 13 ± 2;
  - postmenopausal women 14;
  - **pregnancy 12**.

## Reticulocytes
- **Corrected retic = measured % × (Hct/45).** The absolute count needs no correction.
- **High → bleeding or hemolysis. Low → underproduction (≥75% of anemia; most often iron deficiency or inflammation).**

## Marrow exam
- **Indications:**
  - blasts;
  - **severe pancytopenia**;
  - **retic <0.1%**;
  - nucleated RBCs or teardrops;
  - lymphoma staging;
  - unexplained severe anemia.
- **Biopsy before aspirate.** Normal cellularity ~50% (fat % ≈ age). M:E ratio 2.5:1.

## Classification (Table 66-4, Fig 66-17)
- **Microcytic:** **iron deficiency, thalassemia, inflammation, sideroblastic**.
- **Macrocytic, oval (DNA synthesis):** **B12, folate**, chemotherapy, some antiseizure drugs, **MDS**.
- **Macrocytic, round (membrane):** **alcohol, liver disease, hypothyroidism**, reticulocytosis, smoking, dysproteinemia, hypoxia.
- **Normocytic:** renal disease, marrow invasion, aplastic anemia, red cell aplasia, endocrine causes, myeloma.

**Algorithm:**
1. **High retic** (bleeding excluded) → **LDH, haptoglobin, direct antiglobulin test**, smear.
   - **Hemolysis: ↑ LDH, ↓ haptoglobin**, free Hb.
   - **AIHA: spherocytes + positive DAT.**
2. **Low retic + low MCV → ferritin.**
   - **Ferritin <30 = iron deficiency.** **>200 = some iron stores present** (it's an acute-phase reactant).
   - **Normal ferritin → thalassemia**: **low RDW** in thalassemia vs. **high RDW** in iron deficiency.
   - **Iron indices:** serum iron 50–150, TIBC 300–360, **saturation 25–50%**.
3. **Macrocytic → smear.**
   - **Megaloblastic → B12 and folate.**
   - **Normal B12/folate with oval macrocytes → marrow (MDS).**
   - Round → history (alcohol, liver, thyroid).
4. **Normocytic, low retic → renal function, EPO**; marrow if unexplained.

## Underproduction causes
- **Iron deficiency:** most common nutritional deficiency, especially in premenopausal women.
- **B12 deficiency:** macrocytosis, **hypersegmented neutrophils**, hypercellular megaloblastic marrow, **neuropathy**.
- **Folate deficiency:** rare where flour is fortified.
- **Copper deficiency:** anemia + neutropenia. **Vitamin C deficiency:** scurvy.
- **Marrow replacement:** TB, histoplasmosis, metastases (**prostate**), **myeloma** → myelophthisic smear.
- **Low EPO:**
  - **CKD (almost always if GFR <30)**;
  - **ACE inhibitors**;
  - **inflammation** (↓ EPO + **↑ hepcidin** blocking iron absorption and release);
  - **anemia of aging** (11% of community elderly, up to 40% in nursing homes).
- **Aplastic anemia:** pancytopenia, hypocellular marrow (autoimmune, drugs, toxins, radiation).
- **Pure red cell aplasia:** **parvovirus B19**, autoimmune; very low retic, absent erythroid precursors.
- **Stem cell disease:** AML, CML, **MDS** (macrocytic anemia ± pancytopenia; may evolve to AML).

## Treatment
- **Transfuse** for hypotension or cardiac compromise (angina), or to support marrow failure.
- **Check iron status before and during any anemia treatment.** Causes are often multifactorial (e.g., RA + NSAID GI bleeding).
- **EPO** for CKD and chemotherapy anemia. TGF-β inhibitors for MDS; complement inhibitors for PNH.

## Polycythemia
- **Concern at Hb >17 (men) or >15 (women)**, or Hct >50% / >45%.
  - **Hct >60% (men) or >55% (women)** almost always means a true ↑ red cell mass.
- **History:**
  - smoking, altitude, **diuretics**;
  - congenital heart disease, **sleep apnea**, lung disease;
  - **testosterone, EPO, SGLT2 inhibitors**.
- **Symptoms:**
  - **hyperviscosity** (viscosity rises steeply above Hct 55%): headache, vertigo, tinnitus, visual change;
  - **arterial and venous thrombosis** (Budd-Chiari, splanchnic veins);
  - hypertension.
- **Polycythemia vera clues:**
  - **aquagenic pruritus**;
  - **splenomegaly**;
  - ↑ WBC, **basophils, platelets**;
  - peptic ulcers, bleeding, digital ischemia.
- **Types:**
  - **Spurious** (low plasma volume, Gaisböck).
  - **Primary** (PV).
  - **Secondary (EPO-driven):**
    - **appropriate:** hypoxia — lung disease, altitude, **CO from smoking**, high-affinity Hb;
    - **inappropriate:** **renal cysts or cancer, RAS, hepatoma, uterine leiomyoma, cerebellar hemangioma**.
- **Approach (Fig 66-18):**
  1. Compare with prior counts; repeat in 1 month if new and asymptomatic.
  2. **Exclude hypoxia (SpO₂ <93%).**
  3. **Serum EPO + JAK2.**
     - **Low EPO → PV** (**JAK2 V617F in 90–95%**; exon 12 in many of the rest). Spleen ultrasound.
     - **High EPO:**
       - low SpO₂ → heart or lung workup;
       - smoker → **carboxyhemoglobin** (smoker's polycythemia → stop smoking, phlebotomy if unable);
       - nonsmoker with normal SpO₂ → **O₂ affinity** testing, **abdominopelvic CT** for EPO-secreting tumors or cysts.
