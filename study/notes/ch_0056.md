# Ch 56 — Fluid and Electrolyte Disturbances (high-yield)

## Hypovolemia
- **Causes:**
  - **Renal:** osmotic diuresis (glucose, urea, mannitol), diuretics, acetazolamide, **TMP or pentamidine (ENaC block)**, mineralocorticoid deficiency, tubulointerstitial injury, DI.
  - **Extrarenal:** GI losses (**vomiting → alkalosis; diarrhea → acidosis**), insensible losses (500–650 mL/day), sweat, third spacing (sepsis, burns, pancreatitis), hemorrhage.
- **Reliable signs:** **low JVP, orthostatic HR rise >15–20, orthostatic BP fall >10–20**. Skin turgor and mucous membranes are poor markers in adults.
- **Labs:**
  - **Creatinine is more reliable than BUN.**
  - **Nonrenal loss: urine Na <20, urine osm >450.**
  - **Vomiting or diuretic alkalosis: urine Na can be >20** (bicarbonaturia), so **urine Cl <25 is the better marker of hypovolemia**.
- **Treatment:**
  - **Normal saline** for severe hypovolemia with normal or low Na; **albumin isn't superior**.
  - **Hypernatremia:** D5W (pure water loss) or ½–¼ NS.
  - Diarrheal acidosis: isotonic bicarbonate (150 mEq in D5W).
  - Transfuse for hemorrhage, but not above Hct 35%.

## Hyponatremia (Na <135; up to 22% of inpatients)
- **Plasma Na says nothing about volume status.** Almost always it means **AVP plus free water intake**.
- **Hypovolemic:**
  - **Urine Na <20 (extrarenal):** GI, sweat, burns. **Corrects quickly with NS.**
  - **Urine Na >20 (renal):**
    - **primary adrenal insufficiency** (hyperkalemia + hypotension + high urine Na);
    - salt-losing nephropathy;
    - **thiazides** (mimic SIAD; **loops rarely cause hyponatremia** because they blunt concentration);
    - glycosuria, ketonuria, bicarbonaturia;
    - **cerebral salt wasting** (SAH, TBI, craniotomy; responds to **aggressive NaCl**).
- **Hypervolemic:**
  - **HF, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome:** urine Na **<10**. Severity of hyponatremia = **prognosis**.
  - Renal failure: urine Na >20.
- **Euvolemic:**
  - **SIAD (most common)**;
  - **hypothyroidism**;
  - **secondary adrenal insufficiency** (glucocorticoid deficiency → AVP; **hydrocortisone can cause rapid correction**).
- **SIAD:**
  - **Uric acid <4** (volume-depleted patients tend to be hyperuricemic); urine Na >30.
  - **Causes (Table 56-1):**
    - **pulmonary:** pneumonia, TB, positive-pressure ventilation;
    - **CNS:** SAH, meningitis, tumor, trauma;
    - **small cell lung cancer (75% of malignant SIAD**; ~10% of SCLC present with Na <130);
    - **drugs:** **SSRIs (most common)**, TCAs, carbamazepine, chlorpropamide, cyclophosphamide, vincristine, NSAIDs, opioids, antipsychotics, **MDMA**, desmopressin, oxytocin;
    - nausea, pain, exercise, anesthesia.
  - **Smoker → chest CT** (CXR can miss SCLC).
- **Low solute (beer potomania, extreme diets):** **urine osm <100–200, urine Na <10–20**. **Highest ODS risk** (hypokalemia, alcohol, malnutrition, rapid self-correction).
- **Acute hyponatremia (<48 h; Table 56-2):**
  - **postoperative premenopausal women + hypotonic fluids**;
  - glycine irrigation (TURP);
  - colonoscopy prep, new thiazide, polydipsia;
  - **MDMA**;
  - **marathon runners**.
  - **Effects:** cerebral edema → seizures, herniation, **neurogenic pulmonary edema**, hypercapnic respiratory failure.
- **Chronic (>48 h):** brain osmolytes adapt. Still causes **gait problems, falls and fractures**, so correct it even when "asymptomatic."
- **Osmotic demyelination syndrome (ODS):**
  - **Cause:** correction **>8–10 in 24 h or >18 in 48 h**.
  - **Presentation:** **pontine** — quadriparesis, dysarthria, dysphagia, diplopia, locked-in. Appears ≥1 day later.
  - **Risk factors:** **alcoholism, malnutrition, hypokalemia, liver transplant**.

**Diagnosis:**
- **Serum osmolality** excludes pseudohyponatremia (lipids or protein). Effective osmolality is <275 in true hyponatremia.
- **Hyperglycemia: Na falls 1.6–2.4 per 100 mg/dL of glucose.**
- **Volume exam;** K⁺ (adrenal), uric acid, TSH, cortisol (**cosyntropin** for primary adrenal insufficiency).
- **Urine Na <20–30 → hypovolemic. >30 → SIAD.**
  - **Gold standard for hypovolemia: Na corrects with NS.**
  - **Thiazide:** defer the SIAD diagnosis until 1–2 weeks off the drug.
- **Urine osm:** **<100 → polydipsia. >400 → AVP dominant.**

**Treatment:**
- **Acute or severely symptomatic:** **3% saline 100 mL bolus** (repeatable) to raise Na **4–6 mEq/L** quickly (1–2/h). Then chronic limits apply. Check Na **every 2–4 h**. O₂ and ventilation as needed; IV loop diuretic for pulmonary edema. **No vaptans.**
  - Na deficit = 0.6 × weight × (target − current).
- **Chronic:** **<6–8 in the first 24 h, <6 in each later 24 h** (lower targets in alcoholics or hypokalemia).
- **By cause:**
  - **Hypovolemic → NS** (watch for overcorrection as AVP falls).
  - **HF → optimize HF therapy** (e.g., ACE inhibitor).
  - **Potomania → saline and diet**, carefully.
- **SIAD:**
  - **Fluid restriction guided by (urine Na + K)/plasma Na:**
    - **>1 → <500 mL/day**
    - **~1 → 500–700 mL/day**
    - **<1 → <1 L/day**
  - Then **salt tablets or oral urea**, ± **furosemide 20 mg BID + salt**.
  - **Demeclocycline:** nephrotoxic; avoid in cirrhosis.
  - **Tolvaptan** (oral V₂ antagonist) for persistent SIAD (SCLC):
    - start **in hospital** and **liberalize fluids (>2 L/day)**;
    - **limit to 1–2 months (liver toxicity)**;
    - ~10% overcorrect.
  - Conivaptan IV (V1A/V2; hypotension risk).
- **K⁺ repletion raises Na** and can overcorrect.
- **Overcorrection → relower with DDAVP ± D5W.** Alternatively, give **DDAVP clamp + 3% saline** from the start.

## Hypernatremia (Na >145; mortality 40–60%)
- **Risk:** **elderly with impaired thirst or access**.
- **Adipsic DI:** tumor, ACom aneurysm surgery, sarcoid.
- **Causes:**
  - **insensible losses** (fever, burns, ventilation);
  - **osmotic diarrhea or viral gastroenteritis** (stool Na+K <100; secretory diarrhea is isotonic);
  - **osmotic diuresis** (glucose, urea, postobstructive, mannitol);
  - **DI**;
  - Na loading (hypertonic saline or bicarbonate).
- **Nephrogenic DI:**
  - X-linked V₂ or AQP2 mutations;
  - **hypercalcemia, hypokalemia**;
  - **lithium** (enters via ENaC; **amiloride** helps; may persist after stopping);
  - ifosfamide, antivirals.
- **Gestational DI:** placental vasopressinase → **DDAVP**.
- **Effects:** confusion to coma; brain hemorrhage (mostly children); rhabdomyolysis. **Overly fast correction → cerebral edema (infants).**
- **Workup (Fig 56-6):**
  - **Urine osm >800 with <500 mL/day → extrarenal loss.**
  - **Solute excretion >750–1000 mOsm/day → osmotic diuresis.**
  - **Dilute urine → DI.**
  - **Water deprivation is contraindicated** when already hypernatremic.
  - **DDAVP response:** **central DI** → urine osm rises (with low AVP or copeptin). **Nephrogenic** → rises <50% (high AVP or copeptin).
- **Treatment (Table 56-3):**
  - **Free water deficit = [(Na − 140)/140] × TBW** (TBW = 60% of weight in men, 50% in women).
  - Replace over **48–72 h**, **≤10 mEq/L per day**. Add ongoing losses (electrolyte-free water clearance).
  - **Oral or NG water**, or D5W (watch glucose); ½–¼ NS if hypovolemic.
  - **NS only for severe hypernatremia or hypotension.**
  - **Central DI → DDAVP.**
  - **Chronic nephrogenic DI → thiazide, amiloride (lithium), NSAIDs.** These have **no role acutely**.

## Hypokalemia (K <3.5)
- **10× in-hospital mortality.**
- **Redistribution:**
  - **insulin**;
  - **β₂ agonists** (albuterol, tocolytics, pseudoephedrine);
  - **alkalosis**;
  - theophylline or caffeine;
  - refeeding;
  - B12 or folate therapy, GM-CSF;
  - **barium**;
  - **thyrotoxic periodic paralysis:** Asian or Latin American men, 1–6 am, low K, Phos and Mg; **propranolol 3 mg/kg**.
  - **Pseudohypokalemia** from leukemic WBC uptake.
- **GI:**
  - diarrhea, villous adenoma, laxatives, VIPoma;
  - **vomiting → renal K loss** (secondary hyperaldosteronism + bicarbonaturia).
- **Renal:**
  - **diuretics (thiazides more than loops)**;
  - high-dose penicillins (nonreabsorbed anions);
  - **amphotericin, aminoglycosides, cisplatin, foscarnet** (K and Mg wasting);
  - **↓ Mg (refractory hypokalemia)**.
- **Mineralocorticoid excess:**
  - **Primary hyperaldosteronism:** adenoma ~60%, bilateral hyperplasia ~40%.
    - **Screen: aldosterone/PRA ratio >50.** Antihypertensives that **don't interfere: verapamil, α-blockers, hydralazine, low-dose amiloride**.
    - **FH-I (glucocorticoid-remediable):** test if **age <20 or family history of early stroke or aldosteronism**.
  - **Secondary:** **renal artery stenosis**, renin tumor, malignant hypertension.
  - **SAME** (11βHSD2 loss), **licorice**, itraconazole or posaconazole: **low renin, low aldosterone** → **spironolactone works**.
  - **Liddle** (ENaC gain): low renin and aldosterone; **amiloride works, spironolactone doesn't**.
  - **Ectopic ACTH:** hypokalemia in 60–100% (vs. 10% in pituitary Cushing).
  - CAH (11β- or 17α-hydroxylase).
- **Bartter vs. Gitelman:**
  - **Bartter (TALH, loop-like):** polyuria, **hypercalciuria**.
  - **Gitelman (NCC, thiazide-like):** **hypocalciuria, hypomagnesemia**, chondrocalcinosis; milder.
- **Effects:**
  - arrhythmias, **digoxin toxicity**;
  - ECG: **broad flat T waves, ST depression, QT prolongation** (marked at <2.7);
  - weakness or paralysis, rhabdomyolysis, ileus;
  - NDI, cysts.
- **Workup (Fig 56-7):**
  - **24-h urine K <15 mmol (or <15 mmol/g Cr) → extrarenal.** TTKG <3 is the expected renal response; **K/Cr >13 mmol/g = renal wasting**.
  - **Chronic hypokalemic alkalosis:**
    - **bulimia: urine Cl <10**;
    - **Gitelman: persistently high urine Na, K, Cl**;
    - diuretic abuse: variable → urine diuretic screen.
  - **Normal anion gap acidosis:** distal RTA vs. diarrhea → urine anion gap.
- **Treatment:**
  - **Oral KCl** is the mainstay. K-phosphate if Phos is low; K-citrate or bicarbonate with acidosis.
  - **Correct Mg.**
  - **K falls ~0.27 per 100 mmol deficit**; a 400–800 mmol deficit ≈ 2 mEq/L. Replace over 24–48 h.
  - **IV:** **in saline, not dextrose** (insulin worsens it). Peripheral 20–40 mmol/L. **Central line 10–20 mmol/h** with monitoring for K <2.5.
  - **Propranolol for sympathetic-driven shifts** (TPP, theophylline, head injury); avoids rebound hyperkalemia.
  - Telemetry if QT is prolonged.

## Hyperkalemia (K ≥5.5)
- **Pseudohyperkalemia:**
  - **fist clenching**, hemolysis;
  - **thrombocytosis, leukocytosis, erythrocytosis**;
  - cooling of the sample.
- **Redistribution:**
  - **non-gap acidosis** (not lactic acidosis or ketoacidosis);
  - hyperosmolality (mannitol, IVIG, **D50 without insulin**);
  - nonselective β-blockers;
  - **digoxin**, oleander, toad toxin, fluoride;
  - lysine, arginine, aminocaproic acid;
  - **succinylcholine** with **burns, neuromuscular injury, immobilization**.
- **Intake or necrosis:** salt substitutes, **tumor lysis, rhabdomyolysis**, massive transfusion.
- **↓ Excretion:**
  - **CKD/ESRD**, oliguric AKI;
  - **ACE inhibitors, ARBs, aliskiren, spironolactone or eplerenone, drospirenone**;
  - **ENaC blockers: amiloride, triamterene, trimethoprim, pentamidine**;
  - **NSAIDs, cyclosporine, tacrolimus** (hyporeninemic hypoaldosteronism);
  - **heparin** (blocks aldosterone synthesis);
  - **Addison disease**, HIV adrenalitis, adrenal hemorrhage (APS).
- **Hyporeninemic hypoaldosteronism (type 4):**
  - **diabetics, elderly, CKD**;
  - **~50% have acidosis with urine pH <5.5** and a positive urine anion gap;
  - volume expanded.
- **Tubulointerstitial disease** (sickle cell, SLE, amyloid, obstruction): K out of proportion to GFR.
- **PHA-I:** salt wasting, hypotension. **PHA-II:** hypertension + hyperkalemia + hyperchloremic acidosis; **thiazides fix it** (WNK1/4, KLHL3, CUL3).
- **ECG:**
  - **peaked T (5.5–6.5) → loss of P (6.5–7.5) → wide QRS (7–8) → sine wave (>8)**;
  - Brugada-like pattern;
  - **insensitive, especially in CKD**.
  - Also **ascending paralysis**.
- **Workup:** urine Na <20 means distal delivery is limiting → saline or furosemide. **TTKG expected >7–8** in hyperkalemia; <8 = tubular resistance or hypoaldosteronism.
- **Treatment (ECG changes or K ≥6.5 → emergency; admit, monitor):**
  1. **Stabilize the heart:** **10 mL 10% calcium gluconate IV over 2–3 min.** Onset 1–3 min, lasts 30–60 min; **repeat if the ECG doesn't improve**. **On digoxin → give slowly in 100 mL D5W over 20–30 min.**
  2. **Shift K into cells:**
     - **regular insulin 10 U IV + 50 mL D50**, then D10 infusion. Onset 10–20 min, lasts 4–6 h. **No D50 without insulin.** If glucose ≥200–250 → insulin alone.
     - **Albuterol 10–20 mg nebulized** (additive; ~20% of ESRD patients don't respond, so **never use it alone**).
     - **Bicarbonate has no acute role** (slow, isotonic infusion only).
  3. **Remove K:**
     - **patiromer** (causes hypomagnesemia) or **sodium zirconium cyclosilicate**, preferred over **SPS** (**intestinal necrosis**, especially with ileus or opioids; slow);
     - **loop or thiazide diuretics** if volume-replete; **saline if hypovolemic** with low distal Na;
     - **hemodialysis = most effective**.
