# Ch 17 — Headache (high-yield)

## Framework
- **Primary headache:** the headache *is* the disorder (migraine, tension-type, cluster/TACs). **Secondary headache:** caused by something else; treat the cause.
- Pain-sensitive structures: scalp, meningeal arteries, dural sinuses, falx, proximal large pial arteries. **Brain parenchyma, ependyma, choroid plexus and pial veins don't hurt.**
- Migraine is a **brain disorder**, not a "vascular headache." Cranial autonomic symptoms (tearing, rhinorrhea, ptosis) occur in TACs and often migraine, and get misread as sinusitis.
- In primary care, **>90% of patients with headache have migraine.** Most severe headaches are benign; brain tumor rarely causes isolated headache.

## Red flags (Table 17-2) → evaluate urgently
Sudden onset · first severe or "worst" headache · **vomiting that precedes the headache** · subacute worsening over days to weeks · pain induced by bending, lifting or cough · pain that disturbs sleep or is present on waking · known systemic illness · **onset after 55** · fever or systemic signs · abnormal neuro exam · local tenderness (temporal artery).
- New, recent-onset headache or an abnormal exam → **CT or MRI** (equally sensitive as a screen). LP when needed.

## Secondary headaches
| Condition | Key features | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| **Meningitis** | Severe headache, stiff neck, **fever**, pain worse with eye movement; can mimic migraine (photophobia, vomiting) | **LP mandatory** |
| **SAH** | Maximal in **<5 min**, lasts >5 min, stiff neck, **no fever** | CT; **if CT normal → LP** (small or low bleeds can be missed) |
| **Brain tumor** | ~30% list headache as chief complaint; dull, worse with exertion or position; disturbs sleep in ~10% | **Vomiting weeks before the headache → posterior fossa.** Amenorrhea/galactorrhea → prolactinoma. New headache with known cancer → metastases or carcinomatous meningitis. Abrupt pain with bending, lifting or cough → posterior fossa mass, Chiari, or low CSF volume |
| **Giant cell arteritis** | Age ≥50 (mean 70, 65% women); headache, **PMR, jaw claudication**, fever, weight loss; superficial, dull, boring pain; **scalp tenderness** (can't brush hair), worse at night or in cold; tender temporal arteries | **Biopsy + immediately start prednisone 80 mg/d** for the first 4–6 wk when suspicion is high [verify: guideline may have changed since this edition]. **A normal ESR doesn't exclude GCA.** Untreated, about half go blind (ischemic optic neuropathy = main cause of rapid bilateral blindness at >60) |
| **Acute glaucoma** | Prostrating headache starting with eye pain, nausea, vomiting; **red eye, fixed, moderately dilated pupil** | (Ophthalmologic emergency) |

Trap: migraineurs also improve on prednisone, so a steroid response doesn't prove GCA.

## Chronic daily headache (≥15 days/month; ~4% of adults; Table 17-3)
| Primary, >4 h/day | Primary, <4 h/day | Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| Chronic migraine, chronic tension-type, hemicrania continua, NDPH | Chronic cluster, chronic paroxysmal hemicrania, SUNCT/SUNA, hypnic | Posttraumatic, GCA/sarcoid/Behçet, chronic CNS infection, **medication overuse** |

- **Step 1: find and treat any secondary cause.**
- **Prevention (migraine or tension-type background):** amitriptyline or nortriptyline, starting **10–25 mg**, up to 1 mg/kg. Give about **12 h before waking** to avoid morning sleepiness. Alternatives for migraine: topiramate, valproate, propranolol, candesartan, **CGRP mAbs/gepants**.
- **Refractory:** CGRP antibodies or gepants; noninvasive neuromodulation (single-pulse TMS, vagal stimulation, esp. cluster and hemicranias).

## Medication-overuse headache
- Analgesic overuse → more frequent headache and **failed preventives**. The **most common reason a preventive fails is continued regular analgesic use.**
- **Outpatient:** keep a medication diary. Taper ~**10% every 1–2 wk** or stop abruptly (if safe). **Naproxen 500 mg bid** for residual pain. Start the preventive after the taper or alongside it (both supported by trials).
- **Inpatient** (failed outpatient withdrawal, or diabetes or epilepsy complicating withdrawal):
  - Stop everything on day 1; give fluids and antiemetics; **clonidine for opioid withdrawal**.
  - On days 3–5, give **IV DHE q8h for 5 days** with an antiemetic (5-HT3 antagonist or aprepitant).
  - Alternatives: IV lidocaine, IV valproate, IV eptinezumab.

## New daily persistent headache (patient recalls the exact day it started; Table 17-4)
First, **exclude SAH**. Secondary causes: SAH, low CSF volume, raised CSF pressure, posttraumatic, chronic meningitis. Primary forms are migrainous or featureless. Post-viral and post-COVID onset are described. Primary NDPH usually resolves in 3–5 y; treat by phenotype (TCA, topiramate, valproate, gabapentin, CGRP blockers).

### Low CSF volume headache
- **Orthostatic:** starts on sitting or standing, **resolves within minutes of lying down**. Usually absent on waking and worsens through the day.
- **Most common cause: post-LP leak** (10–30%). Onset within 48 h (up to 12 days). Also epidural injection, Valsalva, or spontaneous. Caffeinated drinks give temporary relief.
- **Mimic: POTS.**
- **Imaging:** **MRI with gadolinium → diffuse meningeal enhancement** (Fig 17-1), diagnostic in context. **Don't decompress an incidental Chiari** (it makes the headache worse). To localize the leak: spinal MRI or CT myelography.
- **Treatment ladder:**
  1. Bed rest.
  2. **IV caffeine 500 mg in 500 mL over 2 h** (get an **ECG first**); give at least 2 infusions before further workup.
  3. Abdominal binder.
  4. **Autologous blood patch** (for post-LP, at the LP site).
  5. Theophylline.
  - CSF-venous fistulas need closure.

### Idiopathic intracranial hypertension
- Obese women (sometimes pregnancy). Headache **on waking that improves during the day and worsens lying down**; **transient visual obscurations**. Papilledema may be **absent** → still do **formal visual fields**.
- **Workup:** **MRI + MR venogram** first (venous sinus obstruction in a few) → **LP while symptomatic**. **Raised opening pressure + relief after removing 20–30 mL** is diagnostic.
- **Treatment:** **acetazolamide 250–500 mg bid** + weight loss → **topiramate** next → ICP monitoring or **shunt** if refractory and disabling.
- Other morning headaches: OSA, poorly controlled hypertension.

### Posttraumatic
Can follow head injury, viral meningitis or flu-like illness. Dizziness, memory complaints; normal exam and imaging. Mimic: chronic subdural hematoma.
